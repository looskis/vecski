# Mathematical theory of linear / near-linear alignment between embedding spaces

Scope: when and why a linear (orthogonal, affine, low-rank) or low-complexity map from embedding model A's space to model B's space exists, how to estimate it, how many paired anchors it needs, what error bounds are known, and what the empirical retrieval numbers look like for sentence/text embedding models. Notation throughout: X ∈ R^{n×d1} are n anchor texts embedded by the source model (rows x_i), Y ∈ R^{n×d2} the same texts embedded by the target model (rows y_i). Unless stated, cited papers use row-vector conventions or the transposed convention (Q X vs. X Q); formulas below are stated in one consistent form and the source's convention is noted when it matters.

---

## Key Question 1: Procrustes, generalized Procrustes, least-squares/ridge, CCA/kernel CCA, affine maps — formulations, closed forms, sample complexity, and which preserves cosine / nearest-neighbour structure best

### Takeaway
All of the classical estimators have SVD/pseudo-inverse closed forms and cost O(n·d1·d2 + d^3); orthogonal Procrustes is the only one that preserves within-set cosine/NN structure *exactly*, and across three independent 2025 text-retrieval studies it recovers roughly 95–97% of full re-embedding retrieval quality from ~10k–20k anchor pairs, while unconstrained/affine/MLP maps buy a further 1–3 points of recall at the cost of distorting the source geometry; empirically, performance saturates at anchor counts of roughly 10–20× the embedding dimension.

### Cited Findings

**Orthogonal Procrustes (Schönemann 1966).**
- Problem: find T with T'T = I minimizing tr(E'E) in A T = B + E; Schönemann's 1966 Psychometrika solution, "in contrast to Green's, is applicable to matrices A and B which are of less than full column rank." — [Schönemann 1966, Psychometrika](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychometrika/article/generalized-solution-of-the-orthogonal-procrustes-problem/AF9BDA5A7C7771F1ECE5632862068F27)
- Closed form used by every modern alignment paper: with (source-side) SVD X^T Y = U Σ V^T, the optimum is Q* = U V^T. Stated e.g. as "W* = V U^T where U Σ V^T = SVD(A^T B)" in mini-vec2vec (their convention transposes) — [mini-vec2vec, arXiv 2510.02348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02348); Drift-Adapter: "g_θ(x) = R x, R orthogonal (R^T R = I), solved via SVD: R = U V^T from A B^T = U Σ V^T", parameter count d², ~2.36 MB for d = 768 — [Drift-Adapter, arXiv 2509.23471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471); "Procrustes post-processing … requiring only computation of SVD(Y X^T) per Algorithm 1" — [When Embedding Models Meet, arXiv 2510.13406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- Equivalent maximization form: Procrustes maximizes tr(Y^T X Q) subject to orthogonal Q; Kornblith et al. relate this to linear CKA, which "uses squared Frobenius norm rather than nuclear norm." — [Kornblith et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00414)
- Why orthogonal (self-consistency argument): if W maps source→target and W^T maps target→source, composing them should return the original vector, giving W^T W = I; the solution is O = U V^T from the SVD of the dictionary matrix M = Y_D^T X_D — [Smith et al. 2017, ICLR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03859)
- Empirical support for the constraint (word vectors, en→it, 5000-word dictionary): Mikolov unconstrained linear map 33.8% P@1 → SVD/orthogonal 36.9% → orthogonal + inverted softmax + dimensionality reduction 43.1%; with a pseudo-dictionary of identical character strings, Mikolov's map collapses to 1.0% while the orthogonal method reaches 39.9% — [Smith et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03859)
- Necessary preconditions for an orthogonal map to be the right estimator (Zhang et al. 2019): (1) length-invariance — "all word vectors should have the same, constant length" (reconciles dot-product training objectives with the Euclidean Procrustes objective), and (2) center-invariance — "orthogonal mappings can only align embedding spaces with equal-magnitude centers." Their Iterative Normalization alternates unit-length normalization and mean-centering until both hold simultaneously. BLI test accuracy: en–ja 1.7% → 44.3%, en–zh 32.5% → 44.2%, en–hi 33.3% → 36.7% — [Zhang, Xu, Yang, Boyd-Graber 2019, ACL](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01622)

**Generalized Procrustes (Gower 1975) and multi-model extensions.**
- Gower's GPA translates, rotates, reflects and scales m configurations to minimize Σ_i Σ_j Δ²(P_j^(i), G_i), where G_i is the centroid of the m copies of point i; the centroid configuration is the "consensus." — [Gower 1975, Psychometrika](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychometrika/article/abs/generalized-procrustes-analysis/912F9374B1CB0294175A3B622C1D7696)
- "Multi-Way Representation Alignment" (ICML 2026, Rodolà group) adapts GPA to build "a shared orthogonal universe that preserves the internal geometry essential for tasks like model stitching," finds that "strict isometric alignment is suboptimal for retrieval" relative to agreement-maximizing CCA, and proposes Geometry-Corrected Procrustes Alignment (GCPA): a GPA universe followed by "a post-hoc correction for directional mismatch," which "consistently improves any-to-any retrieval." Pairwise mapping "scal[es] quadratically with the number of models." — [Achara et al. 2026](https://gladia.di.uniroma1.it/publication/2026-achara-multiway/)

**Least squares / affine / ridge (unconstrained linear).**
- Maiorca et al. compare four estimators of T(x) = R x + b: "ortho" (Procrustes), "l-ortho" (least squares then SVD-projection to orthogonal), "linear" (least squares with b = 0), "affine" (unconstrained R, b, by gradient descent). Finding: "ortho and affine, the narrowest and the broadest transformation classes considered, are the better-performing translation methods"; linear and l-ortho are consistently worse. — [Maiorca et al. NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2311.00664](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00664)
- Drift-Adapter's "Low-Rank Affine" variant: g_θ(x) = U V^T x + t with U, V ∈ R^{d×r}, parameters 2dr + d (r = 32, 64; ~0.39 MB at d = 768, r = 64). Its "Residual MLP": g_θ(x) = x + W₂ σ(W₁ x + b₁) + b₂, one 256-unit GELU hidden layer (~1.57 MB). An optional diagonal scaling matrix (d parameters) adds 0.005–0.015 to the recall-retention ratio. — [Drift-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471)
- Unconstrained linear "cannot perform worse in terms of alignment error" since min_A ||A X − Y||_F ≤ min_{Q∈O} ||Q X − Y||_F, yet "orthogonal alignment consistently outperforms linear, especially when upgrading to a stronger query model … preserving the geometry of the stronger source model retains useful information that would otherwise be lost by unconstrained linear alignment. Conversely, when downgrading to a weaker query model, unconstrained alignment can help." Figure 5 differences (nDCG@10, orthogonal minus linear) range from about −50 to +32 points depending on direction and pair. — [When Embedding Models Meet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- On MovieLens-25M retraining experiments (BPR matrix factorization, versions t₀+1…t₀+3), orthogonal Procrustes beat unconstrained linear post-processing on both similar-movie recall@100 and genre-prediction macro ROC-AUC. — [When Embedding Models Meet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- Lähner & Moeller (UniReps workshop, PMLR v243): "method[s] restricted to [the isometry] assumption perform worse than when just using a linear transformation to align the latent spaces … aligning the latent space with a linear transformation performs best while not needing more prior knowledge." — [Lähner & Moeller 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v243/lahner24a.html)

**CCA / kernel CCA.**
- Kornblith et al. invariance table: CCA (R²_CCA and mean ρ_CCA) is invariant to *invertible linear* transformations, orthogonal transformations and isotropic scaling; SVCCA is conditionally invariant; linear CKA and RBF CKA are invariant to orthogonal transforms and isotropic scaling but NOT to invertible linear transforms; linear regression R² is invariant to invertible linear transforms of Y only. — [Kornblith et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00414)
- Theorem 1 (Kornblith): "any statistic invariant to invertible linear transformation … gives the same result for any representation of width ≥ dataset size" — i.e., with p ≥ n every representation is CCA-equivalent, so CCA-type criteria are uninformative when n ≤ d. — [Kornblith et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00414)
- The 2026 multi-way paper finds CCA "outperforms strict isometric alignment for retrieval tasks" while GPA is what preserves the geometry needed for stitching. — [Achara et al. 2026](https://gladia.di.uniroma1.it/publication/2026-achara-multiway/)

**Sample complexity / number of anchors (empirical).**
- "performance gains appear to saturate after roughly 10 000 samples, indicating relatively modest sample requirements"; each Q* in the text-retrieval experiments was fit on 10 000 uniformly sampled documents; models of dimension 312–1024. — [When Embedding Models Meet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- Drift-Adapter: default 20 000 pairs (2% of a 1M-item corpus); "saturation occurs around 16,000 pairs with ARR > 0.97"; "even 5,000 pairs yielded ARR above 0.97 on AG-News"; training 1–2 minutes, independent of corpus size. — [Drift-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471)
- Maiorca et al. use an anchor count "comparable with the dimensionality of the absolute representation" (~64–768 for cross-architecture; 2000 for cross-modal N24News; 1000 anchors for 500-d autoencoders); their sensitivity plot plateaus "beyond ~100–500 anchors depending on task." — [Maiorca et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00664)
- Relative representations use K = 300–768 anchors (300 for word embeddings, 500 for mixed-dimension vision transformers); uniform random anchor selection "performs comparably" to farthest-point sampling or k-means. — [Moschella et al. ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15430)
- Chroma's *same-model* query-only linear adapter improved retrieval from "as few as 1,500 labeled query-document pairs" (up to 70% nDCG gain on CQADupstackEnglish with all-MiniLM-L6-v2); note this is not a cross-model map. — [Chroma, Embedding Adapters](https://www.trychroma.com/research/embedding-adapters)
- Cross-lingual word vectors: supervised Procrustes baselines use "a dictionary of 5,000 source words" (fastText 300-d) — [Conneau et al. ICLR 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04087); weakly supervised seeds of "500–1,000 translation pairs" outperform fully unsupervised induction, which yields zero BLI performance for 87/210 language pairs — [Vulić et al. EMNLP 2019](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1449/)

**Which variant preserves cosine / NN structure best (direct comparisons).**
- Drift-Adapter (MiniLM → MPNet, 1M items, recall-retention ratio ARR vs. full re-embedding): AG-News R@10 ARR — Orthogonal Procrustes 0.974 ± 0.002, Low-Rank Affine (r=64) 0.983 ± 0.002, Residual MLP 0.992 ± 0.001; DBpedia-14 — 0.968 / 0.979 / 0.990; Emotion — 0.953 / 0.967 / 0.984; MRR ARR follows the same ordering (e.g., AG-News 0.965 / 0.975 / 0.988). Query latency 3.0–3.1 μs (OP), 4.7–4.8 μs (LA), 8.0–8.1 μs (MLP). Image (LAION 1M, CLIP ViT-B → ViT-L/14): misaligned 0.635, OP 0.942, LA 0.961, MLP 0.978 R@10 ARR. — [Drift-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471)
- Maiorca et al., cross-architecture stitching (SVM heads, standard scaling), text datasets: TREC no-stitch 0.87, naive absolute 0.20, relative 0.36, affine 0.82, ortho 0.79; AG News 0.73 / 0.25 / 0.39 / 0.65 / 0.66; DBpedia 0.78 / 0.07 / 0.16 / 0.66 / 0.66; IMDB 0.61 / 0.50 / 0.51 / 0.59 / 0.59. Vision: CIFAR10 0.95 / 0.16 / 0.80 / 0.92 / 0.93; CIFAR100-coarse 0.85 / 0.11 / 0.54 / 0.78 / 0.81. Latent cosine after translation (autoencoders): affine 0.96, ortho 0.93–0.94. — [Maiorca et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00664)
- Lähner & Moeller: "nonlinear transformations are optimal" / isometry "too restrictive," linear best among constrained classes (abstract-level claim). — [Lähner & Moeller 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v243/lahner24a.html)

### Inferences
- Closed forms (standard linear algebra, not attributed to a single paper): unconstrained least squares W* = (X^T X)^{-1} X^T Y = X^+ Y (needs n ≥ d1 for uniqueness); ridge W_λ = (X^T X + λI)^{-1} X^T Y (always well-posed, shrinks toward 0); affine map = least squares on [X, 1]; rectangular Procrustes for d1 ≠ d2 = same SVD recipe with a semi-orthogonal Q (Q^T Q = I on the smaller side). Cost: forming X^T Y is O(n d1 d2); the SVD/inversion is O(d³); Procrustes is therefore no more expensive than least squares and is better conditioned (no matrix inverse).
- Degrees of freedom: orthogonal d(d−1)/2; general linear d1·d2; affine d1·d2 + d2; low-rank-r affine 2dr + d. With n anchors supplying n·d2 scalar equations, OLS is under-determined when n < d1 (it interpolates anchors exactly, per Kornblith's Theorem 1 logic) — so n ≫ d1 is a hard requirement for unconstrained maps, while Procrustes can be estimated from n ≈ d anchors (Maiorca's regime) because the constraint removes roughly half the parameters and the SVD is stable even for rank-deficient X (Schönemann).
- The empirical "saturation at ~10k–20k pairs" (Procrustes-bounds paper, Drift-Adapter) for d = 384–1024 corresponds to n/d ≈ 10–25, consistent with needing σ_min(X) to grow like √n so that the Arias-Castro-type bound (Key Question 1 of error theory, below) becomes small.
- Exact invariance: for orthogonal Q, ⟨Qx, Qy⟩ = ⟨x, y⟩ and ||Qx|| = ||x||, so cosine similarities and kNN graphs *within the mapped source set* are unchanged; only cross-set (mapped-source vs. native-target) similarities depend on the fit. Unconstrained/affine/MLP maps can lower cross-set error but change within-set geometry — exactly the trade-off the Procrustes-bounds paper and Drift-Adapter measure from opposite sides (retrieval vs. recall retention).
- Practical ordering for RAG migration: Procrustes (with centering/unit-norm pre-processing) is the safe default; add a low-rank affine or small residual MLP only when ≥ ~15k paired anchors are available and when 1–3 points of recall matter.

### Gaps
- Lähner & Moeller's numeric tables could not be extracted (PDF text extraction truncated); only the abstract-level conclusion (linear > isometric; nonlinear best) is cited.
- No paper found that states a formal finite-sample generalization bound (n vs. d1·d2) for ridge/OLS maps between neural embedding spaces; the "saturation" numbers are empirical only.
- No published kernel-CCA results for sentence-embedding alignment were found.

## Key Question 2: Theoretical arguments that independently trained representations converge to similar geometry (Platonic Representation Hypothesis, Relative Representations, Latent Space Translation, CKA/SVCCA, isometric equivalence) — what exactly they measure and achieve

### Takeaway
The strongest *theoretical* results are (i) Huh et al.'s proof that contrastive learners converge to a PMI kernel, which pins down the Gram matrix and hence (via the Procrustes bounds in Q6/Q1) an orthogonal map up to the Gram error, and (ii) Kornblith's CKA theory explaining why orthogonal-invariant but not linear-invariant similarity is the meaningful notion; empirically, the headline cross-modal alignment is only ~0.16 mutual-kNN and 2026 re-analyses show convergence is mostly *local-neighbourhood* rather than global, which is precisely the regime where orthogonal maps fit well locally but not globally.

### Cited Findings
- **Platonic Representation Hypothesis (Huh et al. 2024).** Alignment metric: a mutual k-nearest-neighbour score, "the mean intersection of the k-nearest neighbor sets induced by two kernels." Proof: contrastive learners optimized for co-occurrence classification converge to representations whose "dot-product kernel approximates the log odds ratio," i.e. the PMI kernel K_PMI(x_a, x_b), across modalities when observations are bijective projections of an underlying reality. Three mechanisms: multitask scaling ("fewer representations that are competent for N tasks than for M < N"), capacity, simplicity bias. Measured vision–language alignment reaches ≈0.16 (authors ask whether 0.16 "is indicative of strong alignment"); alignment correlates with LLM performance on Hellaswag/GSM8K; LLaVA-style bridging needs only "a 2-layer MLP." Limitations listed: information asymmetry across modalities, lack of metric consensus. — [Huh et al. 2024, arXiv 2405.07987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07987)
- **"Back into Plato's Cave" (2026).** The original evidence used "1024 text-image pairs"; at larger scale "k has to grow proportionally" to prevent collapse of the mutual-kNN score, which therefore "captures only coarse structure"; "the reported increase in alignment with language model strength saturates for recent models"; "one-to-one text-image pairing favors alignment, while alignment decreases with non-bijective data"; conclusion: representations "share coarse structure without evidence of convergence to a shared representation." — [arXiv 2604.18572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18572)
- **"Aristotelian" re-analysis (2026).** Global spectral similarity metrics "are confounded by network scale"; after permutation-based null calibration "the apparent convergence … largely disappears"; what survives is that "local neighborhood similarity, but not local distances, retains significant agreement across different modalities." — [arXiv 2602.14486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14486)
- **Relative Representations (Moschella et al., ICLR 2023).** r_x = (cos(e_x, e_a1), …, cos(e_x, e_aK)) for K anchors; assumes latent spaces of different trainings are related by "angle-preserving transformations — rotations, reflections, and rescaling," to which cosine is invariant. Text stitching across BERT-cased/uncased, ELECTRA, RoBERTa: TREC absolute-stitched 21.49% vs relative-stitched 75.89% (non-stitched 91.70%); DBpedia 6.96% vs 80.47% (98.62%); Amazon 49.58% vs 72.37% (87.81%). Cross-lingual Amazon reviews with Wikipedia parallel anchors: en→es 82.78 F1 (vs 43.67 absolute), en→fr 78.49 (54.41), en→ja 65.72 (48.72). Word embeddings FastText vs word2vec (20k shared vocab): absolute cosine 0.01 → relative cosine 0.86, MRR 0.94, Jaccard of neighbourhoods 0.34. CIFAR-100 ViT-base → RexNet decoder: relative-stitched 91.94 F1 vs 93.15 non-stitched. — [Moschella et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15430)
- **Latent Space Translation via Semantic Alignment (Maiorca et al., NeurIPS 2023).** Translations "via simpler transformations than previously thought," estimated with "standard, well-understood algebraic procedures that have closed-form solutions"; validated across ResNet/CNN/ViT and text encoders, and zero-shot text-encoder→vision-decoder stitching: RoBERTa encoder → vision decoder reaches 0.75 accuracy with ortho on N24News; ViT-base-384 → text decoder 0.41. The translation method beats relative representations without decoder retraining (numbers in Q1). — [Maiorca et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00664); [NeurIPS page](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/ad5fa03c906ca15905144ca3fbf2a768-Abstract.html)
- **CKA (Kornblith et al. ICML 2019).** HSIC(K, L) = tr(K H L H)/(n−1)²; linear CKA(X, Y) = ||Y^T X||_F² / (||X^T X||_F ||Y^T Y||_F); RBF-CKA uses a Gaussian kernel with bandwidth a fraction of the median pairwise distance. Sanity check — accuracy at identifying corresponding layers across identical architectures with different seeds: CCA (ρ̄) 1.4%, CCA (R²) 10.6%, SVCCA 9.9%, PWCCA 11.1%, linear regression 45.4%, linear CKA 99.3%. Linear CKA "effectively weights CCA correlations by the eigenvalues explaining variance in each representation." — [Kornblith et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00414)
- **Latent Functional Maps (NeurIPS 2024)** reproduces the layer-correspondence test: LFM 99.8% vs CKA 99.6% vs CCA 41.4% on CIFAR-10 models; LFM similarity is "robust to perturbations that preserve linear separability, unlike CKA." — [Fumero et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14183)
- **vec2vec (NeurIPS 2025)** — the empirical "universal geometry" claim: unsupervised translation (adversarial + cycle-consistency + vector-space-preservation losses) between GTR (T5, 768), GTE (BERT, 768), E5 (768), Stella (768), Granite (RoBERTa, 768) and CLIP (512), trained on 1M disjoint NQ sequences per model: in-distribution cosine up to 0.92, top-1 up to 1.0, mean rank down to 1.0 (naive identity baseline: near-zero top-1, mean rank ~4096); out-of-distribution TweetTopic cosine 0.67–0.90, MIMIC 0.66–0.91; CLIP↔text only 0.62–0.78 cosine, top-1 0.00–0.72. — [Jha, Zhang, Shmatikov, Morris 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- **mini-vec2vec (2025/26)** shows the same alignments are achievable with a *linear, orthogonal* map: "the transformation that mini-vec2vec learns is linear"; pipeline = pseudo-parallel matching of cluster centroids → Procrustes → iterative refinement; initial Procrustes from pseudo-pairs gives top-1 0.22–0.44, refined final top-1 0.96–1.00 and average rank 1.00–1.10 across all 20 ordered pairs of gtr/gte/e5/stella/granite, matching or exceeding vec2vec (e.g., e5→gtr: vec2vec 0.82 top-1 / rank 2.56 vs mini-vec2vec 0.96 / 1.10); runs on CPU "in less than ten minutes, while vec2vec requires 1–7 days on a GPU"; failure modes are "not catastrophic" (~90% top-1, rank 5–10). — [Dar, arXiv 2510.02348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02348)
- **Word-vector isomorphism (cross-lingual theory).** Søgaard, Ruder & Vulić show nearest-neighbour graphs of word embeddings are "far from isomorphic" even within a language; eigenvector similarity of sampled NN subgraphs is "predictive of unsupervised BDI performance"; Table 2 (P@1 unsupervised / supervised-identical-words / eigenvector-similarity Δ): EN-ES 81.89 / 82.62 / 2.07; EN-ET 0.00 / 31.45 / 6.61; EN-FI 0.09 / 28.01 / 7.33; EN-EL 0.07 / 42.96 / 5.01; EN-HU 45.06 / 46.56 / 3.27; EN-PL 46.83 / 52.63 / 2.56; EN-TR 32.71 / 39.22 / 3.14. A 1.7B-word Finnish corpus still gives P@1 = 0.0 unsupervised; different domains or different embedding algorithms also break alignment. — [Søgaard et al. ACL 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03620)
- Vulić, Ruder & Søgaard 2020 use three isomorphism measures (eigenvector similarity, Gromov–Hausdorff distance minimized over orthogonal maps, relational similarity ρ of cosine distributions); 0.72 correlation between Wikipedia size and BLI; "perfect English-to-English mapping (1.0 MRR) requires 0.4B updates for frequent words and 0.6B for infrequent"; controlling corpora/lemmatization shrinks the es–eu gap from 0.291 to 0.129 MRR; "near-isomorphism arises only with sufficient amounts of training." — [Vulić et al. EMNLP 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04070)
- **Vector Linking (ICML 2026)** quantifies the local-vs-global split for *sentence* encoders: on ArguAna, cross-model Pearson correlation of pairwise distances "exceeded 0.8 for distances under 0.57, then decays rapidly, consistently nearing zero" for long-range distances; "short-range distances are approximately preserved up to a scale factor, while long-range distances are not due to model-specific distortion." — [arXiv 2605.31100](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31100)

### Inferences
- The PMI-kernel theorem gives a *Gram-matrix* statement (X Xᵀ ≈ Y Yᵀ), not a coordinate statement; combined with the tight bound min_Q ||QX − Y||_F ≤ (2D)^{1/4}√ε (Q6), it is exactly what is needed to justify an orthogonal map between two well-trained contrastive text encoders — and nothing more (no affine/MLP is implied).
- The 2026 critiques do not undermine the practical migration result; they say the shared structure is local and coarse. For kNN retrieval, local neighbourhood agreement is the relevant quantity, which explains why Procrustes recovers ~95% recall@10 yet unconstrained maps still gain a few points by correcting long-range distortion.
- Relative representations and Procrustes make the same geometric assumption (isometry + rescaling); the difference is whether you re-train the decoder on anchor-cosines (relative) or map coordinates (translation). Maiorca's tables show translation dominates when a frozen decoder must be reused.

### Gaps
- Exact k used in Huh et al.'s mutual-kNN metric was not visible in the fetched text (Appendix A).
- Raghu et al. SVCCA original numbers were not fetched directly; SVCCA is characterized only via Kornblith's comparison.
- No paper was found that formally proves convergence of *non-contrastive* (e.g., masked-LM pooled) sentence embeddings to a common kernel.

## Key Question 3: Empirical results on sentence/text embedding models — how well a linear map from A to B preserves retrieval metrics vs. a nonlinear MLP

### Takeaway
Three independent 2025 studies on real text-embedding pairs give consistent numbers: orthogonal Procrustes retains ≈95–97% of recall@10/MRR, low-rank affine ≈97–98%, and a small residual MLP ≈98–99% (Drift-Adapter, MiniLM→MPNet); an MLP converter from OpenAI text-embedding-3-small (1536-d) into Gecko-004 (768-d) retains an average BEIR nDCG@10 of 0.5314 vs 0.5609 for native target embeddings (0.5209 for the source model), and Procrustes-aligned query encoders make cross-model retrieval "feasible" (otherwise it "fails almost completely") while occasionally *improving* nDCG@10 by double digits when a stronger query model is swapped in.

### Cited Findings
- **Drift-Adapter (EMNLP 2025).** Setup: 1M-item MTEB-derived corpora, old model all-MiniLM → new model MPNet (both 768-d outputs after projection), 20k paired embeddings, no query embeddings used in training. Recall-retention ratio (ARR = adapted / full-re-embed): AG-News R@10 OP 0.974, LA(r=64) 0.983, MLP 0.992; MRR 0.965 / 0.975 / 0.988; DBpedia-14 R@10 0.968 / 0.979 / 0.990; Emotion 0.953 / 0.967 / 0.984. Operational comparison on AG-News: full re-index ~100 GPU-hours + downtime; dual index ~99.5% recall but double serving cost; Drift-Adapter MLP 99.2% recall, ~0.5 GPU-hours, < 10 μs added latency. Failure analysis: 1–5% of queries not recovered, concentrated on "semantic boundary items" and "rare entities" in sparse regions. Severe-drift case GloVe-300d → MPNet-768d (random projection to match dims): misaligned 0.213 R@10 ARR, OP+DSM 0.587, LA+DSM 0.632, MLP+DSM 0.715 — "best suited for iterative upgrades between model versions of similar architectural families." — [Drift-Adapter, arXiv 2509.23471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471); [ACL Anthology EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.805)
- **Embedding-Converter (ACL 2025, Google).** Converter h: R^{d_f} → R^{d_g} is an MLP trained on unlabeled text with three losses: L_reg = Σ ||h(f(t)) − g(t)||₁, a global loss |Dist(h(f(t₁)), h(f(t₂))) − Dist(g(t₁), g(t₂))| over random pairs with Dist = 1 − cosine, and a local loss over each text's k nearest neighbours. Training data: ~500k passages per large BEIR dataset plus the full 500K MS MARCO query set. In-domain nDCG@10 averaged over 13 BEIR datasets — gecko003 → gecko004: source 0.5067, target 0.5609, converted 0.5362; openai-3-small (1536-d) → gecko004 (768-d): source 0.5209, target 0.5609, converted 0.5314. Out-of-domain (12 CQADupStack): 0.4271 / 0.4814 / 0.4542 and 0.4250 / 0.4814 / 0.4408. Query-side conversion instead of corpus: 0.5263 (gecko) and 0.5171 (openai) in-domain. Per-dataset examples (openai→gecko004 converted vs target): Arguana 0.6049 vs 0.6070, SciFact 0.7668 vs 0.7693, HotpotQA 0.6341 vs 0.6892, Quora 0.8346 vs 0.8621. Ablation (out-of-domain, gecko003→004): removing global+local losses drops retrieval 0.5369 → 0.5219; Transformer instead of MLP 0.5273; training only on MS MARCO 0.5194; 5× larger network 0.5329. Claim: ~100× cheaper than re-embedding. — [Embedding-Converter, ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1237)
- **When Embedding Models Meet (2025).** Seven HuggingFace text models (nomic-embed-text-v1.5 768, bge-small-en-v1.5 384, Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B 1024, all-MiniLM-L6-v2 384, sentence-t5-base 768, LaBSE 768, rubert-tiny2 312) on three MMTEB retrieval tasks (HotpotQA-HN, FEVER-HN, TREC-COVID); Q* fit on 10 000 random documents; 49 query×corpus combinations. "Without alignment, cross-model retrieval fails almost completely. After alignment, retrieval becomes feasible across models, and in two of the three tasks, upgrading to a stronger query model can yield substantial performance gains." Orthogonal vs unconstrained linear (Fig. 5, Δ nDCG@10 points): orthogonal better by up to +12 (HotpotQA), +20 (FEVER), +32 (TREC-COVID) when upgrading; linear better by up to −23 / −50 / −19 when downgrading. Appendix B.2: between two Matryoshka-trained models, Q* "typically aligns the first 16–32 dimensions of one embedding space with the corresponding leading dimensions of the other." — [arXiv 2510.13406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- **vec2vec / mini-vec2vec (same encoders: gtr, gte, e5, stella, granite):** nonlinear vec2vec top-1 0.82–1.00, rank 1.00–5.91; linear mini-vec2vec top-1 0.96–1.00, rank 1.00–1.10 (unsupervised, no paired data in either). — [vec2vec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540); [mini-vec2vec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02348)
- **Vector Linking (ICML 2026)** — global Procrustes *fails* as a record-linkage tool when datasets overlap only partially: on FiQA (Qwen↔KaLM) Procrustes reached 20.6% precision / 1.3% recall vs 79.8% P/R for the local-isometry hashing method (GEH); Mistral↔OpenAI pairs 82–84% precision; FEVER 5.4M corpus: GEH 93.8% precision / 68.9% recall vs CCA 5.22% / 0.61%. — [arXiv 2605.31100](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31100)
- **Reference scale for ada-002 vs text-embedding-3-large:** ada-002 (1536-d) MTEB avg 61.0, MIRACL 31.4; text-embedding-3-large (3072-d) MTEB 64.6, MIRACL 54.9. — [Pinecone, OpenAI embeddings v3](https://www.pinecone.io/learn/openai-embeddings-v3/)
- Context on information content: Vec2Text recovers "92% of 32-token text inputs exactly" from embeddings and recovers full names from clinical notes. — [Morris et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06816)

### Inferences
- The ordering MLP > low-rank affine > orthogonal (by 1–3 recall points) is stable across datasets in Drift-Adapter, but the gap to Procrustes shrinks as model families get closer (0.974 vs 0.992 for MiniLM→MPNet) and widens for distant families (0.587 vs 0.715 for GloVe→MPNet). Linear maps are therefore sufficient for "version upgrade" migrations and insufficient for architecture/paradigm jumps.
- Embedding-Converter's gap to the native target (0.5314 vs 0.5609, i.e., ~95% of target nDCG@10) is of the same magnitude as Drift-Adapter's 95–99% ARR, despite a much larger training set (~millions of passages) — suggesting a floor set by information mismatch rather than by estimator capacity (see Q6).
- Because text embeddings are near-invertible (Vec2Text), a deterministic map A→B exists in principle (compose inversion with B's encoder); what linear methods test is whether that map is *close to linear in the dense regions* of the space.

### Gaps
- No paper found that directly reports a Procrustes/linear baseline for the specific pair ada-002 → text-embedding-3-large (or any 1536→3072 OpenAI pair). Closest: openai-3-small (1536) → gecko004 (768) with an MLP (Embedding-Converter), and OP vs MLP on MiniLM→MPNet (Drift-Adapter).
- Embedding-Converter does not report a linear/Procrustes baseline in the sections extracted; its "100×" cost claim is relative to re-embedding, not to a linear map.
- Drift-Adapter's absolute nDCG values (not ratios) were not extracted; only ARR ratios.

## Key Question 4: Dimensionality mismatch (1536→3072, 768→1024), rectangular Procrustes, low-rank maps, Matryoshka interplay

### Takeaway
Every published text-embedding alignment handles d1 ≠ d2 by either zero-padding the smaller space before orthogonal Procrustes (preserves source geometry exactly) or by fitting a rectangular/low-rank map; Matryoshka-trained targets make the problem easier because the first 16–32 (up to 256) target dimensions carry most of the retrieval signal and Procrustes maps between MRL models are observed to align leading dimensions to leading dimensions.

### Cited Findings
- Zero-padding before Procrustes: "When models have different dimensionalities, we pad the smaller embeddings with zeros, thus preserving their original geometry" (models from 312 to 1024 dims). — [When Embedding Models Meet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406); likewise "we zero-pad the smaller one to match the dimension of the other without changing its underlying structure," tested across encoders of 384–1280 dims. — [Maiorca et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00664)
- Drift-Adapter maps R^{d_N} → R^{d_O}; for the 300-d GloVe → 768-d MPNet case a random linear projection was used to match dimensions; low-rank affine with r = 32/64 has 2dr + d parameters. — [Drift-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471)
- Embedding-Converter explicitly converts 1536-d openai-3-small into 768-d gecko004 ("even with inter-model conversion and dimensionality reduction") with an MLP and reports 0.5314 avg nDCG@10 vs 0.5609 target. — [Embedding-Converter](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1237)
- Schönemann's solution applies to "matrices A and B which are of less than full column rank" — i.e., the SVD closed form does not require square or full-rank inputs. — [Schönemann 1966](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychometrika/article/generalized-solution-of-the-orthogonal-procrustes-problem/AF9BDA5A7C7771F1ECE5632862068F27)
- Matryoshka Representation Learning: loss (1/N) Σ_i Σ_{m∈M} c_m · L(W^(m) · F(x_i)_{1:m}; y_i) over O(log d) nested prefixes; ImageNet-1k ResNet-50: 256-d MRL 76.47% vs 75.71% fixed-feature baseline, 128-d 76.30% vs 75.29%, 8-d 66.63% vs 65.29%; naive truncation of non-MRL features degrades badly (64-d random feature selection 49.91% 1-NN vs MRL 70.17%); adaptive retrieval gives "up to 128× theoretical (FLOPS) and 14× wall-clock speedups" at comparable mAP@10. — [Kusupati et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13147)
- OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large truncated to 256 dims outperforms ada-002 at 1536 dims on MTEB (secondary source summarizing OpenAI's release). — [insertchat glossary](https://insertchat.com/glossary/matryoshka-embeddings); [Pinecone](https://www.pinecone.io/learn/openai-embeddings-v3/)
- Between MRL-trained models, the fitted orthogonal Q* "typically aligns the first 16–32 dimensions of one embedding space with the corresponding leading dimensions of the other." — [When Embedding Models Meet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- Whitening-with-truncation (keep top-k columns of W = U Λ^{-1/2}) "is theoretically equivalent to PCA"; BERT-whitening-256 scores 67.51 vs 68.19 full on STS-B. — [Su et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15316)

### Inferences
- Rectangular Procrustes: for d1 < d2 (e.g., 1536 → 3072) the minimizer of ||X Q − Y||_F over Q ∈ R^{d1×d2} with Q Q^T = I_{d1} is still Q = U V^T from the thin SVD of X^T Y = U Σ V^T (U ∈ R^{d1×d1}, V ∈ R^{d2×d1}); the mapped vectors live in a d1-dimensional subspace of the target space, so the map can never reproduce the extra d2 − d1 target directions — this is the structural reason a 1536→3072 map leaves a residual even with infinite anchors. Zero-padding is the same thing written with a square Q.
- For d1 > d2 (e.g., 3072 → 1536 or 1536 → 768) the map necessarily discards d1 − d2 source directions; the least-squares/Procrustes solution picks those most correlated with the target, which is why Embedding-Converter's 1536→768 case (0.5314) is nearly as good as the 768→768 Gecko-to-Gecko case (0.5362).
- Matryoshka structure suggests a cheap two-stage recipe: map into the first m target dims (m = 256–512) where most retrieval signal lives, since both the MRL loss and the observed leading-dimension alignment imply the residual error from the orthogonal fit is concentrated in low-variance trailing dims.

### Gaps
- No published experiment aligning a non-MRL source (e.g., ada-002) into a *truncated* MRL target (e.g., text-embedding-3-large @256) was found; the two-stage recipe above is an inference.
- No paper quantifies how much of the Procrustes residual is attributable to the rank deficiency (d1 < d2) versus geometric non-isometry.

## Key Question 5: Failure modes — anisotropy, hubness, the "cone" effect; does whitening/centering before alignment help?

### Takeaway
Contextual/sentence embeddings are strongly anisotropic (random-word cosine ≈0.99 in GPT-2's top layer; mean-vector norm ≈34–38% of average norm in static vectors), which inflates all cosines, creates hubs, and violates the zero-mean/unit-norm conditions under which Procrustes is the correct estimator; centering + unit-norm (iteratively) and CSLS-style hubness correction give large, documented gains (e.g., en–ja BLI 1.7% → 44.3%; CSLS +4–5 P@1 over NN; Procrustes + centering is SOTA on MixBench), but the Procrustes-bounds paper proves centering alone is *not* sufficient for alignment.

### Cited Findings
- **Anisotropy measurements.** Ethayarajh 2019: average cosine between random words rises with depth — ELMo ~0.0 (input) → ~0.4 (layer 2); BERT ~0.2 → ~0.6 (penultimate), lower at layer 12; GPT-2 ~0.6 (layers 2–8) → ≈0.99 at layer 12, "so anisotropic that two random words will on average have almost perfect cosine similarity"; self-similarity and intra-sentence similarity must be anisotropy-adjusted; "less than 5% of the variance in a word's contextualized representations can be explained by their first principal component." — [Ethayarajh 2019, EMNLP](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.00512)
- Mimno & Thompson 2017: skip-gram vectors "occupy a narrow cone, diametrically opposed to the context vectors"; the narrower the cone the greater the anisotropy. — [Mimno & Thompson 2017, EMNLP](https://aclanthology.org/D17-1308/)
- Mu & Viswanath 2018 (All-but-the-Top): common mean vector norm ratio 0.69/2.04 ≈ 34% (word2vec) and 3.15/8.30 ≈ 38% (GloVe); top ~10 singular values decay "near exponentially" then plateau; algorithm: subtract mean, PCA, remove top D ≈ d/100 components; GloVe-300d word-similarity average 49.19 → 56.76; STS average +~4% over 21 datasets; text classification improved in 34/40 cases. — [Mu & Viswanath 2018, ICLR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.01417)
- **Whitening.** BERT-whitening: μ = mean, Σ = U Λ Uᵀ, W = U Λ^{-1/2}, x̃ = (x − μ) W; STS-B Spearman×100 for BERT-base first-last-avg: 59.04 raw → 68.19 whitened (NLI) → 67.51 at 256 dims; BERT-flow 58.56; STS-14 whitening-256 66.17 vs flow 64.66. — [Su et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15316)
- **Hubness.** Radovanović et al. 2010: the distribution of k-occurrences "becomes considerably skewed as dimensionality increases," producing hubs that are "popular" nearest neighbours; "an inherent property of data distributions in high-dimensional vector space." — [JMLR 2010](https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/radovanovic10a.html)
- CSLS (Conneau et al. 2018): CSLS(W x_s, y_t) = 2 cos(W x_s, y_t) − r_T(W x_s) − r_S(y_t), with r the mean cosine to the K = 10 nearest neighbours in the other space; supervised Procrustes (5k dictionary) P@1 with NN vs CSLS: en–es 77.4 → 81.4, en–fr 74.9 → 81.1, en–de 68.4 → 73.5, en–ru 47.0 → 51.7, en–zh 40.6 → 42.7; en–it sentence retrieval P@1 42.6 → 66.1; Procrustes-CSLS beats inverted softmax (ISF) "almost systematically … while being computationally faster." — [Conneau et al. ICLR 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04087)
- Inverted softmax P(j|i) = exp(β S_ij) / (α_j Σ_n exp(β S_in)) normalizes over source words to suppress hubs. — [Smith et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03859)
- **Centering/normalization as a precondition.** Zhang et al. 2019: orthogonal maps "can only align embedding spaces with equal-magnitude centers," and length normalization is required; iterative normalization (alternate unit-length and zero-mean) yields en–ja 1.7 → 44.3, en–zh 32.5 → 44.2. — [Zhang et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01622)
- Maiorca et al.: standard scaling (zero-mean/unit-variance per feature, statistics computed on anchors only) or L2 normalization before translation; "text modality more norm-sensitive with L2 normalization; vision more robust." — [Maiorca et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00664)
- Procrustes-bounds paper: Theorem 1 "applies to uncentered embeddings"; "Appendix B.3 explicitly argues centering is insufficient for alignment" via a 2-D counterexample ("Centering does not imply alignment"). Yet on MixBench, "simply centering and renormalizing the underlying text and image embeddings significantly improves retrieval," and orthogonal alignment *followed by* centering "yields the best overall performance," establishing state of the art on all four subsets — i.e., centering fixes the modality gap (text and image embeddings "cluster in disjoint regions"), Procrustes fixes rotation. — [When Embedding Models Meet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- Drift-Adapter failure analysis: residual errors concentrate on "rare entities" in sparse regions where "drift [is] more idiosyncratic"; the global adapter is best on "dense, well-represented" regions. — [Drift-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471)
- Vector Linking: long-range cross-model distance correlation "nearing zero" — global isometry fails even though local isometry (ρ > 0.8 below distance 0.57 on ArguAna) holds. — [arXiv 2605.31100](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31100)
- Isomorphism depends on training, not only on data: undertrained spaces are non-isomorphic (0.4–0.6B updates needed for en→en MRR 1.0). — [Vulić et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04070)

### Inferences
- Anisotropy harms alignment through two separate channels: (a) a large common mean vector dominates X^T Y, so the Procrustes SVD spends its leading singular direction matching the two means rather than semantic axes — centering removes this; (b) a narrow cone compresses cosines toward 1, so a given Frobenius residual translates into large *rank* errors in kNN retrieval — unit-norm + whitening restores dynamic range. This is consistent with Zhang et al.'s two conditions and with Su et al.'s STS gains.
- Hubness after mapping is expected even for a perfect isometry (it is intrinsic to the target distribution), so CSLS / inverted-softmax retrieval should be used at query time regardless of the map; the +4–5 P@1 from CSLS on Procrustes maps is a lower bound on what to expect for sentence embeddings, where dimension is 2–10× larger than the 300-d word vectors studied.
- Recommended pre-processing order (synthesis of Zhang 2019, Maiorca 2023, Procrustes-bounds): center with anchor-set means → L2-normalize → (optionally) whiten or drop top-D ≈ d/100 PCs → fit Procrustes → re-center/renormalize outputs; evaluate retrieval with CSLS or a hubness-aware reranker.

### Gaps
- No controlled study was found that measures the effect of whitening/All-but-the-Top *specifically on cross-model map quality for sentence encoders* (as opposed to STS or cross-lingual BLI); the benefits are inferred from word-vector and modality-gap results.
- Hubness magnitudes (k-occurrence skew) for modern 1024–3072-d sentence embeddings after Procrustes mapping were not found in any source.

## Key Question 6: Error bounds and conditions under which linearity is provably insufficient

### Takeaway
Two complementary theorems bound the orthogonal-alignment error purely in terms of Gram-matrix (dot-product) mismatch — a tight, data-independent (2D)^{1/4}√ε bound and an older condition-number-dependent (1+√2)||X‡||ε² bound — and the same quantity lower-bounds the error, so linearity (orthogonal) is sufficient *iff* pairwise inner products are approximately preserved; it is provably insufficient when Gram matrices differ (modality gap, undertrained/non-isomorphic spaces, local-only isometry), when the target encodes information absent from the source, or when the anchor set is too small relative to d (any map fits anchors exactly but cannot generalize).

### Cited Findings
- **Tight upper bound (2025).** Theorem 1: if ||X^T X − Y^T Y||_F ≤ ε then min_{Q∈O_D} ||Q X − Y||_F ≤ (2D)^{1/4} √ε, and the bound is tight (explicit construction in Appendix A.2); proof via a canonical Gram factorization and an extension of the Powers–Størmer (Kittaneh) inequality for ||·|X| − |Y|·||₂. Corollary 1: if E_{i,j}[(x_iᵀx_j − y_iᵀy_j)²] ≤ δ² then E_i ||x̄_i − y_i||² ≤ √(2D)·δ. Corollary 2 extends to unit-norm embeddings and bounds cross-modal similarity deviations. The bound "does not depend on minimum singular values," unlike Tu et al. 2016 and Arias-Castro et al. 2020, which matters "when ε = Nδ with small δ and large N." Empirically, across 21 model pairs "the distances remain well below our theoretical worst-case bound and appear to approximately follow the power-law trend suggested by theory." — [When Embedding Models Meet, arXiv 2510.13406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- **Condition-number bound (Arias-Castro, Javanmard, Pelletier, JMLR 2020).** For tall X (full rank) and Y with ε² = ||Y Yᵀ − X Xᵀ||_p (Schatten p-norm): min_{Q∈O} ||Y − X Q||_p ≤ ||X‡|| ε² + [(1 − ||X‡||² ε²)^{-1/2} ||X‡|| ε²] ∧ (d^{1/2p} ε) when ||X‡|| ε < 1 (and ||X‡|| ε² + d^{1/2p} ε otherwise); under ||X‡|| ε ≤ 1/√2 this simplifies to min_Q ||Y − X Q||_p ≤ (1 + √2) ||X‡|| ε², where X‡ is the Moore–Penrose pseudo-inverse (so ||X‡|| = 1/σ_min(X)). The authors note the Procrustes perturbation bound "appear[s] to be new, which may be surprising as these methods have been in wide use for decades." — [arXiv 1810.09569](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09569)
- **Information/sample argument (Kornblith Theorem 1).** With p ≥ n features vs examples, any two representations are related by an invertible linear map on the sample, so linear-invariant similarity "gives the same result for any representation" — fitting is trivial, meaning is lost. — [Kornblith et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00414)
- **Empirical insufficiency of global linear maps.** (i) Severe drift GloVe→MPNet: OP 0.587 vs MLP 0.715 R@10 ARR (both far below the 0.97+ seen for same-family upgrades) — [Drift-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471). (ii) Partial-overlap linking: Procrustes 20.6% precision / 1.3% recall on FiQA Qwen↔KaLM vs 79.8% for a local-isometry method; cross-model distance correlation → 0 at long range — [Vector Linking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31100). (iii) Non-isomorphic word spaces: P@1 ≈ 0 for EN–ET/FI/EL with eigenvector-similarity Δ ≥ 5, vs 82 for EN–ES at Δ = 2.07 — [Søgaard et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03620). (iv) Cross-modal: CLIP↔text vec2vec cosine only 0.62–0.78 vs up to 0.92 unimodal — [vec2vec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540); text→vision stitching 0.75 but vision→text only 0.41 (asymmetric information) — [Maiorca et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00664). (v) Downgrading query encoder: orthogonal underperforms unconstrained linear by up to 50 nDCG points because the weaker model's geometry is not isometric to the stronger one's — [When Embedding Models Meet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406).
- **Few-anchor regime.** With 5 anchors, Procrustes/relative baselines reach MRR 0.01 on FastText↔word2vec vs 0.99 for spectral Latent Functional Maps; at 50 anchors baselines reach ~0.55; LFM uses 3,000-node kNN graphs (k = 300) and 50 Laplacian eigenvectors. — [Fumero et al. NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14183)
- Huh et al.'s stated limitation: "Information asymmetry: different modalities capture fundamentally different information; abstract concepts resist visual grounding." — [Huh et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07987)
- The 2026 re-analyses: shared structure is "coarse" / "local neighborhood similarity, but not local distances." — [arXiv 2604.18572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18572); [arXiv 2602.14486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14486)

### Inferences
- **Matching lower bound (derivation).** For any orthogonal Q, (QX)ᵀ(QX) = XᵀX, so ||XᵀX − YᵀY||_F = ||(QX)ᵀ(QX) − YᵀY||_F ≤ (||QX||_op + ||Y||_op)·||QX − Y||_F, hence min_Q ||QX − Y||_F ≥ ||XᵀX − YᵀY||_F / (||X||_op + ||Y||_op). Combined with the (2D)^{1/4}√ε upper bound, Gram-matrix mismatch ε is both necessary and sufficient for orthogonal alignability; the sublinear √ε upper vs linear-in-ε lower bound is the "power-law" gap observed empirically.
- **When linearity is provably insufficient**, in the strict sense that *no* linear map can achieve small error: (a) ε is large (Gram matrices differ) — rotation cannot change inner products, and unconstrained linear maps can only reshape them by a fixed d×d bilinear form, which cannot fix *locally varying* scale/orientation distortions of the kind Vector Linking measures (ρ > 0.8 locally, ≈0 globally); (b) rank deficiency d1 < d2: the image of any linear map is a d1-dim subspace, so the residual is at least the energy of Y outside its best d1-dim subspace (PCA tail), irrespective of anchors; (c) information asymmetry: if y = g(text) depends on features not recoverable from x = f(text), no deterministic map (linear or MLP) can close the gap — this is the floor that makes Embedding-Converter (MLP, millions of pairs) and Drift-Adapter (MLP, 20k pairs) both stop at ~95–99% of target quality.
- **When it is provably sufficient**: two contrastive encoders trained to convergence on the same co-occurrence distribution both approximate the PMI kernel (Huh et al.), so XᵀX ≈ YᵀY up to estimation error and the Procrustes bound guarantees an orthogonal map; undertraining (Vulić 2020), different domains/algorithms (Søgaard 2018), and modality gaps break the kernel equality and hence the guarantee.
- Estimation (not representation) failure: with n < d anchors, OLS/affine maps interpolate (Kornblith Thm 1) and generalize poorly; Procrustes remains well-posed but its error is governed by σ_min(X)^{-1} (Arias-Castro), which is large for small or poorly spread anchor sets — the reason the few-anchor regime (≤ 50) favours spectral/local methods and ≥ 10k anchors favours plain Procrustes.

### Gaps
- No published bound converts Frobenius alignment error into retrieval-metric (recall@k/nDCG) degradation; the link is empirical only (Procrustes-bounds Fig. 3/6, Drift-Adapter ARR).
- No theorem was found that characterizes exactly which nonlinear map class (e.g., residual MLP of given width) is needed as a function of Gram mismatch ε; the GloVe→MPNet and partial-overlap cases are empirical demonstrations only.
- The tightness construction for (2D)^{1/4}√ε and the MixBench centering counter-example were only described, not reproduced, from the fetched text.
