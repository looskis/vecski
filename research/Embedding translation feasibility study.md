# Embedding translation buys time, not parity

Translating stored embeddings from an old text-embedding model into a newer model's space is mathematically well-founded and computationally close to free, but it is not a substitute for re-embedding when the goal is the new model's quality. The theory is now settled: an orthogonal map between two embedding spaces exists with error at most **(2D)^{1/4}·√ε** whenever their Gram matrices differ by ε, the bound is tight, and a matching lower bound means inner-product agreement is both necessary and sufficient ([Maystre et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)). Such a map is a closed-form SVD on **~10,000 paired texts**, costs **$10–200 per billion vectors** to apply, and the best peer-reviewed result for the *query-side* direction (new queries mapped into the old index) retains **98.4–99.2% of full re-embedding's Recall@10** with a 256-unit MLP on 20,000 pairs ([Drift-Adapter, EMNLP 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471)). The catch is that this direction caps quality at the old model. In the *document-side* direction that businesses actually want (old vectors moved into the new space, native new-model queries), Google's 35M-parameter converter trained on millions of passages recovers only **26–54% of the nDCG@10 gap** between source and target models on BEIR and never reaches parity ([Embedding-Converter, ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1237)). The reason is information-theoretic, not estimator-limited: the data-processing inequality caps any translator at what the old encoder retained, and embeddings of 128-token inputs already pin down only 8% of their text exactly ([vec2text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06816)). Economically, re-embedding at hosted-API prices runs **$8 to $52,000 per billion 400-token chunks**, so translation only pays at the 100M+ scale, under rate limits, or when source text is gone; vector-database write units (**~$49–55K per billion 3072-d vectors on Pinecone**) dwarf the embedding bill either way and are avoided only by query-side translation. The feasibility verdict is therefore split by regime: translation is a credible production tool as an instant migration bridge within a model lineage, as a fallback when text is unrecoverable, and as a staged-backfill accelerator; it is not credible as a permanent replacement for re-indexing in cross-vendor, cross-architecture, or quality-driven upgrades, and no company has publicly confirmed deploying it in production ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/your-embedding-model-will-deprecate-heres-what-to-do)).

## Orthogonal alignment exists exactly when inner products agree

The mathematical core of embedding translation is older than deep learning. Given n anchor texts embedded by a source model as rows of X ∈ R^{n×d1} and by a target model as rows of Y ∈ R^{n×d2}, the orthogonal Procrustes problem asks for Q with QᵀQ = I minimizing ‖XQ − Y‖_F, and Schönemann's 1966 solution, Q* = UVᵀ from the SVD XᵀY = UΣVᵀ, applies even when X and Y "are of less than full column rank" ([Schönemann 1966](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychometrika/article/generalized-solution-of-the-orthogonal-procrustes-problem/AF9BDA5A7C7771F1ECE5632862068F27)). Every modern alignment paper uses this closed form; Drift-Adapter writes it as R = UVᵀ from ABᵀ = UΣVᵀ with d² parameters (about 2.36 MB at d = 768), and Maystre et al. reduce their entire post-processing recipe to "computation of SVD(YXᵀ)" ([Drift-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471); [Maystre et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)). The competing estimators have equally simple forms: unconstrained least squares W = X⁺Y, ridge W_λ = (XᵀX + λI)⁻¹XᵀY, the affine map as least squares on [X, 1], and the rectangular case d1 ≠ d2 handled either by zero-padding the smaller space ("thus preserving their original geometry," as both Maystre et al. and Maiorca et al. do) or by a semi-orthogonal Q from the same thin SVD ([Maiorca et al. NeurIPS 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00664)). All cost O(n·d1·d2 + d³), and Procrustes is the best-conditioned because it inverts nothing.

Why prefer the orthogonal constraint when unconstrained least squares "cannot perform worse in terms of alignment error"? Three arguments converge. Smith et al.'s self-consistency argument is that if W maps source to target and Wᵀ maps back, composing them should be the identity, forcing WᵀW = I; on English–Italian word vectors this lifted P@1 from 33.8% (Mikolov's unconstrained map) to 36.9%, and when the dictionary was replaced by a noisy pseudo-dictionary of identical strings the unconstrained map collapsed to 1.0% while the orthogonal map held at 39.9% ([Smith et al. ICLR 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03859)). The invariance argument is exact: for orthogonal Q, ⟨Qx, Qy⟩ = ⟨x, y⟩, so cosine similarities and kNN graphs *within* the mapped set are untouched and only cross-set similarities depend on the fit. The empirical argument is the strongest. Maystre et al. find that "orthogonal alignment consistently outperforms linear, especially when upgrading to a stronger query model," by margins of up to +12, +20 and +32 nDCG@10 points on HotpotQA, FEVER and TREC-COVID, because "preserving the geometry of the stronger source model retains useful information that would otherwise be lost"; unconstrained maps win only when *downgrading* to a weaker query model, by as much as 50 points ([Maystre et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)). Maiorca et al. likewise found that "ortho and affine, the narrowest and the broadest transformation classes considered, are the better-performing," with plain linear and least-squares-then-orthogonalize consistently worse ([Maiorca et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00664)). Orthogonality is also conditional on preprocessing: Zhang et al. prove that orthogonal maps "can only align embedding spaces with equal-magnitude centers" and require unit-length vectors, and their iterative normalization (alternating centering and unit-norm) took English–Japanese bilingual induction from 1.7% to 44.3% ([Zhang et al. ACL 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01622)). Centering is necessary but not sufficient; Maystre et al.'s Appendix B.3 gives a two-dimensional counterexample, while on the MixBench cross-modal benchmark centering plus Procrustes set the state of the art on all four subsets ([Maystre et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)).

### Tight bounds pin the error to Gram-matrix mismatch

The decisive theoretical result is Theorem 1 of Maystre et al.: if ‖XᵀX − YᵀY‖_F ≤ ε then min_{Q∈O_D} ‖QX − Y‖_F ≤ (2D)^{1/4}√ε, the dependence on both D and ε is tight by explicit construction, and the bound "does not depend on minimum singular values," unlike the earlier Arias-Castro et al. bound of (1 + √2)‖X‡‖ε² (with ‖X‡‖ = 1/σ_min(X)), which degrades when anchors are few or poorly spread ([Maystre et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406); [Arias-Castro, Javanmard & Pelletier, JMLR 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09569)). Corollary 1 is the practitioner's form: if the mean squared dot-product discrepancy over pairs is δ², then E_i‖Q*x_i − y_i‖² ≤ √(2D)·δ, independent of n. A matching lower bound follows from one line of algebra. Since (QX)ᵀ(QX) = XᵀX for any orthogonal Q, ‖XᵀX − YᵀY‖_F ≤ (‖X‖_op + ‖Y‖_op)·‖QX − Y‖_F, so min_Q‖QX − Y‖_F ≥ ε/(‖X‖_op + ‖Y‖_op). Gram mismatch is therefore both necessary and sufficient for orthogonal alignability, with a √ε upper and linear-in-ε lower bound whose gap is the "power-law trend" the authors observe empirically across 21 model pairs, where measured distances "remain well below our theoretical worst-case bound" ([Maystre et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)).

This reframes the feasibility question as: when do two independently trained encoders share a Gram matrix? Huh et al.'s Platonic Representation Hypothesis supplies the positive case. They prove that contrastive learners optimized for co-occurrence classification converge to representations whose "dot-product kernel approximates the log odds ratio," the PMI kernel, which is a Gram-matrix statement and therefore exactly what the Procrustes bound needs ([Huh et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07987)). Two contrastive text encoders trained to convergence on similar co-occurrence distributions should thus be orthogonally alignable up to kernel-estimation error, and nothing in the theorem implies that an affine or nonlinear map is needed. The negative case comes from the cross-lingual word-vector literature, which established a decade ago that near-isomorphism is fragile. Søgaard, Ruder and Vulić showed that nearest-neighbour graphs are "far from isomorphic" even within one language, that unsupervised alignment falls to literally 0.00 P@1 for English–Estonian, English–Finnish and English–Greek while reaching 81.89 for English–Spanish, and that eigenvector similarity Δ of kNN-graph Laplacians (2.07 for en–es, 6.61–7.33 for the failures) predicts alignability; swapping the *algorithm* (skip-gram vs CBOW) drove en–es from 81.89 to 0.00 even though both monolingual spaces were fine, and different-domain corpora gave "close to zero" ([Søgaard et al. ACL 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03620)). Vulić et al. then showed that isomorphism is mostly a function of training sufficiency: a perfect English-to-English mapping needs 0.4–0.6 billion updates, and under-trained spaces are non-isomorphic regardless of language ([Vulić et al. EMNLP 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04070)). The mapping onto sentence encoders is direct: "different algorithm" is different backbone or training objective, "different domain" is different pre-training mixture, "under-trained" is a small or weak model.

### Convergence is local, which is enough for kNN retrieval but not for global geometry

Two 2026 re-analyses temper the universality claim without undermining the practical result. "Back into Plato's Cave" shows the original cross-modal evidence rests on 1,024 text–image pairs, that the mutual-kNN score "captures only coarse structure" unless k grows with scale, and concludes that representations "share coarse structure without evidence of convergence to a shared representation" ([arXiv 2604.18572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18572)); the "Aristotelian" analysis finds that after permutation-null calibration "the apparent convergence ... largely disappears" and only "local neighborhood similarity, but not local distances, retains significant agreement" ([arXiv 2602.14486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14486)). Vector Linking quantifies this for sentence encoders: on ArguAna the cross-model Pearson correlation of pairwise distances "exceeded 0.8 for distances under 0.57, then decays rapidly, consistently nearing zero" at long range, so "short-range distances are approximately preserved up to a scale factor, while long-range distances are not" ([arXiv 2605.31100](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31100)). This is precisely the regime in which a global orthogonal map fits well in dense neighbourhoods and poorly at the margins. It explains why Procrustes recovers ~95% of recall yet unconstrained and MLP maps still gain 1–3 points by correcting long-range distortion, and why Drift-Adapter's unrecovered 1–5% of queries concentrate on "semantic boundary items" and "rare entities" in sparse regions ([Drift-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471)). It also explains the striking failure of Procrustes as a *record-linkage* tool when two corpora only partially overlap: 20.6% precision and 1.3% recall on FiQA (Qwen↔KaLM) versus 79.8% for a local-isometry hashing method ([Vector Linking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31100)).

Anisotropy is the main structural obstacle to the preconditions above. Ethayarajh measured the average cosine between random words rising with depth to ≈0.99 in GPT-2's final layer, "so anisotropic that two random words will on average have almost perfect cosine similarity" ([Ethayarajh EMNLP 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.00512)); Mu and Viswanath found the common mean vector carries 34–38% of average norm in static embeddings and that removing it plus the top ≈d/100 principal components raised GloVe word-similarity from 49.19 to 56.76 ([Mu & Viswanath ICLR 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.01417)). A large shared mean dominates XᵀY and makes the leading singular direction of the Procrustes SVD match the two means rather than semantic axes; a narrow cone compresses cosines so that a fixed Frobenius residual becomes a large *rank* error. Hubness, "an inherent property of data distributions in high-dimensional vector space," survives even a perfect isometry ([Radovanović et al. JMLR 2010](https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/radovanovic10a.html)), so retrieval over mapped vectors should use CSLS, whose hub penalty lifted supervised Procrustes P@1 by 4–5 points on every MUSE pair and Italian sentence retrieval from 42.6 to 66.1 ([Conneau et al. ICLR 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04087)). The synthesized preprocessing order is: center with anchor-set means, L2-normalize, optionally whiten or drop top components (BERT-whitening took STS-B from 59.04 to 68.19 and is "theoretically equivalent to PCA" when truncated ([Su et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15316))), fit Procrustes, re-normalize outputs, and retrieve with a hubness-aware score.

### Ten thousand pairs saturate the linear estimator

Sample complexity has both a theoretical and an empirical answer, and they agree. Each output coordinate of a linear map is a d1-parameter regression; Hsu, Kakade and Zhang's random-design bound gives excess MSE ≈ σ²(d + 2√(dt) + 2t)/n once n ≥ 6ρ₀²d(log d + t), and for ridge the d is replaced by an effective dimension that shrinks with the eigenvalue decay ([Hsu, Kakade & Zhang 2012](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1106.2363)). The relative estimation overhead is therefore d1/n regardless of d2: for a 1536-dimensional source, 10K pairs adds ~15% of the noise floor, 100K adds ~1.5%, and the OLS validity condition (~68K·ρ² pairs at d = 1536) says unconstrained least squares is unreliable below ~100K pairs without ridge, while the fast spectral decay of anisotropic text embeddings makes the ridge-effective dimension a few hundred, which is why 10–20K pairs suffices in practice. Orthogonal Procrustes has d(d−1)/2 free parameters constrained to a rotation and is estimable from n ≈ d anchors, which is Maiorca et al.'s regime of "a quantity comparable with the dimensionality" (plateau beyond ~500–1,000 anchors, ~15% drop at 250) ([Maiorca et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00664)). Kornblith's Theorem 1 marks the other boundary: with n ≤ d, any two representations are related by an invertible linear map on the sample, so an unconstrained fit interpolates and means nothing ([Kornblith et al. ICML 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00414)). Empirically, Maystre et al. report that "performance gains appear to saturate after roughly 10 000 samples" across 21 pairs of 312–1024-dimensional models ([Maystre et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)); Drift-Adapter finds "saturation occurs around 16,000 pairs with ARR > 0.97" and "even 5,000 pairs yielded ARR above 0.97," with training taking 1–2 minutes independent of corpus size ([Drift-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471)). For 384–1024-dimensional models this is n/d ≈ 10–25. The few-anchor regime belongs to spectral methods: with 5 anchors Procrustes reaches MRR 0.01 on FastText↔word2vec versus 0.99 for Latent Functional Maps built on kNN-graph Laplacians ([Fumero et al. NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14183)), but no business migrating a corpus is anchor-starved, since pairs are made by embedding a sample with the new model.

### Unsupervised alignment works, and its best form is linear

The fully unpaired setting is now solved for same-vintage contrastive encoders, with a decisive lesson about model class. vec2vec (NeurIPS 2025) learns CycleGAN-style translators (space-specific MLP adapters around a shared latent, trained with adversarial, reconstruction, cycle-consistency and vector-space-preservation losses) from 1M *disjoint* NQ embeddings per side and reaches mean cosine 0.74–0.92 and top-1 matching 0.82–1.00 over 8,192-candidate pools across GTR, GTE, E5, Stella and Granite, against a naive baseline at random rank ≈4,096; but it costs 1–7 GPU-days per pair (176 GPU-days total), only 3 of 15 seeds converge for e5→gtr, every loss term is necessary (removing any collapses top-1 to 0.00), and the 10K-embedding ablation gives top-1 0.01 ([Jha, Zhang, Shmatikov & Morris 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)). mini-vec2vec then showed that "the transformation that mini-vec2vec learns is linear": k-means in each space, a quadratic-assignment match of 20–30 centroid similarity matrices, relative-representation pseudo-pairs, Procrustes, and ICP-style refinement reaches top-1 0.96–1.00 and mean rank 1.00–1.10 on all 20 directed pairs, matching or beating vec2vec (e5→gtr: 0.96/1.10 vs 0.82/2.56), on a CPU "in less than ten minutes" from ~26K unpaired vectors per side, with failures that are "not catastrophic (~90% top-1, rank 5–10)" ([Dar, arXiv 2510.02348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02348)). This recapitulates the entire cross-lingual word-vector arc: adversarial MUSE, Artetxe's self-learning from a 0.52%-accuracy structural seed, Gromov–Wasserstein on intra-space distance matrices, and Wasserstein–Procrustes with a convex Birkhoff relaxation all end at the same orthogonal map refined by ICP and CSLS, converging to ~82% P@1 on en–es and ~73% on en–de, matching 5K-pair supervised Procrustes, at runtimes from 37 minutes (GW) to 957 minutes (adversarial) against 3 minutes for supervised ([Conneau et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04087); [Artetxe et al. ACL 2018](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1073.pdf); [Alvarez-Melis & Jaakkola EMNLP 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.00013); [Grave et al. AISTATS 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.11222)). The marginal value of supervision is steep from zero and flat thereafter: across 210 language pairs, the most robust unsupervised method yielded near-zero performance in 87 setups (41.4%), while 500–1,000 seed pairs plus self-learning failed in none and averaged MRR 0.274 versus 0.140 unsupervised and 0.302 at 5K seeds ([Vulić et al. EMNLP 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01638)). For a migration product this means unsupervised alignment is a diagnostic and a fallback, never the primary path: a few hundred re-embedded documents remove both the instability and the 20–30K-unpaired-vector requirement.

### The information-theoretic ceiling is set by what the old encoder discarded

Translation has a hard ceiling that no estimator can cross. With T the text and A = e_A(T), B = e_B(T) deterministic, the chain A→T→B is Markov, the Bayes-optimal translator under squared loss is f*(a) = E[B | A = a], and the irreducible error is E[Var(B | A)] by the law of total variance ([MMSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_mean_square_error)); the data-processing inequality gives I(f(A); B) ≤ I(A; B) ≤ min(I(A;T), I(T;B)) for any f ([DPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_processing_inequality)). The floor is zero exactly when T is recoverable from A, and inversion results calibrate how close real embeddings come. vec2text recovers 92.0% of 32-token inputs exactly from GTR embeddings (BLEU 97.3) and 60.9% from ada-002, but at 128 tokens ada-002 exact match falls to 8.0% (BLEU 55.0, token F1 84) even though reconstructions re-embed to cosine ≈0.99; "reconstruction accuracy inversely correlates with example length" ([Morris et al. EMNLP 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06816)). The embedding of a long chunk fixes its semantics but not its wording, so Var(B | A) is small for the semantic component of B and nonzero for the lexical component, and for 400–500-token RAG chunks no inversion data exists at all. Three concrete cases make the ceiling bite hardest: tokens beyond the old model's context window (a 512-token encoder saw nothing of a chunk's tail that an 8K-context target would encode), languages or modalities the old model treated as noise (Cohere's English-only v2 was retired for multilingual v3/v4 ([Cohere changelog](https://docs.cohere.com/changelog/2026-04-04-embed-v2-aya-8b-retirement))), and fine distinctions the old model collapsed. Huh et al. name the same limit as "information asymmetry" across modalities ([Huh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07987)), and it is why vec2vec's text↔CLIP pairs degrade to cosine 0.62–0.78 with top-1 as low as 0.00 and why Granite captions translated into CLIP space achieve MS COCO R@16 of 0.23 against 0.75 native ([vec2vec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)). Rank deficiency adds a purely linear-algebraic floor for upward dimension changes such as 1536→3072: the image of any linear map is a d1-dimensional subspace, so the residual is at least the energy of Y outside its best d1-dimensional subspace regardless of anchors. Matryoshka-trained targets soften this, because the leading 16–32 dimensions carry most retrieval signal and Procrustes between two MRL models "typically aligns the first 16–32 dimensions of one embedding space with the corresponding leading dimensions of the other" ([Maystre et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406); [Kusupati et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13147)).

### Backward-compatible learning established the problem and the compatibility tax

The vision community formalized the "serve new queries against an old gallery" problem in 2020 and spent five years measuring what post-hoc methods give up relative to training-time ones. Shen et al.'s Backward Compatible Training defines compatibility by the empirical criterion M(φ_new, φ_old) > M(φ_old, φ_old) and adds an "influence loss" that runs the frozen old classifier on new features; on IJB-C face search, new/old TPIR rose from 59.34 (old/old) to 70.70 against a fully backfilled paragon of 76.88, an "update gain" of 64.77%, at a compatibility tax of "less than 3%" on the new model's own accuracy; independently trained new/old scored 0.0, and plain ℓ2 regression of the new model toward old features scored 3.10 TAR ([BCT, CVPR 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11942)). The follow-ups (LCE, UniBCT, RACT/Hot-Refresh, AdvBCT, Darwinian, BT², d-Simplex) are different ways to trade the tax against cross-test accuracy, and BT²'s Lemma 1 proves "there exist cases where backward compatibility will significantly limit the potential of the new model," motivating parking incompatible information in 32 extra orthogonal dimensions ([BT², ICCV 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03989)). All of these require control of the new model's training and are unavailable to a third party holding two frozen encoders. The post-hoc regime, which is what a translation business lives in, has its own prior art. Apple's Forward Compatible Training ablation shows an MLP mapping old to new ImageNet features with no side information reaches 61.8% top-1 against 46.5% old/old and 68.1% new/new, recovering about 70% of the upgrade gain, with transformation cost of 0.8–5.7 MMACs versus 4.1 GMACs to re-run ResNet-50 and storage of 0.5–1 KB versus 164.5 KB per image ([FCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02805)). The warning in the same literature is consistent: Hu et al.'s post-hoc linear map between recommender embedding versions degraded downstream consumers by 6–10% versus under 1% when co-trained ([Hu et al. KDD 2022](https://ai.stanford.edu/~jure/pubs/bcemb-kdd22.pdf)); Csiszárik et al. found direct least-squares stitching reaches only ≈80% relative accuracy at the worst layers while task-loss stitching is near-perfect ([Csiszárik et al. NeurIPS 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.14633)); Budnik and Avrithis note that a learned translator between two fixed large networks "performs poorly, with up to 40% mAP loss," and that for asymmetric retrieval plain regression onto the fixed gallery space is the only objective that works, relational and ranking distillation failing "completely" at mAP 1–4 ([Budnik & Avrithis CVPR 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16331)). Maps fit by regression on activations systematically underperform maps fit with the downstream objective, which is a design constraint for any translator.

The two headline numbers, 98–99% and 26–54%, are not contradictory; they measure different things. Drift-Adapter maps the *new* query encoder's output into the *legacy* space so the existing ANN index is untouched; its ratio is recall relative to re-embedding everything with the new model *and still searching for old-model-quality neighbourhoods*, and it reports that in a 1M-item AG-News deployment the MLP variant held 99.2% recall for ~0.5 GPU-hours against ~100 GPU-hours plus downtime for a full re-index, adding 3–8 μs per query ([Drift-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471)). The mapped query only has to land in the right old neighbourhood; it cannot exceed the old model's representational ceiling, and the authors call the method "Deferral, Not Elimination: full re-embedding eventually necessary." Maystre et al.'s 49-pair grid shows the full range of outcomes for this direction. Without alignment every off-diagonal cell is ≈0 nDCG@10. With a 10K-pair Procrustes map, a strong query encoder on a weak index can *exceed* the index model's own score (TREC-COVID: nomic queries on a LaBSE index 54 versus LaBSE/LaBSE 16, on a rubert-tiny2 index 46 versus 9; FEVER: nomic queries on a sentence-t5 index 41 versus 31), but a weaker or distant query encoder can halve it (HotpotQA: nomic or bge-small queries on a MiniLM index score 10–19 versus MiniLM/MiniLM 50), and cross-model scores sit far below the new model's native score (Qwen3 reaches 85 on FEVER natively but 37 when mapped onto nomic's index) ([Maystre et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)). The outcome is pair-specific, which rules out any blanket parity promise.

Embedding-Converter measures the direction businesses want: old document vectors converted into the new space, queries encoded natively by the new model ("to isolate the impact of corpus embedding conversion"). Its converter is a 4-layer SELU MLP with hidden widths five times the output dimension, trained with an L1 regression loss plus global pairwise-distance and local k=100-neighbour distance-matching losses on roughly half a million passages from each of 14 BEIR datasets plus the full 500K MS MARCO query set, selected by retrieval nDCG on SciFact ([Embedding-Converter](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1237)). Computed from its Tables 1–2, the converter recovers 54% of the source-to-target nDCG gap in-domain and 50% out-of-domain for the same-family upgrade gecko003→gecko004, but only 26% and 28% for the cross-vendor pair openai-3-small→gecko004. On some datasets converted vectors beat the target (Arguana 0.6103 vs 0.6070, TREC-COVID 0.8079 vs 0.7840), which can only happen through noise or regularization and signals a high-variance estimator; on others they fall *below the source* (HotpotQA converted 0.5923 vs source 0.6248 vs target 0.6892, a multi-hop task; Quora 0.8392 vs 0.8626). The ablation is informative about where the shortfall originates: a 5× larger network gained nothing (0.5329 vs 0.5369), a Transformer did worse (0.5273), removing the relational losses cost 1.5 points (0.5219), and training only on MS MARCO cost 1.75 points (0.5194), so the converter is coverage-limited rather than capacity-limited, and the authors conclude that "re-embedding is still necessary to achieve the full potential of a new model" ([Embedding-Converter](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1237)). Corpus-side conversion beat query-side conversion in every setting (0.5362 vs 0.5263 for gecko; 0.5314 vs 0.5171 for OpenAI, the latter below the source model's own 0.5209), consistent with queries being short and documents long.

### Small-data and cross-family translators fail, and cosine is a misleading proxy

Results below the 10K-pair, same-family regime are poor. The only community project with honest retrieval numbers, mapping a 384-d MiniLM into 768-d multilingual-e5 with 800 pairs, found ridge regression closed "only ~16–18% of the gap to full re-embedding," was *worse than the untranslated old model* in MRR on a 2,200-document index (0.764 vs 0.787), gave no significant gain on an unseen topic (p = 0.23), caused a 12.5-point decline on synthetic corporate text, and, critically, reached cosine 0.91 to native vectors while "predicting the mean B vector already achieved 0.86" ([makltd123/embedding-translator](https://github.com/makltd123/embedding-translator)). That last observation disqualifies cosine-to-native as a quality metric: the QuerynAi Hugging Face adapters covering 49 directed pairs including ada-002→text-embedding-3-small report only a test cosine of 0.8718 for a linear map (0.8648 for an MLP) and no recall, nDCG or MRR at all ([QuerynAi ada-002→te3-small card](https://huggingface.co/QuerynAi/queryn-adapter-ada-002_to_te3-small)). vec2vec's MIMIC results show the same decoupling from the other side: cross-backbone cosine stays at 0.66–0.91 while top-1 collapses to 0.01–0.60 (gtr→e5 top-1 0.01, mean rank 1,414 of 8,192), so a translated vector can look "close" and rank the wrong neighbours ([vec2vec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)). The cross-family story is uniform: Drift-Adapter's GloVe→MPNet case retains 71.5% at best and the authors state the method is "best suited for iterative upgrades between model versions of similar architectural families"; vec2vec's Qwen3-Embedding-4B↔gte pair reached cosine only 0.50 in one direction, attributed to differences in "architecture, training methodology, size (37× larger), context length and recency"; same-backbone BERT encoders (gte, e5, stella) are so close that the *identity* map already gives top-1 0.99–1.00 ([Drift-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471); [vec2vec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)). Every published number for a 1536→3072 OpenAI pair, a Cohere v3→v4 pair, or any production cross-vendor pair with retrieval metrics is absent from the literature; the closest proxy is openai-3-small→gecko004 at 26% gap recovery.

Domain shift degrades every translator that was tested on it. Embedding-Converter's gap recovery narrows out of domain; vec2vec trained on NQ keeps top-1 0.95 on tweets but drops to 0.08 (rank 346) on clinical notes for Granite→GTE; the ICML 2026 HMoE paper derives bounds on "systematic error amplification under OOD inputs, mixing and chaining" and limits mixing/chaining loss to 0.5–2.6% Recall@100 against 7.2–92.3% for baselines ([Yang & Cao, ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/61383)). The practical corollary, which ERA's 1,000-document-per-task adapter, Drift-Adapter's 2%-of-corpus sample and Maystre's 10K all embody, is that the map must be fit on the deployment corpus, not on a generic benchmark ([ERA](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03403)). Hybrid indexes mixing translated and native vectors are a second hazard. In Embedding-Converter's controlled 50/50 mix the average was fine (0.5419, between all-converted 0.5362 and all-native 0.5609) with per-dataset losses on NQ and Quora; in the 800-pair community test, translated vectors took 97% of top-5 slots against 35% expected, a "ranking collapse" ([Embedding-Converter](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1237); [makltd123](https://github.com/makltd123/embedding-translator)). The difference is calibration: Google's converter L2-normalizes outputs and trains with distance-matching losses, so translated and native vectors share a score scale; an uncalibrated ridge map does not. Thresholds do not transfer either; OpenAI users found similarity cutoffs moved from ~0.80 to ~0.50 after switching generations ([OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/transitioning-to-the-new-embeddings-models-from-ada/602761)).

### Retrieval tolerates per-vector noise, which is why 95–99% is achievable at all

Why does an imperfect map preserve recall so well in the query-side direction? Embedding quantization provides a calibrated analogy. int8 quantization retains ~99.3% of MTEB nDCG@10 and binary quantization, which for Gaussian-like coordinates is equivalent to a per-vector cosine of √(2/π) ≈ 0.80 with the original, retains 92.53% unrescored and 96.45% with int8 rescoring ([Hugging Face / mixedbread](https://huggingface.co/blog/embedding-quantization)); Cohere reports 99.99% for int8 and 90–98% for binary ([Cohere](https://cohere.com/blog/int8-binary-embeddings)). A translator at cosine 0.85–0.92 is a smaller perturbation in norm than binary quantization, and for unit vectors the score noise on a query q is q·e with variance ≈ ‖e‖²/d_eff, tiny relative to score gaps when the residual spreads over hundreds of dimensions. The 2026 "score-gap survival" model, logit(p) ≈ β₀ + β₁·log(margin/noise_std), predicts ANN recall under transforms to within 0.03 and finds that "near neighbors perturb coherently so the naive independent-perturbation model over-predicts fidelity loss by 3–4×" ([arXiv 2609.00364](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00364)). The difference between quantization and translation is that translation error is *structured* (a collapsed topic, a systematically mis-rotated subspace), which is the component correlated with queries that actually swaps ranks; the SVD-denoising result formalizes the breaking point at residual noise exceeding ~1/k^{1/4} of the signal scale on the semantic subspace ([arXiv 2604.03831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03831)). Over-fetch-then-rescore, the same design that makes binary quantization usable, is the natural way to hide translation loss: retrieve top-3k through translated vectors, rescore with a native cross-encoder or with native vectors for documents already lazily re-embedded.

## Translation costs $10–200 per billion vectors against $8–52,000 to re-embed, but write units dominate

The economic case rests on the ratio between running a transformer over hundreds of tokens and multiplying a stored vector by a matrix. Hosted embedding prices in late 2026 cluster at $0.02–0.20 per million tokens: text-embedding-3-small $0.02, text-embedding-3-large $0.13, ada-002 $0.10 ([OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)); voyage-4-lite $0.02 and voyage-4 $0.12 with a 33% batch discount ([Voyage](https://docs.voyageai.com/docs/pricing)); Gemini Embedding 2 $0.20 standard and $0.10 batch ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)); Titan Text Embeddings V2 $0.02 ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/get-started-with-amazon-titan-text-embeddings-v2-a-new-state-of-the-art-embeddings-model-on-amazon-bedrock/)). Self-hosting changes the arithmetic: a single H100 at max batch runs bge-m3 (568M params) at 640,425 tokens/s, about $0.0011 per million tokens at $2.49/hour spot, while LLM-class embedders such as Qwen3-Embedding-8B (29,262 tok/s, $0.0236/M) and E5-Mistral-7B (27,522 tok/s, $0.0251/M) land at roughly the cheapest API price ([The Embedder's Dilemma, COLM 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.12875)); RunPod's independent bge-m3 benchmark (~1,240 512-token texts/s on an H100 SXM, $1.53 per billion tokens) agrees almost exactly ([RunPod](https://www.runpod.io/blog/gpu-embedding-workloads-benchmark)). At 400 tokens per chunk, the resulting re-embedding bill is:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1M chunks | 0.4B | $8 | $52 | $80 / $40 | $0.44 | $9.4 |

Wall-clock is often the binding constraint rather than dollars. At the 1M-tokens-per-minute rate limit Google's paper assumed, 400B tokens takes ~6,700 hours, which is why Embedding-Converter's appendix puts re-embedding 1B documents (256 tokens each) with openai-3-large at **$16,640 and 4,266 hours** against **$185 and 37 hours** for conversion, and 50M documents at $832 and 213 hours against $10 and 1.9 hours, with converter inference alone on 50M documents taking "only 20 minutes" on two V100s ([Embedding-Converter](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1237)). Self-hosted bge-m3 needs ~174 H100-hours (7.2 days on one GPU) per billion chunks; an 8B embedder needs ~3,800 H100-hours, which means a multi-node fleet or weeks; re-embedding the 138M-document MS MARCO v2 "with 8×H200 GPUs ... exceeds 10 hours" ([arXiv 2604.05480](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.05480)). The translator, by contrast, is 2·d_in·d_out FLOPs per vector (9.4 MFLOP for 1536→3072), so a billion vectors is ~24 seconds of H100 matrix multiply and the job is I/O-bound on reading 6.1 TB and writing 12.3 TB; a 32-core CPU box handles it in under two hours with no GPU, and the Show HN "Drift" tool computes its rotation from a few thousand pairs "in ~15 seconds on CPU" ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48475082)). Even Google's unoptimized 35M-parameter MLP extrapolates to ~7 GPU-hours per billion vectors. The 100× figure that both Google and Rutgers report is conservative against API targets and shrinks to 2–20× against a self-hosted small encoder, where translation's I/O dominates.

The costs that translation does *not* avoid are larger than the embedding bill. Pinecone serverless charges one write unit per KB at roughly $4–4.50 per million units on the Standard plan, so upserting a billion 3072-d fp32 vectors costs **~$49–55K** and a billion 1536-d vectors ~$25K, identical whether the vectors came from re-embedding or translation; storage at $0.33/GB/month adds ~$2,000–4,000 per month per index copy during a dual-index cutover ([Pinecone pricing](https://www.pinecone.io/pricing/md/)). Weaviate's add-a-second-vector method causes a "permanent storage increase" because "the old vector cannot be deleted," and Qdrant's blue-green migration "duplicates payloads across both collections" ([Weaviate](https://docs.weaviate.io/weaviate/tutorials/vectorizer-migration); [Qdrant](https://qdrant.tech/documentation/tutorials-operations/embedding-model-migration/)). Dual-serving with RRF fusion raises query latency 1.5–2× ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/your-embedding-model-will-deprecate-heres-what-to-do)). Only query-side translation into the untouched old index avoids all of these, at zero writes, zero extra storage, and 3–8 μs per query; this is the one configuration where "near-free" is literally true. Re-embedding is also sometimes impossible rather than expensive: Qdrant states it "requires access to the original data used to create the embeddings," which is absent for ephemeral data embedded on ingest, licensed corpora where only derived vectors may be retained, deletion-after-embedding privacy regimes, and third-party-supplied vectors; no source quantifies how common vector-only stores are. The inversion route (vec2text then re-embed) is not a cost saver, needing ~400 embedder calls per text and 5M training pairs per source model ([vec2text threat analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12784)).

### Deprecations arrive on fixed clocks and force migration waves

Demand is real and recurring. OpenAI retired 16 first-generation embedding models on January 4, 2024 with six months' notice ([OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)); Google retired embedding-001 on August 14, 2025 and text-embedding-004 on January 14, 2026, promising no re-embedding only for the experimental-to-GA transition of the *same* gemini-embedding-001 ([Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/gemini-embedding-available-gemini-api/)); Cohere retired all three embed-v2.0 models on April 4, 2026 ([Cohere changelog](https://docs.cohere.com/changelog/2026-04-04-embed-v2-aya-8b-retirement)); Azure's lifecycle policy retires GA models 18 months after launch, states "Retirement dates aren't extendable," and now lists ada-002, 3-small and 3-large all retiring on **2028-02-09**, the first concrete end-of-life for the OpenAI embedding family and the largest single forced wave to date ([Azure retirement schedule](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/openai/concepts/model-retirement-schedule)). The pain is documented in engineering tickets: GitLab's gecko@003 retirement issue tested "whether we can still use the existing 003 embeddings with a 005 question embedding" before conceding that "regenerating all existing embeddings becomes necessary" ([GitLab issue 521836](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-org/gitlab/-/issues/521836)); a dev.to case saw a deprecated service make "every RAG query start returning 404s" and measured only 82% top-result overlap between old and new native models after a 48-hour migration ([dev.to](https://dev.to/humzakt/zero-downtime-embedding-migration-switching-from-text-embedding-004-to-text-embedding-3-large-in-1292)). Demand is bimodal: an OpenAI forum user re-embedded 150,000 vectors for under $1 ([OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/new-embedding-model-mapping-with-old-ada002-possible/607804)), so below ~1M chunks the pain is purely operational, whereas at 100M+ chunks the API bill, rate-limited wall-clock, write units and dual-index storage become budget items. Quality pull compounds the clock: MIRACL rises from 31.4% (ada-002) to 54.9% (3-large) ([Pinecone](https://www.pinecone.io/learn/openai-embeddings-v3/)), and that upgrade motive is exactly the one translation serves worst.

The competitive landscape is a research niche with no productized incumbent. Google's Embedding-Converter paper explicitly proposes that vendors ship converters alongside new models, yet no vendor does, and MongoDB/Voyage's docs state that "Voyage AI models aren't trained to preserve relevance across models," requiring re-embedding "even when dimensions and element types match" ([MongoDB](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/voyageai/tutorials/migrate-embedding-model/)). Vector databases are productizing re-embedding itself rather than translation: MongoDB autoEmbed regenerates on model change, Qdrant named vectors, Weaviate aliases, Pinecone integrated inference. Open-source entrants are Drift (Show HN, query-time SVD rotation with a 0.97-recall gate and full re-index fallback), vec2vec, mini-vec2vec, QuerynAi's adapters and makltd123's negative result; Zilliz's FAQ says flatly that "there is no in-place transform that makes the old vectors valid" ([Zilliz](https://zilliz.com/ai-faq/change-embedding-model-without-reindexing)). The ecosystem consensus is both the market gap and the credibility hurdle: a translation product must overturn a position every vendor has published.

## Verdict: feasible as a bridge and a fallback, not as a substitute for re-indexing

Mathematically, the answer is yes with a known ceiling. Orthogonal alignment is the correct model class between contrastively trained encoders of similar vintage, exists with tightly bounded error whenever Gram matrices agree, is estimable in closed form from ~10K pairs in minutes on a CPU, and can be found with no pairs at all. Computationally, translation is one to three orders of magnitude cheaper than re-embedding and, in the query-side configuration, near-free. Where the question fails is "credible alternative to re-embedding the whole corpus": no published method, supervised or unsupervised, linear or 35M-parameter MLP, trained on 20K pairs or millions, reaches re-embedding parity in the direction that captures the new model's quality, and the shortfall has an information-theoretic component that cannot be engineered away for long chunks, truncated contexts, or unseen languages. The regimes separate cleanly.

Translation works, with evidence, when the upgrade stays within a model lineage or architectural family (gecko003→004 at 54% gap recovery; MiniLM→MPNet at 97–99% query-side recall; continual fine-tuning where a single drift vector *beat* re-indexing at 54.1 vs 50.1 nDCG@10 ([QDC](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00037v2))); when the deployment is query-side with the old index left intact; when the corpus is ≥100M chunks, rate-limited, or has no recoverable text; when the map is fit on a 2% sample of the customer's own corpus and validated on real retrieval metrics; and as a transition accelerator, since FastFill shows uncertainty-ordered partial backfilling captures most of the benefit at ~25% backfill and "online backfilling with no regret" exceeds even the independent new model over the backfill curve ([FastFill](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.04766); [Online Backfilling](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.03767)). It fails, with equal evidence, across architecture or paradigm jumps (GloVe→transformer 71.5%; Qwen3-4B↔gte cosine 0.50; text↔CLIP top-1 ≈ 0), across vendors (openai-3-small→gecko at 26%), on small pair counts (800 pairs made ranking worse than the old model), off the training distribution (MIMIC top-1 0.08), when the new model's context window, language coverage or modality exceeds the old one's, and whenever the business case is "we want the new model's quality on old documents," because that is the target-quality gap that translators recover only a quarter to a half of. The ada-002→text-embedding-3-large migration that motivates much of the market sits in an untested middle: same vendor but a 1536→3072 rank-deficient map into an MRL target with no published retrieval number at all.

A pilot must therefore prove six things before productionizing, in order of how often the literature has seen them fail. First, per-pair nDCG@10 and Recall@k on the customer's own corpus and query logs against a native re-embedded baseline, for the specific model pair, in the specific direction, with a pre-registered acceptance gate such as Drift's 0.97 recall ratio; cosine-to-native is inadmissible as evidence. Second, the learning curve: fit the nested family Procrustes → ridge → low-rank affine → residual MLP → MLP with global/local relational losses on 1K, 5K, 20K and 100K pairs and confirm the plateau, because the plateau of the highest-capacity model is the empirical upper bound on the irreducible floor and the linear-to-MLP gap is what nonlinearity is worth. Third, stratification by chunk length, language and source-norm, which turns the information-theoretic limit into a budgeting rule: strata whose error sits far above the global plateau were never encoded and must be natively re-embedded first. Fourth, hybrid-index calibration: score distributions of translated versus native vectors in one index, over-fetch-and-rescore in place, and a measured top-k share test, since both a benign 50/50 result and a 97%-of-slots collapse are on record. Fifth, ANN behaviour, because every post-hoc retrieval number in the literature is exact search on MTEB-sized corpora and no study reports HNSW or IVF-PQ recall over translated vectors at 10⁷+ scale. Sixth, a pre-flight alignability diagnostic on a small paired sample, the Gram discrepancy δ of Maystre et al. or a Søgaard-style Laplacian eigenvalue similarity, so the product can decline pairs it cannot serve before a customer commits.

## Conclusion

The research closes a question that practitioners have treated as folklore. "Embeddings are not round-trippable" is false in the strict sense: between well-trained encoders of similar design, the spaces are near-isometric, a rotation recovers them, and the rotation is essentially free. But the folklore is right about what matters commercially, because the premise of an upgrade is that the new model knows something the old one did not, and a map from old vectors can only redistribute information the old encoder kept. The correct mental model is not "translation versus re-embedding" but a staged system: an instant query-side rotation that keeps the old index alive from day one, a forward translation that moves the bulk corpus most of the way into the new space, native re-embedding targeted at the strata a diagnostic flags as unrecoverable and at the hottest documents, and a rescoring layer that hides the residual. That system is buildable today from closed-form linear algebra plus a small MLP, and its economics are compelling above 100M chunks or under rate limits.

What would move translation from bridge to substitute is not a better estimator; Google's ablation shows capacity is not the bottleneck. It is vendor cooperation of the kind the backward-compatible-training literature has shown works at a 1–3% tax: converters or compatibility constraints shipped with the new model, trained with retrieval objectives on the vendor's own data, which no vendor currently offers even as their own researchers advocate it. Until then, a translation product's honest promise is deferral with measured loss, and its defensible asset is the validation harness that tells a customer, per model pair and per corpus stratum, exactly how much of the new model they are getting.
