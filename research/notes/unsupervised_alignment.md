# Unsupervised and weakly-supervised alignment of embedding spaces (no paired texts)

Scope note for the report writer: every result below is tagged **[WORD]** (static word vectors, usually fastText/word2vec 300-d, cross-lingual) or **[SENT]** (sentence/document embedding models such as GTE, GTR, E5, nomic, bge). The practical question is whether vector translation between two *sentence* embedding models can replace re-embedding; most of the theory and failure analysis comes from the [WORD] literature, and only the 2025-2026 line (vec2vec, mini-vec2vec, Procrustes-bounds) is directly [SENT].

Primary sources were read in full text (PDF) for: vec2vec (NeurIPS 2025, arXiv v4 Jan 2026), mini-vec2vec (arXiv v4 Feb 2026), "When Embedding Models Meet: Procrustes Bounds" (Oct 2025), MUSE (Conneau et al. 2018), Søgaard et al. 2018, Alvarez-Melis & Jaakkola 2018, Grave et al. 2019, Artetxe et al. 2018, Vulić et al. 2019. Abstract-level only: Vulić et al. 2020, Patra et al. 2019, Moschella et al. 2023 (partial full-text via a mirror), Lähner & Moeller 2024.

## Key question 1: vec2vec ("Harnessing the Universal Geometry of Embeddings", Jha, Zhang, Shmatikov, Morris, NeurIPS 2025) — architecture, losses, results, follow-ups

### Takeaway
vec2vec is a CycleGAN-style, fully unsupervised translator between sentence-embedding spaces (two space-specific adapters around a shared MLP latent), trained on 1M *disjoint* NQ embeddings per side. In-distribution it reaches mean cosine 0.74–0.92 and top-1 matching 0.82–1.00 over 8192-candidate pools for cross-backbone pairs (naive/OT baselines ≈ random), but it is expensive (1–7 GPU-days per pair, 176 GPU-days total), unstable (3/15 seeds converge for e5→gtr), and its top-1 accuracy collapses on far-OOD data (MIMIC: 0.01–0.60 cross-backbone) even while cosine stays ≈0.7–0.85. The 2025-26 follow-up mini-vec2vec shows an *orthogonal linear* map found by cluster-matching + Procrustes + ICP refinement matches or beats vec2vec (top-1 0.96–1.00, mean rank 1.00–1.10) in under 10 CPU-minutes on 60k sentences.

### Cited Findings — architecture and objective [SENT]
- Setting: given only a dump of embeddings {u_i = M1(d_i)} from an unknown encoder M1 (no queries, no documents) plus a known, queryable encoder M2, learn F with F(u_i) ≈ M2(d_i). Training uses two sets of embeddings from *disjoint* documents (no overlap at all, unlike cross-lingual word translation which exploits overlapping vocabularies) — [vec2vec, arXiv 2505.12540](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- Architecture: input adapters A1, A2 : R^d → R^Z, shared backbone T : R^Z → R^Z, output adapters B1, B2 : R^Z → R^d. Translators F1 = B2∘T∘A1, F2 = B1∘T∘A2; reconstructions R1 = B1∘T∘A1, R2 = B2∘T∘A2. All modules are MLPs with residual connections, layer normalization and SiLU; discriminators mirror this but drop residuals. Discriminators exist at both the output level (D1, D2) and latent level (Dℓ1, Dℓ2) — [vec2vec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- Objective: θ* = argmin_θ max_{D} L_adv + λ_gen L_gen with
  - L_adv = L_GAN(D1,F1) + L_GAN(D2,F2) + L_GAN(Dℓ1, T∘A1) + L_GAN(Dℓ2, T∘A2) (standard GAN loss at both embedding and latent level)
  - L_rec = E_x‖R1(x) − x‖² + E_y‖R2(y) − y‖²
  - L_CC (cycle consistency) = E_x‖F2(F1(x)) − x‖² + E_y‖F1(F2(y)) − y‖²
  - L_VSP (vector space preservation) = (1/B²) Σ_i Σ_j [ ‖M1(x_i)·M1(x_j) − F2(M2(y_i))·F2(M2(y_j))‖² + ‖M2(y_i)·M2(y_j) − F1(M1(x_i))·F1(M1(x_j))‖² ], i.e., pairwise dot-products within a batch must be preserved after translation
  - L_gen = λ_rec L_rec + λ_CC L_CC + λ_VSP L_VSP. The paper text does not list the numeric λ values (they are in the released code: [github.com/rjha18/vec2vec](https://github.com/rjha18/vec2vec)) — [vec2vec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- Training data/compute: 1M 64-token NQ sequences per side (disjoint), evaluation on a 65,536-text NQ subset chunked in batches of 8192; "due to GAN instability we select the best of multiple initializations". Full models took 1–7 days per pair on 2080Ti/L4/A40/A100; ≈176 GPU-days total; (qwen, gte) took 20 days on an A100 — [vec2vec Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- Models: gtr (T5, 110M, 768-d), e5 (BERT, 109M, 768-d), gte (BERT, 109M, 768-d), stella (BERT, 109M, 768-d), granite (RoBERTa, 278M, 768-d, multilingual), clip (512-d, multimodal), qwen3-embedding (4B, 2560-d, one pair only) — [vec2vec Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- Metrics: mean cosine between F(u_i) and v_i; top-1 accuracy = fraction where v_i is the nearest of n candidates to F(u_i); mean rank (random = n/2 = 4096 for n = 8192). Baselines: Naïve (F(x)=x) and an *oracle-aided* optimal-assignment baseline that is given the candidate set and solved with Hungarian / EMD / Sinkhorn / (entropic) Gromov-Wasserstein, best solver reported — [vec2vec §4.2, App. B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)

### Cited Findings — numeric results [SENT]
- In-distribution (NQ, Table 2; cos / top-1 / mean rank): gra→gtr 0.80/0.99/1.19; gra→gte 0.87/0.95/1.18; gra→e5 0.85/0.98/1.11; gtr→gte 0.87/0.93/2.31; gtr→e5 0.83/0.84/2.88; gte→gtr 0.75/0.91/2.64; gte→stella 0.89/1.00/1.00; gte→e5 0.87/0.99/5.19; stella→gte 0.92/1.00/1.00; e5→gtr 0.74/0.82/2.56; e5→gte 0.90/1.00/1.01. Best cosine 0.92 (stella→gte). Naïve baseline: cos ≈ 0.00–0.04 and rank ≈ 4080–4210 (random) for cross-backbone pairs, but cos 0.38–0.68 and top-1 0.99–1.00 for same-backbone BERT pairs (gte/stella/e5), i.e., those spaces are already nearly compatible. Oracle OT baseline: cos 0.67–0.85 but top-1 0.00 and rank 2664–3888 on cross-backbone pairs — [vec2vec Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- Out-of-distribution (trained on NQ): TweetTopic (800 tweets) cos 0.67–0.90, top-1 0.53–1.00; MIMIC (8192 clinical records) cos 0.66–0.91 but top-1 only 0.01–0.60 for cross-backbone pairs (e.g., gtr→e5 top-1 0.01, rank 1413.8; e5→gtr 0.01, rank 1029.6; gra→gte 0.08, rank 346) vs 1.00 for same-backbone gte↔stella — [vec2vec Table 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- CLIP (512-d, multimodal) pairs are much weaker: gra→clip cos 0.78 / top-1 0.35 / rank 226.6; gte→clip top-1 0.00 / rank 3233; clip→gra 0.74/0.72/4.46; clip→gte 0.00 top-1 / rank 2679 — [vec2vec Table 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- Qwen3-Embedding (2560-d) ↔ gte (768-d), 1024-candidate pools: gte→qwen cos 0.50 / top-1 0.92 / rank 2.28; qwen→gte 0.84/0.88/2.49 — [vec2vec Table 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- Ablation (gte→gtr): full 0.75/0.91/2.64; −VSP 0.58/0.00/4197; −CC 0.50/0.00/3941; −latent GAN 0.49/0.00/3897; −VSP&CC 0.47/0.00/3365; no hyperparameter tuning 0.50/0.00/4012. Every component is necessary; without them cosine rises above naive but geometry is not preserved — [vec2vec Table 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- Data scale (gte→gtr, 1M GTR + N GTE embeddings): N = 10k → cos 0.57, top-1 0.01, rank 1462; 50k → 0.74/0.81/3.91; 100k → 0.74/0.85/4.52; 500k and 1M → 0.75/0.92/2.73 — [vec2vec Table 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- Seed stability (10 fixed epochs, 15 seeds): e5→gte (shared BERT backbone) 14/15 seeds reach ≥80% top-1; e5→gtr (cross-backbone) only 3/15 converge — [vec2vec Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- Information leakage: zero-shot attribute inference on translated embeddings beats naive baseline and sometimes the "ideal" same-space baseline (TweetTopic top-1 0.24–0.38 vs ideal 0.24–0.34; MIMIC top-10 0.09–0.90). Zero-shot inversion (via an off-the-shelf inverter) leaks information judged by GPT-4o for up to 80% of Enron emails and 67% of tweets for some model pairs — [vec2vec Tables 5, Fig. 5, Fig. 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- Only retrieval-style result in the paper: MS COCO text→image retrieval with Granite captions translated into CLIP space: R@16 0.23, mean rank 233.7 (of 5000) vs CLIP-native 0.75 / 23.2 — [vec2vec Table 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- Authors' own framing: results are "a lower bound"; they "leave more robust training to future work"; the hypothesis (Strong Platonic Representation Hypothesis) is that networks trained with the same objective and modality converge to a universal latent space enabling translation without pairwise correspondence — [vec2vec §2, §9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)

### Cited Findings — follow-ups and critiques (2025–2026) [SENT]
- mini-vec2vec (Guy Dar, arXiv 2510.02348, v4 Feb 2026): "While it usually finds a near-perfect alignment, [vec2vec] is expensive and unstable." Proposes a *linear (near-orthogonal)* map found in three steps: (1) approximate matching — k-means in each space (c = 20 clusters; 30 for gtr↔gte), match centroids by solving the QAP π* = argmax_π Σ_ij S^A_ij S^B_π(i)π(j) on centroid cosine-similarity matrices (scipy 2-OPT, 30 restarts), build relative representations r_i = [cos(x_i, c_1), …, cos(x_i, c_C)] against matched centroids, ensemble s = 30 runs by concatenation; pseudo-pairs = each A point ↔ average of its k = 50 nearest B points in relative space; (2) Procrustes W* = VUᵀ from SVD(AᵀB); (3) Refine-1 = ICP with k' = 50 averaged neighbours, 50–100 iterations on 10k subsamples, exponential smoothing W ← (1−α)W + αW_new (α = 0.5); Refine-2 = one round of cluster-based Procrustes with c' = 500 clusters (more than one round hurts). Embeddings are mean-centered and unit-normalised first — [mini-vec2vec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02348)
- mini-vec2vec results (NQ, 60k sentences total, ~26k per side, 8192 eval, 3 runs, CPU Colab, < 10 min per run): top-1 / rank — gra→gtr 0.99/1.02 (vec2vec 0.99/1.19); gtr→e5 0.98/1.06 (vec2vec 0.84/2.88); gtr→gte 0.98/1.05 (0.93/2.31); gte→gtr 0.98/1.04 (0.91/2.64); e5→gtr 0.96/1.10 (0.82/2.56); stella↔gte, stella↔e5, e5↔gte 1.00/1.00. Std over runs ≈ 0.00. Reported cosine after Refine-2 is 0.49–0.71 but is *not* comparable to vec2vec's because the mean vector (30–70% of the norm) was removed and the map does not alter geometry — [mini-vec2vec Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02348)
- mini-vec2vec failure modes: "extremely rare"; only gtr→gte occasionally converges to a sub-optimal solution, and failures are not catastrophic (~90% top-1, rank 5–10). Method is robust to hyperparameters; sample efficiency 60k vs 2M — [mini-vec2vec §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02348)
- mini-vec2vec's reading of the OT baselines: in vec2vec's *oracle* setup, OT/GW methods "didn't work better than the most naive baseline", so OT on raw sentence embeddings is insufficient even as a first step; the key is matching a *small* set of stable landmarks (clusters) rather than all points — [mini-vec2vec §3, §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02348)
- Related 2026 lead (not read): "SOTAlign: Semi-Supervised Alignment of Unimodal …" (arXiv 2602.23353) — appears in search results as a semi-supervised alignment paper; contents unverified — [arXiv 2602.23353](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23353)

### Inferences
- The strong vec2vec top-1 numbers are *matching* accuracies on pools of 8192 candidates where the true target exists; they are not retrieval nDCG on a real corpus, and the MS COCO result (R@16 0.23 vs 0.75) is the only retrieval-like datapoint and is poor. For the re-embedding use case, the MIMIC results are the warning sign: cosine stays high (0.7–0.85) while discriminative rank collapses — a translated query may look "close" yet rank wrong neighbours.
- mini-vec2vec's success with an orthogonal map implies that for 768-d sentence encoders trained with similar contrastive objectives the two spaces are approximately isometric after centering; this is the same assumption the [WORD] literature shows is fragile when training data/objectives differ (see Q3). Its evaluation is on the same five 768-d BERT/T5/RoBERTa-era models on in-domain NQ only; no OOD or dimension-mismatch (e.g., 768→1536/3072) results exist yet.
- vec2vec's own data-scale ablation (10k embeddings → top-1 0.01) shows fully unsupervised adversarial alignment needs ≥50k unpaired embeddings per side; mini-vec2vec needs ~26k. Both are far above the 1k–10k *paired* seeds that suffice for supervised Procrustes (Q4).

### Gaps
- Exact λ_rec, λ_CC, λ_VSP, latent dimension Z, layer counts and optimizer settings are only in the GitHub repo, which was not inspected.
- No independent replication or critique of vec2vec's numbers was found beyond mini-vec2vec; no peer-reviewed negative results on vec2vec surfaced in searches.
- No paper reports vec2vec/mini-vec2vec translations evaluated with standard retrieval metrics (nDCG@10 on BEIR/MTEB) in text-only settings.

## Key question 2: Classical unsupervised alignment of word vectors — adversarial (MUSE), Procrustes refinement, CSLS, self-learning (Artetxe), Gromov-Wasserstein, Wasserstein-Procrustes, Sinkhorn

### Takeaway
All classical [WORD] methods estimate an orthogonal map W ∈ O_d plus a correspondence; they differ in how the correspondence is bootstrapped (adversary, similarity-distribution sorting, GW coupling, convex relaxation of the permutation) and all converge to the same ~80–83% P@1 on en↔es/fr and ~72–75% on en↔de on the MUSE benchmark once Procrustes + CSLS refinement is applied — essentially matching 5k-pair supervised Procrustes — but they fall to 26–52% on en↔ru, 23–43% on en↔zh, and are much slower/less stable than Procrustes (3 min vs 37–957 min).

### Cited Findings — MUSE / Conneau et al. 2018 [WORD]
- Supervised baseline: with a seed dictionary {(x_i, y_i)}, W* = argmin_{W∈O_d} ‖WX − Y‖_F = UVᵀ where UΣVᵀ = SVD(YXᵀ) (Schönemann 1966 Procrustes) — [Conneau et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04087)
- Adversarial step: discriminator (MLP, 2 hidden layers of 2048, Leaky-ReLU, dropout on inputs) is trained to classify Wx_s vs y_t; W is trained to fool it (loss L_W = −(1/n)Σ log P_D(source=0 | Wx_i) − (1/m)Σ log P_D(source=1 | y_i)). SGD, batch 32, lr 0.1, decay 0.95; only the 50,000 most frequent words are fed to the discriminator because rare words hurt — [Conneau et al. 2018 §2.1, §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04087)
- Orthogonality is kept by the update W ← (1+β)W − β(WWᵀ)W after each step (β = 0.01 in the paper), keeping eigenvalues of W near 1 — [Conneau et al. 2018 §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04087)
- Refinement: build a synthetic dictionary of mutual nearest neighbours (under CSLS) among frequent words, then re-solve Procrustes; iterate — [Conneau et al. 2018 §2.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04087)
- CSLS (hubness fix): r_T(Wx_s) = (1/K) Σ_{y_t ∈ N_T(Wx_s)} cos(Wx_s, y_t) (K = 10 nearest target neighbours), r_S(y_t) analogous; CSLS(Wx_s, y_t) = 2cos(Wx_s, y_t) − r_T(Wx_s) − r_S(y_t). It penalises hubs (vectors in dense regions) and expands isolated regions — [Conneau et al. 2018 §2.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04087)
- Unsupervised model-selection criterion: mean cosine between the 10k most frequent source words and their CSLS translations; it correlates well with P@1 and is used for early stopping/lr decay — [Conneau et al. 2018 §2.4, §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04087)
- Results (P@1, fastText Wikipedia, en→es/es→en/en→fr/fr→en/en→de/de→en/en→ru/ru→en/en→zh/zh→en/en→eo/eo→en): Procrustes-NN (5k seed) 77.4/77.3/74.9/76.1/68.4/67.7/47.0/58.2/40.6/30.2/22.1/20.4; Procrustes-CSLS 81.4/82.9/81.1/82.4/73.5/72.4/51.7/63.7/42.7/36.7/29.3/25.3; Adv-CSLS (no refinement) 75.7/79.7/77.8/71.2/70.1/66.4/37.2/48.1/23.4/28.3/18.6/16.6; Adv-Refine-CSLS 81.7/83.3/82.3/82.1/74.0/72.2/44.0/59.1/32.5/31.4/28.2/25.6 — [Conneau et al. 2018 Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04087)
- The unsupervised model is within ≈3 points of supervised on en-es/en-fr but 7–10 points behind on en-ru and en-zh (no shared alphabet; refinement from a weak adversarial init gains less) — [Conneau et al. 2018 §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04087)
- Code and benchmark dictionaries: [facebookresearch/MUSE](https://github.com/facebookresearch/MUSE)

### Cited Findings — Artetxe, Labaka, Agirre 2018 (VecMap self-learning) [WORD]
- Initialization from structural similarity: with length-normalised, centered X, Z compute M_X = XXᵀ, M_Z = ZZᵀ; under exact isometry these are equal up to a row/column permutation. Sort each row independently → sorted(√M_X), sorted(√M_Z) (√M = USUᵀ is "closer in nature to the original embeddings"), normalise, and do nearest-neighbour retrieval between rows to get an initial dictionary — [Artetxe et al. 2018, §3.2](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1073.pdf)
- How weak the init is: on en-it, mean cosine over gold pairs is 0.009 (random), 0.582 (supervised optimum), 0.112 (this init); the init dictionary's accuracy is only 0.52%, yet it suffices to seed self-learning — [Artetxe et al. 2018, §3.2](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1073.pdf)
- Robust self-learning loop: (1) Procrustes on current dictionary; (2) re-induce dictionary with: stochastic dictionary induction (keep each similarity entry with prob p, p = 0.1 doubled whenever the objective improves < 1e-6 for 50 iterations — simulated annealing), frequency cutoff to the k = 20,000 most frequent words, CSLS retrieval, bidirectional induction, symmetric re-weighting — [Artetxe et al. 2018, §3.3–3.4](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1073.pdf)
- Results on the harder Dinu/Artetxe dataset (P@1, avg of 10 runs, en-it/en-de/en-fi/en-es): proposed 48.13/48.19/32.63/37.33, converging in 10/10 runs; Conneau et al. code 45.15/46.83/0.38/35.38 averaged over successful runs only, with only 3/10, 9/10, 0/10, 6/10 successes (>5% acc); Zhang et al. 2017 adversarial 0.00 everywhere. Supervised 5k-dict (Artetxe 2018a) gets 45.27/44.13/32.94/36.60 — [Artetxe et al. 2018 Tables 2–3](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1073.pdf)
- Code: [artetxem/vecmap](https://github.com/artetxem/vecmap)

### Cited Findings — Gromov-Wasserstein (Alvarez-Melis & Jaakkola, EMNLP 2018) [WORD]
- GW compares *intra-space* distance matrices rather than cross-space distances: GW(C, C', p, q) = min_{Γ∈Π(p,q)} Σ_{i,j,k,l} L(C_ik, C'_jl) Γ_ij Γ_kl, with C, C' cosine-distance matrices (normalised), L = L2; the discrete problem is a QAP (NP-hard, non-convex) — [Alvarez-Melis & Jaakkola 2018 §3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.00013)
- Solved by projected gradient (Peyré et al. 2016): each iteration builds a pseudo-cost Ĉ_Γ = C_xy − h1(C) Γ h2(C')ᵀ and projects onto Π(p,q) via entropic OT ⟨Γ, Ĉ⟩ − λH(Γ) with Sinkhorn-Knopp (K = exp(−Ĉ_Γ/λ)). Complexity O(N1²N2 + N1N2²) instead of O(N1²N2²) — [Alvarez-Melis & Jaakkola 2018 §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.00013)
- Γ* is itself a (soft, normalised) translation table, so no hubness correction is needed. To scale beyond K ≈ 20,000 words, recover an orthogonal map from the coupling: P* = UVᵀ with UΣVᵀ = X_{1:m} Γ* Y_{1:m}ᵀ, then map everything — [Alvarez-Melis & Jaakkola 2018 §3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.00013)
- Results on MUSE data (P@1, en→es/es→en/en→fr/fr→en/en→de/de→en/en→it/it→en/en→ru/ru→en; minutes per pair on a quad-core CPU): Procrustes 5k 77.6/77.2/74.9/75.9/68.4/67.7/73.9/73.8/47.2/58.2 (3 min); Procrustes+CSLS 81.2/82.3/81.2/82.2/73.6/71.9/76.3/75.5/51.7/63.7; Conneau unsup. 81.7/83.3/82.3/82.1/74.0/72.2/77.4/76.1/52.4/61.4 (957 min); GW λ=1e-4 78.3/79.5/79.3/78.3/69.6/66.9/75.3/74.1/26.1/35.4 (70 min); GW λ=1e-5 81.7/80.4/81.3/78.9/71.9/72.8/78.9/75.2/45.1/43.7 (37 min) — [Alvarez-Melis & Jaakkola 2018 Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.00013)
- Optimization properties vs adversarial: objective decreases monotonically, tracks translation accuracy, and does not degrade with over-training; convergence is faster for en-fr than en-ru. The authors caution that MUSE's high scores "belie the hardness of the general problem" because fastText Wikipedia vectors are large and highly comparable across languages — [Alvarez-Melis & Jaakkola 2018 §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.00013)

### Cited Findings — Wasserstein-Procrustes (Grave, Joulin, Berthet, AISTATS 2019) [WORD]
- Joint problem: W₂²(XQ, Y) = min_{Q∈O_d} min_{P∈P_n} ‖XQ − PY‖_F² (P a permutation). Each sub-problem is closed-form/LP given the other (Procrustes via SVD of XᵀPY; matching via Hungarian or Sinkhorn), but naive alternation "quickly converges to bad local minima even on small problems" — [Grave et al. 2019 §2–3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.11222)
- Stochastic algorithm: at each step draw batches X_t, Y_t of size b, solve the batch matching with Sinkhorn (regularised OT, O(b²)), take a gradient step on Q and re-project onto O_d; batch size starts at 500 and doubles at 1/3 and 2/3 of training. Initialised by a convex relaxation (Gold & Rangarajan: permutations → Birkhoff polytope of doubly-stochastic matrices) solved on the 2,500 most frequent words — [Grave et al. 2019 §3–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.11222)
- Same-language sanity check (English fastText trained with different seeds / window 2 vs 10 / skipgram vs cbow, first 10k points): convex-initialised stochastic algorithm recovers the ground-truth matching with accuracy 1.00 / 1.00 / 0.987 / 0.985 across the four settings, while random init "sometimes converges for the easy problems" and its success rate "rapidly decreases" — [Grave et al. 2019 Table 1, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.11222)
- Cross-lingual P@1 with refinement (ICP+CSLS; en-es/es-en/en-fr/fr-en/en-de/de-en/en-ru/ru-en): Procrustes (sup.) 82.7/84.2/82.7/83.4/74.8/73.2/51.3/63.7; Adversarial 81.7/83.3/82.3/82.1/74.0/72.2/44.0/59.1; ICP 82.1/84.1/82.3/82.9/74.7/73.0/47.5/61.8; Ours 82.8/84.1/82.6/82.9/75.4/73.3/43.7/59.1. Without refinement, Ours-CSLS 79.8/81.8/79.8/78.0/69.4/66.4/37.5/50.3 vs Adv-CSLS 75.7/79.7/77.8/71.2/70.1/66.4/37.2/48.1 — [Grave et al. 2019 Tables 2–3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.11222)
- Batch-size effect (P@1 after 4,000 iterations, NN retrieval): b = 100/200/400/800/1600 → en-es 68.5/73.8/74.9/75.0/76.3, en-ru 23.7/27.9/29.9/32.3/33.2; runtime 1m47s → 22m13s — [Grave et al. 2019 Table 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.11222)
- Non-adversarial alternative (Hoshen & Wolf 2018): PCA-based init + ICP, but needs hundreds (≈500) of random re-initialisations to find a good start — [Hoshen & Wolf 2018](https://aclanthology.org/D18-1043/); characterised in [mini-vec2vec §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02348)

### Inferences
- Across four very different bootstrapping strategies the end point is the same: an orthogonal Procrustes map refined by ICP/CSLS. The bootstrapping only has to produce a dictionary with slightly-above-chance precision (Artetxe's init had 0.52% accuracy, cosine 0.112 vs 0.009 random) — self-learning does the rest *when the spaces are near-isometric*.
- GW and the convex Wasserstein-Procrustes relaxation give monotone, non-adversarial optimisation on ≤20k–30k points; this is exactly the regime mini-vec2vec exploits (QAP on 20–30 cluster centroids). The lesson for [SENT]: run the combinatorial step on a small set of landmarks, not on all vectors (vec2vec's oracle-OT on 8192 raw sentence embeddings failed).

### Gaps
- Sinkhorn-only methods (e.g., Zhang et al. 2017b's EMD approach, Xu et al. 2018) were not read in primary form; Grave and Alvarez-Melis both use Sinkhorn as the inner solver, which is what the question most likely refers to.
- Ruder, Vulić & Søgaard's JAIR 2019 survey was not fetched; it is cited by the Procrustes-bounds paper as the comprehensive overview ([JAIR 65:569–631](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/11640)).

## Key question 3: The isomorphism assumption — when are two spaces approximately isometric, when does unsupervised alignment fail, and what predicts alignability?

### Takeaway
Unsupervised alignment presupposes that the two spaces are (approximately) isometric; the [WORD] literature shows this holds for large, same-domain, same-algorithm Wikipedia fastText spaces of related languages and fails — to literally 0% P@1 — when corpora differ in domain, when the embedding *algorithms* differ (skip-gram vs CBOW, even for en-es), when a language is morphologically rich/under-resourced, or when one side is under-trained. Alignability is predicted by eigenvalue similarity of nearest-neighbour-graph Laplacians (Søgaard) and by Gromov-Hausdorff distance (Patra), and a 2025 theorem gives a *tight, data-independent* bound: Procrustes error ≤ (2D)^{1/4}·√(Gram-matrix discrepancy).

### Cited Findings — Søgaard, Ruder, Vulić 2018 "On the Limitations of Unsupervised Bilingual Dictionary Induction" [WORD]
- Eigenvector (isospectrality) similarity: build kNN graphs G1, G2 of the two embeddings, Laplacians L_i = D_i − A_i; choose the smallest k such that the top-k eigenvalues sum to > 90% of the spectrum (take the min k over the two graphs); Δ = Σ_{i=1}^{k} (λ_1i − λ_2i)². Δ = 0 means isospectral; approximately isomorphic ⇒ approximately isospectral (not conversely). In the experiments Δ is computed on 10 random source NN-subgraphs of 10 nodes and their translation subgraphs — [Søgaard et al. 2018 §2, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03620)
- Table 2 (P@1, MUSE adversarial unsupervised / weakly supervised with identical-string seed / Δ): EN-ES 81.89 / 82.62 / 2.07; EN-ET 0.00 / 31.45 / 6.61; EN-FI 0.09 / 28.01 / 7.33; EN-EL 0.07 / 42.96 / 5.01; EN-HU 45.06 / 46.56 / 3.27; EN-PL 46.83 / 52.63 / 2.56; EN-TR 32.71 / 39.22 / 3.14; ET-FI 29.62 / 24.35 / 3.98. The abstract claims a "near-perfect correlation" between Δ and unsupervised BDI performance — [Søgaard et al. 2018 Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03620)
- Data size is not the fix: training Finnish on the large Finnish WaC corpus still gave P@1 = 0.0 — [Søgaard et al. 2018 §4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03620)
- Random restarts: for Estonian, Finnish and Greek roughly 1 in 10 runs lands on par with Turkish, i.e., the failure is partly optimisation instability — [Søgaard et al. 2018 fn. 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03620)
- Domain mismatch (EuroParl vs Wikipedia vs EMEA medical): unsupervised P@1 is "close to zero" whenever the two corpora come from different domains; en-es drops from 81.19% to 46.52% just by using the smaller Wikipedia corpora; identical-word weak supervision still "achieves decent performance in many set-ups" — [Søgaard et al. 2018 §4.3, Fig. 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03620)
- Algorithm mismatch (Table 3, en-es): same skip-gram both sides 81.89; window 10 vs 2 → 81.28; char n-gram 2-7 vs 3-6 → 80.74; both changed → 80.15; but English skip-gram vs Spanish CBOW → 0.00 / 0.07 / 0.00 / 0.13 in every setting, although the Spanish CBOW space is monolingually fine (Spearman ≈ 0.7 on SemEval-2017). Conclusion: "the unsupervised approach is unlikely to work on pre-trained word embeddings unless they are induced on same-domain data using the same underlying algorithm" — [Søgaard et al. 2018 §4.4, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03620)
- Dimensionality: at 40-d, P@1 falls for es/hu/pl/tr (50.33/21.81/20.11/22.03) but *rises* for et/fi/el (13.53/15.33/24.17 vs ≈0 at 300-d), suggesting over-fitting to language-specific peculiarities at high dimension — [Søgaard et al. 2018 §4.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03620)

### Cited Findings — Vulić, Ruder, Søgaard 2020 "Are All Good Word Vector Spaces Isomorphic?" (EMNLP 2020) [WORD]
- Variance in alignability across language pairs "can mostly be attributed to the size of the monolingual resources available" and to under-training, rather than typology; same-language embeddings trained on different corpora or different random seeds show surprisingly low isomorphism; metrics used are eigenvector similarity, Gromov-Hausdorff distance and relational similarity (RSIM). Unsupervised alignment works when both spaces are well-trained on substantial, comparable data — [Vulić et al. 2020 (abstract-level)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04070)

### Cited Findings — Patra et al. 2019 BLISS (ACL 2019) [WORD]
- Proposes Gromov-Hausdorff distance (min over isometries of the Hausdorff distance, approximated by the bottleneck distance) as a quantitative test of the isometry assumption; shows it weakens as languages become etymologically distant; proposes a semi-supervised objective combining a small seed lexicon with the unsupervised (adversarial) signal and a relaxed (weak) orthogonality constraint plus hubness filtering; SOTA on 15/18 MUSE pairs, "particularly well when the embedding spaces don't appear to be isometric" — [Patra et al. 2019 (abstract-level)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06625)

### Cited Findings — Theory for orthogonal alignment of two embedding models (Maystre et al. 2025, "When Embedding Models Meet: Procrustes Bounds and Applications") [SENT]
- Theorem 1: for X, Y ∈ R^{D×N} with ‖XᵀX − YᵀY‖_F ≤ ε, min_{Q∈O_D} ‖QX − Y‖_F ≤ (2D)^{1/4} √ε; the dependence on D and ε is tight (explicit equality example). Corollary 1: if E_{i,j}[(x_iᵀx_j − y_iᵀy_j)²] ≤ δ² then E_i‖Q*x_i − y_i‖² ≤ √(2D)·δ (independent of N). Corollary 2 (unit-norm): cross-model similarities after alignment deviate from both models' own cosines by at most √(2D)·δ in mean square. Prior bounds (Tu et al. 2016, Arias-Castro et al. 2020) scale as ε/σ_min(X), i.e., O(Nδ²), and need full rank — [Maystre et al. 2025 §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- Empirically, for 21 pairs of 7 sentence encoders the measured Procrustes distance lies well below the bound and follows the predicted power-law trend vs dot-product preservation — [Maystre et al. 2025 Fig. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- Motivation stated: loss functions depend only on distances/dot-products, so trained embeddings are under-specified up to an orthogonal transform; this is why orthogonal alignment is the right model class when two models "capture similar geometrical relationships" — [Maystre et al. 2025 §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)

### Cited Findings — evidence from the vec2vec family on sentence encoders [SENT]
- Same-backbone BERT sentence encoders (gte, e5, stella) are already nearly aligned with the identity map (naive cos 0.38–0.68, top-1 0.99–1.00), whereas cross-backbone pairs (T5-based gtr, RoBERTa-based granite vs BERT) have naive cos ≈ 0 and random rank — [vec2vec Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- vec2vec's latents for the same text across models reach cosine 0.75–0.96 even when inputs have cosine ≤ 0.03 — [vec2vec Fig. 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- The one pair with a large capability/architecture gap (Qwen3-4B 2560-d vs gte) yields cosine only 0.50 in the gte→qwen direction, which the authors attribute to differences in architecture, training methodology, size (37× larger), context length and recency — [vec2vec App. C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)

### Inferences
- The [WORD] failure conditions map directly onto the [SENT] question: "different algorithm" ≈ different training objective/backbone family; "different domain corpora" ≈ different pre-training mixtures; "under-trained space" ≈ small/weak model. Expect orthogonal alignment to be good between contrastively trained encoders of similar vintage and dimension (where mini-vec2vec succeeds), and to degrade for (a) much larger/newer LLM-based embedders (Qwen3 result), (b) multimodal spaces (CLIP results), (c) Matryoshka/low-rank truncated spaces, and (d) spaces with very different dimensionality.
- Søgaard's Δ (Laplacian eigenvalue similarity on kNN subgraphs) and the Gram-discrepancy δ of Maystre et al. are cheap pre-flight diagnostics that *do not need paired data* (Δ) or need only a small paired sample (δ). They can tell you in advance whether unsupervised translation has a chance for a given model pair.

### Gaps
- Numerical GH distances per language pair and the exact BLISS objective (Patra 2019) could not be extracted (PDF text extraction failed); only abstract claims are recorded.
- No paper computes Søgaard-style eigenvector similarity or GH distance for pairs of *sentence* encoders; the only [SENT] isomorphism diagnostic found is the Gram-discrepancy vs Procrustes-distance plot in Maystre et al.

## Key question 4: Weak supervision — how many paired/seed examples are needed, and does a small seed close the gap to full supervision?

### Takeaway
[WORD]: 500–1,000 seed pairs + robust self-learning converge in 210/210 language pairs and beat fully unsupervised in every setup (avg MRR 0.274 at 1k vs 0.140 unsupervised; 87/210 unsupervised failures vs 0), and are within ≈0.03 MRR of 5k seeds. [SENT]: supervised orthogonal Procrustes between sentence encoders saturates at roughly 10,000 paired documents, and with it a different query encoder can be used against a fixed corpus index; without it cross-model retrieval nDCG@10 is ≈0.

### Cited Findings — Vulić, Glavaš, Reichart, Korhonen 2019 "Do We Really Need Fully Unsupervised Cross-Lingual Embeddings?" (EMNLP 2019) [WORD]
- 15 languages, 210 BLI setups; the most robust unsupervised method (VecMap, Artetxe 2018b; seed extraction over the 4K most frequent words) yields (near-)zero performance in 87/210 setups (41.4%) at MRR ≤ 0.01 (94/210 at MRR ≤ 0.05); weakly supervised methods seeded with 500–1,000 pairs and the same self-learning (+symmetric reweighting) have 0 unsuccessful setups and "never" lose to the unsupervised model — [Vulić et al. 2019 abstract, Table 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01638)
- Average MRR over all setups: UNSUPERVISED 0.140; 1K:FULL+SL+SYM 0.274; 5K:FULL+SL+SYM 0.302; 1K seed *without* self-learning only 0.101 (so self-learning, not the seed size, is what closes the gap); FULL+SL+SYM beats plain supervised Procrustes in 210/210 setups when |D0| = 500 or 1,000 and in 164/210 when |D0| = 5k — [Vulić et al. 2019 Table 3, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01638)
- On setups where unsupervised failed completely (MRR 0.000–0.005), 1K seeds give MRR 0.137–0.306 and 500 seeds give 0.109–0.285 — [Vulić et al. 2019 Table 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01638)
- Identical strings (shared spellings) also serve as a free seed: Søgaard et al. obtain 31.45 (en-et), 28.01 (en-fi), 42.96 (en-el) P@1 from identical-string seeds where adversarial training got 0 — [Søgaard et al. 2018 Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03620)
- Self-learning from 25 word pairs already reaches 37.27 (en-it) / 39.60 (en-de) / 28.16 (en-fi) vs 45.27 / 44.13 / 32.94 with 5k pairs (Artetxe 2017/2018a numbers) — [Artetxe et al. 2018 Table 3](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1073.pdf)

### Cited Findings — supervised Procrustes between sentence encoders (Maystre et al. 2025) [SENT]
- Setup: 7 public encoders (nomic-embed-text-v1.5, bge-small-en-v1.5, Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, all-MiniLM-L6-v2, sentence-t5-base, LaBSE, rubert-tiny2), 3 MMTEB retrieval tasks (HotpotQA-HN, FEVER-HN, TREC-COVID). Q* learned from 10,000 corpus documents sampled at random and embedded by both models; dimension mismatch handled by zero-padding the smaller space; queries embedded with model B are mapped by Q* and matched against the fixed model-A corpus index — [Maystre et al. 2025 §4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- Without alignment, every off-diagonal (cross-model) nDCG@10 is 0 (a handful of 1–26 for bge-small↔MiniLM). With Procrustes-aligned queries, e.g., HotpotQA corpus=nomic (own baseline 73): bge-small query 30, Qwen3 31, MiniLM 24; FEVER corpus=nomic (87): bge-small 52, Qwen3 37; TREC-COVID corpus=nomic (82): bge-small 72, Qwen3 68, MiniLM 48, sentence-t5 51; corpus=rubert-tiny2 (baseline 9) with nomic queries 46 — i.e., upgrading the query encoder on a weak fixed index can *raise* nDCG well above the index model's own score — [Maystre et al. 2025 Fig. 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- Orthogonal vs unconstrained linear: orthogonal is better in most cells, by up to +20 (FEVER, bge-small queries on nomic corpus) and +32 (TREC-COVID, Qwen3 queries on LaBSE corpus); unconstrained linear only helps when "downgrading" to a weaker query model — [Maystre et al. 2025 Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- Sample size: Procrustes distance and nDCG@10 vs number of paired samples (10¹–10⁶) "appear to saturate after roughly 10,000 samples" across 21 model pairs — [Maystre et al. 2025 Fig. 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- Between two Matryoshka-trained models Q* mostly aligns the first 16–32 leading dimensions of each space with each other — [Maystre et al. 2025 §4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- MovieLens retraining experiment (not text): orthogonal post-processing gives the best similar-item recall@100 among alignment methods and beats unconstrained linear on genre classification; methods that change training (warm-start, autoencoding regulariser, BC-Aligner) do better still — [Maystre et al. 2025 §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)

### Cited Findings — scale of *unpaired* data needed by the unsupervised [SENT] methods (for contrast)
- vec2vec needs ≥50k unpaired source embeddings for near-full performance (10k → top-1 0.01) — [vec2vec Table 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- mini-vec2vec uses ~26k unpaired embeddings per side — [mini-vec2vec §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02348)

### Inferences
- The [WORD] evidence says the marginal value of seeds is steep from 0 → 500 and nearly flat from 1k → 5k, *provided* an ICP/self-learning refinement is run on top. mini-vec2vec's Refine-1 is exactly such a refinement; seeding it with a few hundred re-embedded documents instead of cluster matching should remove its (already rare) failure modes and its need for 20–30k unpaired vectors.
- For the re-embedding question, the Maystre et al. numbers are the most directly relevant: with 10k paired docs an orthogonal map lets a *new* query encoder work against an *old* index, but cross-model nDCG is typically 40–90% of the index model's own score and far below the new model's own score (e.g., Qwen3 85 on FEVER vs 37 when mapped onto nomic's index). Translation is a bridge, not a replacement for re-indexing when top retrieval quality matters.
- Combining the two lines: seeds anchor the map (supervised Procrustes), the full unlabeled corpus refines it (ICP on nearest-neighbour averages). No paper was found that explicitly benchmarks this hybrid on sentence encoders.

### Gaps
- No [SENT] study sweeps seed size for a *hybrid* (seed + self-learning) pipeline; the 10k-saturation figure is for pure supervised Procrustes.
- Maystre et al. report nDCG as integers in heat-maps; exact per-cell values beyond those transcribed here were not extracted.

## Key question 5: Relative representations / anchor-based approaches that avoid learning a map

### Takeaway
Relative representations (Moschella et al., ICLR 2023) replace a vector by its cosine similarities to K anchor inputs embedded in each model (r(z) = [cos(E(z), E(a_1)), …, cos(E(z), E(a_K))]); this is exactly invariant to rotations/reflections and isotropic scaling, so no map is learned — but it *requires parallel anchors* (the same K inputs embedded by both models), i.e., it is weakly supervised by K ≈ 300–768 paired items, and stitching accuracy is typically below same-model accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- Definition and invariance: r_{A,E}(z) = [sim(E(z), E(a_1)), …, sim(E(z), E(a_k))] with cosine sim; if z and all anchors are transformed as z ↦ αUz (U orthogonal, α > 0) the relative representation is unchanged — invariance to linear isometries and isotropic rescaling. Core observation: "the angles between the encodings within distinct latent spaces do not change" across training runs/architectures under consistent data — [Moschella et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15430); invariance statement restated in [Lähner & Moeller 2024 §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10967)
- Word vectors [WORD]: fastText vs word2vec on a 20k shared English vocabulary with 300 randomly drawn *parallel* anchors — absolute spaces: neighbourhood Jaccard 0.00, MRR 0.00, cosine 0.01; relative: Jaccard 0.34–0.39, MRR 0.94–0.98, cosine 0.86 — [Moschella et al. 2023 Table 1, via full-text mirror](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/ICLR/2023/relative_representations_enable_zeroshot_latent_space_communication__98b7a849.txt)
- Text [SENT-ish; frozen transformer encoders + trained classifier heads]: encoders bert-base-cased, bert-base-uncased, electra-base-discriminator, roberta-base; 768 anchors; datasets TREC, DBpedia, Amazon Reviews coarse/fine. Cross-lingual Amazon coarse zero-shot stitching (English encoder → decoder trained on another language's encoder): es 43.67 (absolute) → 82.78 (relative); fr 54.41 → 78.49; ja 48.72 → 65.72 — [Moschella et al. 2023 Table 4, via mirror](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/ICLR/2023/relative_representations_enable_zeroshot_latent_space_communication__98b7a849.txt)
- Cross-architecture text table (as extracted; interpretation uncertain — the "Absolute" column is most likely the non-stitched same-encoder reference and "Relative" the stitched cross-encoder mean): TREC 91.70±1.39 vs 75.89±5.38; DBpedia 98.62±0.58 vs 80.47±21.14; Amazon coarse 87.81±1.58 vs 72.37±7.32; Amazon fine 55.35±3.19 vs 33.24±7.21 — i.e., stitching across BERT/ELECTRA/RoBERTa via 768 anchors loses 15–22 points and is high-variance — [Moschella et al. 2023 Table 5, via mirror](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/ICLR/2023/relative_representations_enable_zeroshot_latent_space_communication__98b7a849.txt)
- Anchors must be in correspondence across the two domains: "we can sample anchors A_X ⊆ P_X and obtain corresponding anchors on the other domain directly as Γ(A)" where Γ is a (partial) correspondence; performance improves monotonically with the number of anchors when the absolute encoders are frozen — [Moschella et al. 2023 §3, Fig. 6, via mirror](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/ICLR/2023/relative_representations_enable_zeroshot_latent_space_communication__98b7a849.txt)
- Robust variant (Lähner & Moeller, NeurIPS 2024 workshop / arXiv 2409.10967): batch-normalise before the relative transform, trading invariance to general isometries for invariance to permutations, non-isotropic (diagonal) rescalings and shifts (symmetries of ReLU/GELU networks); approximation error shrinks as O((ln m)/√m) in dimension m. RoBERTa, Amazon Reviews (1%), 768 anchors: en→fr accuracy absolute 24.28±10.11, relative 40.96±2.40, robust 49.92±1.51; fr→en 24.96±9.27 / 35.42±1.16 / 60.74±0.88 — [Lähner & Moeller 2024 Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10967)
- vec2vec classifies relative representations and related stitching methods as relying on paired data that is "difficult to reduce", citing Cannistraci et al. 2023 "Bootstrapping Parallel Anchors for Relative Representations" — [vec2vec §8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540); [Cannistraci et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00721)
- mini-vec2vec makes relative representations unsupervised by using matched k-means centroids (c = 20–30, ensembled over 30 runs) as anchors, then uses the relative space only to find pseudo-pairs before fitting an explicit orthogonal map; a "centroid-only" variant that skips relative representations also converges after refinement — [mini-vec2vec §3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02348)

### Inferences
- For the re-embedding use case, relative representations are a *K-dimensional, map-free* alternative: embed K anchor texts with both models once, then represent every stored vector and every new query by cosine-to-anchors. It needs only K paired texts (hundreds to ~768), no training, and is exactly invariant to the orthogonal ambiguity. The costs are (i) a new K-d index (cannot reuse the existing vector index as-is), (ii) loss of fidelity relative to the native space (15–22 point drops in the stitching tables above), and (iii) sensitivity to anchor choice and to non-isometric differences, which the robust variant only partly fixes.
- Relative representations and Procrustes are complementary: the relative space is where mini-vec2vec finds correspondences; given correspondences, an orthogonal map restores the native geometry (and index compatibility).

### Gaps
- Moschella et al.'s Table 5 column semantics could not be verified from the primary PDF (fetch exceeded size limits); the mirror text was summarised by a secondary model. Treat the exact cross-architecture numbers as approximate.
- Cannistraci et al. 2023 (bootstrapping anchors with fewer parallel pairs) was not read; no numbers recorded.

## Key question 6: Results specifically on sentence/document embedding models (model pairs, datasets, metrics)

### Takeaway
Only four sources give sentence-encoder numbers: vec2vec (5 × 768-d encoders + CLIP + Qwen3; NQ, TweetTopic, MIMIC, Enron, MS COCO; cosine/top-1/rank), mini-vec2vec (same 5 encoders; NQ; top-1/rank), Maystre et al. (7 encoders incl. Qwen3-0.6B, nomic, bge-small, MiniLM, sentence-t5, LaBSE, rubert-tiny2; MMTEB HotpotQA-HN/FEVER-HN/TREC-COVID; nDCG@10, supervised with 10k pairs), and Moschella/Lähner (BERT-family classifiers via 768 anchors; TREC/DBpedia/Amazon; accuracy/F1). None evaluates an unsupervised map with standard retrieval metrics on a text corpus.

### Cited Findings (consolidated [SENT] table)
- vec2vec, unsupervised, NQ in-domain: cos 0.74–0.92, top-1 0.82–1.00, rank 1.00–5.19 over 8192 candidates for gtr/gte/e5/stella/granite pairs; CLIP pairs top-1 0.00–0.72; Qwen3↔gte cos 0.50/0.84 — [vec2vec Tables 2, 4, 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- vec2vec OOD: TweetTopic top-1 0.53–1.00; MIMIC top-1 0.01–0.60 (cross-backbone) — [vec2vec Table 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- vec2vec cross-modal retrieval MS COCO: R@16 0.23 (granite→clip) vs 0.75 (clip native) — [vec2vec Table 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)
- mini-vec2vec, unsupervised linear, NQ: top-1 0.96–1.00, rank 1.00–1.10 for all 20 directed pairs among gtr/gte/e5/stella/granite; < 10 CPU-minutes — [mini-vec2vec Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02348)
- Maystre et al., supervised orthogonal (10k pairs), MMTEB retrieval: cross-model nDCG@10 goes from ≈0 to 24–72 depending on pair/dataset; orthogonal > unconstrained linear; saturation at ~10k pairs; zero-padding for dimension mismatch — [Maystre et al. 2025 Figs. 4–6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- Relative representations, weakly supervised (768 parallel anchors), BERT/ELECTRA/RoBERTa classification stitching: cross-lingual Amazon 65.7–82.8 (vs 43.7–54.4 absolute); cross-architecture drops of ~15–22 points vs native — [Moschella et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15430); [Lähner & Moeller 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10967)
- Same-backbone contrastive encoders (gte/e5/stella, all BERT-base 768-d) are near-identical up to small rotations: identity map already gives top-1 0.99–1.00 on 8192-way matching — [vec2vec Table 2 naive baseline](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540)

### Inferences
- Evidence quality ranking for the feasibility question: (1) Maystre et al. — real retrieval metric, real corpora, but supervised; (2) mini-vec2vec — unsupervised, cheap, but matching metric on one in-domain dataset and five similar-era 768-d models; (3) vec2vec — broadest model coverage and OOD tests, but matching metric only, expensive, unstable; (4) relative representations — classification only.
- Nothing yet shows an *unsupervised* map achieving retrieval quality comparable to re-embedding; the closest proxies (MIMIC top-1 collapse; MS COCO R@16 0.23 vs 0.75; supervised cross-model nDCG at 40–90% of the index model's own score) all point to a measurable quality loss.

### Gaps
- No results for the most common production pairs (e.g., OpenAI text-embedding-3 ↔ open models, Cohere, Voyage, 1024/1536/3072-d spaces), for Matryoshka-truncated targets with unsupervised methods, or for long-document embeddings.
- No study of how translation error compounds in approximate-nearest-neighbour indexes (HNSW/IVF-PQ) built on the original space.
