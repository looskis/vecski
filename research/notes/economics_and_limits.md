# Embedding Translation: Economics of Re-embedding vs. Translating, and Theoretical Limits on Translation Quality

Research date: 2026-10-06. All prices/dates were checked against live pages on this date unless noted; third-party price trackers are flagged as such.

## Q1. Current embedding API prices and self-hosted throughput/cost

### Takeaway
Hosted embedding APIs in late 2026 cluster at $0.02–$0.20 per 1M tokens (text-embedding-3-small, voyage-4-lite and Titan V2 at the $0.02 floor; text-embedding-3-large $0.13; Gemini Embedding 2 $0.20 standard / $0.10 batch), with batch discounts of 33–50% where offered. Self-hosting a ~0.5B-parameter encoder (bge-m3) on an H100 at max batch runs ~640K tokens/s, i.e. ~$0.001–0.0015 per 1M tokens — 15–100x cheaper than APIs — while 7–8B "LLM-embedders" (Qwen3-Embedding-8B, e5-mistral) run ~27–30K tokens/s (~$0.024/M tokens), i.e. still comparable to or cheaper than the cheapest APIs.

### Inferences
- The RunPod H100 figure (1,240 texts/s x 512 tokens = ~635K tok/s) and the Embedder's Dilemma figure (640K tok/s) for bge-m3 agree almost exactly, so ~0.6M tok/s per H100 is a robust planning number for 0.5B encoders at max batch; the Spheron 60–90K figures reflect low-concurrency/online serving and should be treated as the *floor* you get with a poorly batched pipeline (a ~7–10x spread driven purely by batching/engine).
- Cost per token of a self-hosted 7–8B embedder (~$0.02–0.025/M at spot) roughly equals the cheapest API ($0.02/M for 3-small/voyage-4-lite/Titan V2) and is ~5–8x cheaper than premium APIs ($0.12–0.20/M); a 0.5B encoder is 15–200x cheaper than any API. Cost is therefore *not* the dominant barrier for small/medium corpora; wall-clock, rate limits, and engineering are.

### Gaps
- No official per-token price could be retrieved from Cohere, Mistral, or Jina's own pages (Cohere now foregrounds instance-priced Embed 5; Jina shows token packages only post-login). Values above for those are from third-party trackers and should be re-verified before publication.

## Q2. Worked cost model: re-embedding vs. running a translator; dual-index and text-retrieval costs

### Takeaway
At ~400 tokens/chunk, re-embedding costs scale as $8 (1M chunks) → $8K (1B) → $80K (10B) on the cheapest APIs and $52 → $52K → $520K on text-embedding-3-large; self-hosting a 0.5B encoder cuts this to ~$0.4 → $440 → $4.4K but costs ~7 GPU-days per 1B chunks per H100. A learned translator (linear or small MLP) costs ~$10–200 per 1B vectors and runs in minutes-to-hours — a 100x+ reduction documented independently by Google (Embedding-Converter) and Rutgers (Drift-Adapter) — but the dominant costs of a migration (vector-DB write units, dual-index storage, and recovering source text) are often larger than the embedding bill and are only avoided by *query-side* translation that leaves the old index untouched.

### Cited Findings
- Embedding-Converter's cost appendix (256 tokens/doc assumed): re-embedding 1B docs with OpenAI-3-large = $16,640 and 4,266 hours at 1M TPM rate limit; with 3-small = $2,560 / 4,266 h; with Gecko-004 = $20,480 / 2,222 h. Converting instead: $185 / 37 h (3-large target), $115 / 23 h (3-small), $75 / 15 h (gecko004). For 50M docs: $832→$10 and 213 h→1.9 h (3-large). Converter inference alone on 50M docs "takes only 20 minutes" on 2 V100s ($4.96/hr on Google Cloud); the converter is a 4-layer MLP with hidden widths 5x output dim (35M params for gecko003→gecko004), 50,000 Adam iterations at batch 64. — [Embedding-Converter, ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1237.pdf)
- Drift-Adapter cost comparison for a 1M-item database: full re-indexing ~100 GPU-hours plus 4–8 hours of downtime; dual-index serving doubles resource consumption during transition; adapter training ~0.5 GPU-hours (>100x reduction). Adapters add 3 μs (orthogonal Procrustes), ~5 μs (low-rank affine r=64), ~8 μs (residual MLP) per query. — [Drift-Adapter, arXiv 2509.23471](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23471v1)
- Show HN "Drift" creator: "a billion vectors is ~$6k and a stale index for most of a day"; rotation matrix computed via SVD from "a few thousand paired vectors" in ~15 seconds on CPU. — [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48475082)
- Re-embedding MS MARCO v2 (138M docs) "with 8xH200 GPUs and 8x22 vCPUs exceeds 10 hours"; via OpenAI APIs ~$166 (3-small) and ~$1,079 (3-large). — [arXiv 2604.05480 "Black-Hole Attack"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.05480)
- A worked example from a migration guide: 100M vectors x 300 tokens = 30B tokens x $0.13/M = $3,900 on text-embedding-3-large. — [ResumeLens blog](https://www.resumelens.org/blog/ai/embedding-model-migration)
- Pinecone serverless (2026): storage $0.33/GB/month; Standard plan read units ~$16–18/M, write units ~$4–4.50/M (Enterprise ~$24–27 and ~$6–6.75); 1 WU per 1 KB written (min 5 WU/request); 1 RU per 1 GB of namespace scanned (min 0.25 RU/query); $50/month minimum. — [Pinecone pricing](https://www.pinecone.io/pricing/md/); [hackceleration](https://hackceleration.com/labs/pinecone-pricing)
- Qdrant: "Re-embedding requires access to the original data used to create the embeddings. This data can come from a primary database, or it may be stored in the payloads of the points." Blue-green migration "duplicates payloads across both collections. For text-heavy collections where the payload is large, this can have a significant impact"; named vectors (v1.18+) avoid copying but hold both vectors until the old one is deleted. — [Qdrant migration tutorial](https://qdrant.tech/documentation/tutorials-operations/embedding-model-migration/)
- Weaviate: migration via collection aliases requires re-vectorizing into a new collection; the add-a-second-vector method causes "permanent storage increase" because "the old vector cannot be deleted". — [Weaviate vectorizer migration](https://docs.weaviate.io/weaviate/tutorials/vectorizer-migration)
- MongoDB/Voyage: "Always regenerate the embeddings for your entire corpus with the successor model"; with autoEmbed, changing `model`, `numDimensions` or `quantization` "regenerates the index and the embeddings, which incurs additional embedding costs"; old index keeps serving until rebuild completes; recommended batch size 100 and monitoring token usage because "re-embedding a large corpus can exhaust your rate limits". — [MongoDB Voyage migration docs](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/voyageai/tutorials/migrate-embedding-model/)
- Gradual-rollover (dual vector fields fused with RRF) raises query latency "roughly 1.5x to 2x". — [Hackernoon](https://hackernoon.com/your-embedding-model-will-deprecate-heres-what-to-do)
- Ridge/OLS excess risk ≈ σ²(d + 2√(dt) + 2t)/n when n ≥ 6ρ₀²d(log d + t) (relevant to translator training cost being negligible: a linear map is a closed-form solve). — [Hsu, Kakade, Zhang 2012](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1106.2363)

### Inferences (worked model; all assumptions explicit)

**Assumptions.** t = 400 tokens/chunk (so 1M chunks = 0.4B tokens; 100M = 40B; 1B = 400B; 10B = 4T). API prices from Q1. Self-host: bge-m3 at 640K tok/s/H100 at $2.49/hr spot (= $0.0011/M); Qwen3-Embedding-8B at 29.3K tok/s (= $0.0236/M). Translator FLOPs use a *not-fetched* assumption of ~400 TFLOP/s sustained bf16 GEMM on an H100 (roughly 40% of the ~990 TFLOPS dense-BF16 datasheet peak); treat as order-of-magnitude.

**(a) Re-embedding cost, C_embed = N · t · p**

| Corpus | Tokens | 3-small / voyage-4-lite / Titan V2 ($0.02) | 3-large ($0.13) | voyage-4 ($0.12; batch $0.08) | Gemini Emb 2 ($0.20; batch $0.10) | Self-host bge-m3 ($0.0011) | Self-host Qwen3-E-8B ($0.0236) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1M | 0.4B | $8 | $52 | $48 / $32 | $80 / $40 | $0.44 | $9.4 |
| 100M | 40B | $800 | $5,200 | $4,800 / $3,216 | $8,000 / $4,000 | $44 | $944 |
| 1B | 400B | $8,000 | $52,000 | $48,000 / $32,160 | $80,000 / $40,000 | $440 | $9,440 |
| 10B | 4T | $80,000 | $520,000 | $480,000 / $321,600 | $800,000 / $400,000 | $4,400 | $94,400 |

**(b) Re-embedding wall-clock.** API: bounded by rate limits — at the 1M TPM tier assumed by Google's paper, 400B tokens = 400,000 min ≈ 6,700 hours ≈ 9 months of continuous calls; at a 10M TPM tier, ~28 days; batch APIs give a 12–24 h completion window per job but still subject to per-org throughput. Self-host bge-m3: 400B / 640K tok/s = 625,000 s ≈ 174 GPU-hours ≈ 7.2 days on one H100 (≈22 h on 8 H100s). Qwen3-Embedding-8B: 400B / 29.3K = 13.7M s ≈ 3,800 GPU-hours ≈ 158 days on one H100, ~20 days on 8, ~2.5 days on 64 — i.e., for 1B+ chunks with an LLM-class embedder you need a multi-node fleet or weeks. 10B chunks: 10x all of the above (1,740 H100-hours for bge-m3; 38,000 H100-hours for an 8B embedder ≈ $95K at spot).

**(c) Translator cost.** Linear map W ∈ R^{d_in x d_out}: 2·d_in·d_out FLOPs/vector = 9.4 MFLOP for 1536→3072 (= 2.4 KB fp32 input, 12 KB fp32 output). 1B vectors = 9.4e15 FLOP ≈ 24 s of H100 GEMM at 400 TFLOP/s; the job is I/O-bound: reading 1B x 1536 x 4 B = 6.1 TB and writing 12.3 TB. A 35M-param MLP (Embedding-Converter scale): ~70 MFLOP/vector → 7e16 FLOP ≈ 3 min of H100 GEMM per 1B vectors; Google's measured unoptimized rate (50M docs in 20 min on 2 V100) extrapolates to ~7 GPU-hours/1B vectors ≈ $17 on their $4.96/hr pair, or $75–185 all-in including data loading per their table. CPU-only: 9.4 MFLOP/vector at ~50 GFLOP/s/core (AVX-512 sgemm, assumption) ≈ 5K vectors/s/core → a 32-core box ≈ 160K vectors/s ≈ 1.7 h per 1B vectors — translation is feasible without any GPU. **Net: translator compute is $10–200 per 1B vectors vs $440–$80,000 to re-embed; the 100x figure from both papers is conservative for API targets and ~2–20x for self-hosted small encoders (where the I/O of translation dominates).**

**(d) Hidden costs that translation does NOT avoid when documents are translated A→B (forward translation):**
- Vector-DB writes: Pinecone bills 1 WU per KB; 1B vectors x 3072 fp32 dims = 12.3 KB each → ~12.3B WU ≈ $49K–55K (Standard) just to upsert the new index — 6x the $8K API bill for 3-small and larger than the 3-large bill. (For 1536-d, ~$25K.) This is identical whether vectors come from re-embedding or translation.
- Dual-index storage during cutover: 1B x 1536 x 4 B = 6.1 TB at $0.33/GB/month ≈ $2,000/month per copy (12.3 TB / ~$4,000 for 3072-d); self-managed Qdrant/Weaviate instead double RAM/disk and, in blue-green, duplicate payload text.
- Query cost during dual-serving: RRF over two indexes ≈ 1.5–2x latency and ~2x read units.
- **Query-side translation (map new-model queries B→A into the old index; Drift-Adapter's design) avoids all of (d)** — zero writes, zero extra storage, +3–8 μs/query — at the price of being capped by the old model's quality (see Q8).

**(e) Cost/feasibility of retrieving the original text.** Re-embedding is *impossible*, not merely expensive, when text is unavailable: Qdrant and MongoDB both require the source text from a primary DB or payload; cases where it is absent include ephemeral/streaming data embedded on ingest and discarded, licensed corpora where only derived vectors may be retained, privacy regimes where text was deleted but vectors kept, and third-party-supplied vectors. (This is an inference from the provider docs; no source quantified how common vector-only stores are — see Gaps.) Where text exists but lives in a separate system, the re-embedding job also pays an egress/scan pass over the full corpus (e.g., 1B x ~1.6 KB of text ≈ 1.6 TB read).

### Gaps
- No public data on what fraction of production vector stores lack recoverable source text.
- Current OpenAI/Voyage/Gemini per-tier TPM limits for embeddings were not retrieved; wall-clock estimates above use the 1M TPM figure from the 2025 Google paper plus a 10M TPM hypothetical.
- H100 sustained GEMM throughput and CPU GFLOP/s figures are assumptions, not fetched measurements.

## Q3. Evidence of demand: deprecation history and documented migration pain

### Takeaway
Embedding deprecations are now routine and on fixed clocks: OpenAI killed 16 first-generation embedding models on Jan 4, 2024 (6 months' notice); Google retired embedding-001 (Aug 14, 2025), text-embedding-004 (Jan 14, 2026) and textembedding-gecko@003 (May 2025); Cohere retired all three embed-v2.0 models on April 4, 2026; Azure sets an 18-month GA lifecycle (embeddings get "extended timelines," currently all retiring 2028-02-09) and grants no exceptions. Practitioner evidence (OpenAI forum, GitLab, dev.to, HN) consistently frames re-embedding as mandatory, operationally painful, and the thing people most want to avoid.

### Cited Findings

**Deprecation record**
- OpenAI announced on July 6, 2023 and shut down on Jan 4, 2024 all first-generation embedding models: text-similarity-{ada,babbage,curie,davinci}-001, text-search-*-{doc,query}-001, code-search-*-{code,text}-001; recommended replacement text-embedding-3-small. Policy: ≥6 months' notice for GA models, ≥3 months for specialized variants, as little as 2 weeks for previews. text-embedding-ada-002 is not listed as deprecated and is still priced ($0.10/M). — [OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations); [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)
- Hackernoon counts this as "16 first-generation models" shut down Jan 4, 2024. — [Hackernoon](https://hackernoon.com/your-embedding-model-will-deprecate-heres-what-to-do)

Comparing query embeddings from a new model against stored embeddings from an older one degrades retrieval quality" — re-embedding required "even when dimensions and element types match". — [MongoDB docs](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/voyageai/tutorials/migrate-embedding-model/)
- Hackernoon survey: "No company has publicly confirmed deploying these [alignment] techniques in production"; full re-embedding remains "the actual practice" at Uber, Pinterest, Google; quality pull is also a driver (MIRACL 31.4% ada-002 → 44.0–54.9% on newer OpenAI models). — [Hackernoon](https://hackernoon.com/your-embedding-model-will-deprecate-heres-what-to-do)
- Open-source issue trackers requesting automatic embedding migration without re-extracting sources: [Thoth-Software/cake #283](https://github.com/Thoth-Software/cake/issues/283); [michaelkrauty/mcp-docs #96](https://github.com/michaelkrauty/mcp-docs/issues/96)

- Every major provider has retired at least one embedding generation in the last 30 months, with notice windows of 6 months (OpenAI) to ~12 months (Google, Azure GA policy); Cohere's 2026 v2 retirement and Google's Jan 2026 text-embedding-004 shutdown are the most recent forced migrations. ## Q4. Who else is attacking this (commercial and open source)

### Takeaway
The problem is now an active research and product niche: Google Cloud AI's Embedding-Converter (ACL 2025) proposes vendors ship converters with new models; Rutgers' Drift-Adapter (EMNLP 2025) and the open-source Show-HN "Drift" do query-side Procrustes/MLP alignment; Cornell's vec2vec (NeurIPS 2025) shows *unsupervised* translation is possible; and vector databases are productizing re-embedding itself (MongoDB autoEmbed auto-regenerates on model change; Qdrant named vectors; Weaviate aliases; Pinecone integrated inference), while backward-compatible training (Apple FCT, Tencent BCT, 2025–26 adapter papers) attacks it from the model-training side.

### Cited Findings
- Embedding-Converter (Yoon & Arik, Google Cloud AI, ACL 2025): 4-layer MLP with regression + global-similarity + local-similarity (k=100 neighbors) losses; "By promoting the release of converters alongside new embedding models, Embedding-Converter fosters a more dynamic… ecosystem"; converted embeddings fall between source and target quality; limitations: "converted embedding performance does not fully match that of the target model, meaning re-embedding is still necessary to achieve the full potential". — [ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1237.pdf)
- Drift-Adapter (Vejendla, Rutgers, EMNLP 2025): maps new queries into the legacy space; Orthogonal Procrustes, low-rank affine, residual MLP; recovers 95–99% of Recall@10/MRR of full re-embedding on MTEB corpora and a CLIP ViT upgrade (1M items); <10 μs latency; >100x recompute reduction. — [arXiv 2509.23471](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23471v1)
- Drift (Show HN, 2026): orthogonal rotation via SVD from a few thousand pairs, applied at query time; enforces a 0.97 recall threshold and falls back to full re-index otherwise; GloVe→MPNet only ~71.5% recall. — [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48475082)
- vec2vec (Jha, Zhang, Shmatikov, Morris, Cornell, NeurIPS 2025): unsupervised translation through a shared latent (Platonic Representation Hypothesis), no paired data; framed as a vector-database security threat. — [arXiv 2505.12540](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4); [NeurIPS 2025](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/4175dee33d6145cb8f0323703d138a53-Abstract-Conference.html)
- Backward/forward-compatible training line: Forward Compatible Training (Apple, 2021) — [arXiv 2112.02805](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.02805); Binary embedding BCT at Tencent — [arXiv 2302.08714](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.08714); Query Drift Compensation for continual retrieval models (2025) — [arXiv 2506.00037](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00037); Multi-modal Knowledge Preserving Adapter for backward compatibility (2026) — [arXiv 2609.16875](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16875v1); Spherical Interpolation for Backward-Compatible Multimodal Representations (2026) — [arXiv 2609.39836](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39836v1); Align-then-Adapt adapters for asymmetric dense retrieval (2026) — [arXiv 2604.03403](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03403)
- Vector-DB productization: MongoDB Vector Search autoEmbed re-embeds the corpus automatically when the index's `model` changes (billing the embedding cost) — [MongoDB](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/voyageai/tutorials/migrate-embedding-model/); Qdrant named vectors (1.18+) for in-collection migration — [Qdrant](https://qdrant.tech/documentation/tutorials-operations/embedding-model-migration/); Weaviate collection aliases with zero-downtime swap — [Weaviate](https://docs.weaviate.io/weaviate/tutorials/vectorizer-migration); Pinecone integrated inference (index accepts text, Pinecone embeds) — [Pinecone](https://www.pinecone.io/blog/integrated-inference/); Zilliz FAQ "change embedding model without re-indexing" — [Zilliz](https://zilliz.com/ai-faq/change-embedding-model-without-reindexing)
- "Can You Trust the Vectors in Your Vector Database? Black-Hole Attack from Embedding Space Defects" (2026) cites re-embedding cost as the reason stale/defective vectors persist. — [arXiv 2604.05480](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.05480)
- Vendor-side compatibility promise exists only within a model lineage (Google exp→GA gemini-embedding-001: no re-embed needed). — [Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/gemini-embedding-available-gemini-api/)

### Inferences
- No vendor currently ships an official cross-generation converter (e.g., OpenAI ada-002→3-large), despite Google's own paper advocating it; the commercial gap is a *productized, validated* translator with quality guarantees and fallback — exactly what Drift's "0.97 recall threshold or fall back" hints at.
- Vector DBs are converging on making re-embedding cheap to *orchestrate* (auto-embed, named vectors), which reduces operational pain but not API cost, rate-limit time, write units, or the no-source-text case; a translator is complementary to, not displaced by, those features.

### Gaps
- No funded startup specifically selling embedding translation was identified beyond the open-source Drift project; "embedding versioning" as a named DB feature was not found at Pinecone/Turbopuffer in the fetched sources.

## Q5. Information-theoretic ceiling on translation quality and how to estimate it

### Takeaway
With A = e_A(text) and B = e_B(text), the Bayes-optimal translator is f*(a) = E[B | A = a] and the irreducible error is E[Var(B | A)] — nonzero exactly to the extent that B depends on text features A discarded. Because embeddings of short texts are nearly invertible (vec2text: 92% exact recovery of 32-token inputs from GTR, 61% from ada-002, falling to 8% exact at 128 tokens), the ceiling is near-perfect for short chunks and degrades with length; empirically, the best supervised converters today recover 26–54% of the quality gap between source and target models (Embedding-Converter) and 95–99% of recall when mapping *into* the old space (Drift-Adapter), so observed gaps are a mix of true irreducible error and estimator limits that the protocol below separates.

### Cited Findings
- MMSE estimator: for square loss, the optimal estimator of B from A is the conditional expectation E[B|A], and the minimum MSE equals E[Var(B|A)] (law of total variance). — [Wikipedia: Minimum mean square error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_mean_square_error)
- Data processing inequality: for a Markov chain X→Y→Z, I(X;Z) ≤ I(X;Y); no post-processing of Y can increase information about X. — [Wikipedia: Data processing inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_processing_inequality)
- vec2text (Morris et al., EMNLP 2023): on 32-token inputs, GTR-base inversion reaches 92.0% exact match, BLEU 97.3, token F1 99; OpenAI ada-002 on 32-token MS MARCO: 60.9% exact, BLEU 83.4, token F1 96; 128-token ada-002: 8.0% exact, BLEU 55.0, token F1 84; "reconstruction accuracy inversely correlates with example length"; reconstructions re-embed to cosine ≈ 0.99 with the original; BEIR out-of-domain token F1 41–99% depending on corpus. — [arXiv 2310.06816](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06816)
- vec2vec (unsupervised, no pairs): translated-vs-true cosine 0.85–0.92 with top-1 up to 1.00 and mean rank 1.00 for same-backbone pairs (GTE↔Stella, E5↔Stella); cross-backbone e.g. GTE→GTR 0.87 / top-1 0.95 / mean rank 1.18, Granite→GTE 0.87 / 0.95 / 1.18; OOD on tweets 0.85–0.86 cosine; on MIMIC notes GTE→E5 top-1 fell to 0.54 (mean rank 17.7); CLIP cross-modal pairs degrade to cosine 0.62–0.78 and top-1 0.00–0.61; GTE↔Qwen (4B) underperformed, attributed to "architecture, training methodology, size (14x larger), context length"; naive identity baseline ≈ random (mean rank ~4,100 of 8,192); zero-shot attribute inference 24–38% (tweets) to up to 98% (MIMIC); up to 80% of Enron emails leaked information under inversion; only 3/15 GAN seeds converged for some cross-backbone pairs. Trained on 1M unpaired embeddings per model; 50K "almost as good", 10K degraded. — [arXiv 2505.12540v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- Embedding-Converter (supervised MLP, 35M params): in-domain BEIR nDCG@10 average — gecko003 0.5067 (source), gecko004 0.5609 (target), converted 0.5362; openai-3-small→gecko004: source 0.5209, target 0.5609, converted 0.5314 (1536-d → 768-d). Out-of-domain (CQADupStack): gecko003→004 source 0.4271 / target 0.4814 / converted 0.4542; openai→gecko: 0.4250 / 0.4814 / 0.4408. Per-dataset, converted beat the target on Arguana (0.6103 vs 0.6070) and TREC-COVID (0.8079 vs 0.7840) but lost badly on HotpotQA (0.5923 vs 0.6892, below source 0.6248). Ablation: 5x larger network 0.5329 vs base 0.5369 (no gain); 1/5x network 0.5263; training only on MS MARCO 0.5194 (diversity matters more than capacity). — [ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1237.pdf)
- Drift-Adapter: Procrustes recovers 95–97% Recall@10, low-rank affine 97–98%, residual MLP 98–99% of full re-embedding; "even with only 5,000 paired samples, an ARR of over 0.97"; saturates by ~20K pairs; GloVe→transformer only 71.5%; 1–5% of long-tail/boundary queries unrecovered. — [arXiv 2509.23471](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23471v1)

### Inferences (formal treatment)
- **Setup.** Text T ~ P; A = e_A(T) ∈ R^{d_A}, B = e_B(T) ∈ R^{d_B} (deterministic encoders, so A ⟂ B | T, i.e., A→T→B is Markov). For any translator f, E‖f(A) − B‖² = E‖f(A) − f*(A)‖² + E[Var(B|A)]; the second term is the floor, the first is estimation error. By DPI, I(f(A); B) ≤ I(A; B) ≤ min(I(A;T), I(T;B)): a translator can never reconstruct aspects of B that depend on text content A did not encode.
- **When is the floor ≈ 0?** If T is recoverable from A (A is injective on the support), then B = e_B(T(A)) is a deterministic function of A and Var(B|A) = 0; the only question is estimability. vec2text's 92% exact recovery (32 tokens, GTR) and ~0.99 re-embedding cosine show that for ≤32-token texts A is *practically* injective, so the ceiling for short chunks is essentially perfect translation. At 128 tokens exact recovery drops to 8% but token F1 stays 84 and re-embedding cosine ~0.99 — i.e., the embedding fixes the *semantics* but not the exact wording; since e_B also mostly depends on semantics, Var(B|A) stays small for retrieval purposes but is nonzero for the lexical component of B. For 400–500-token chunks (the typical RAG setting), no inversion data exists; extrapolating the monotone decline, a materially larger irreducible component should be expected and measured.
- **Why observed converters fall short of the floor.** Embedding-Converter's converted embeddings recover only 54% (gecko→gecko in-domain), 50% (OOD), and 26%/27% (OpenAI→gecko, in/out-domain) of the source→target gain. Three facts suggest much of this is estimation, not floor: (i) a 5x larger MLP did not help (so not capacity-starved); (ii) data diversity gave +3.4 nDCG points (0.5194→0.5369) — coverage of the input manifold is the bottleneck; (iii) per-dataset results exceed the target on some sets — a translator can only *beat* the target by noise/regularization, which indicates a high-variance estimator. Drift-Adapter's 98–99% recall recovery in the reverse direction (into the old space, where no new information is required) is the cleanest empirical estimate of how little is lost to geometry alone.
- **Empirical protocol to estimate the ceiling (recommended).** (1) Draw n paired (a_i, b_i) over the *deployment* text distribution (not just MS MARCO). (2) Fit a nested family: Procrustes → full linear/ridge → MLP (widths 1x–5x d_B) → MLP with local/global structure losses; evaluate held-out MSE/cosine and downstream Recall@k against native B. (3) Plot held-out error vs n (log scale): the plateau of the highest-capacity model is an *upper bound* on E[Var(B|A)] (any remaining gap is unestimated structure), and the linear-vs-MLP plateau gap bounds what nonlinearity buys. (4) Stratify by chunk length and language to expose where the floor rises. (5) Where feasible, a constructive lower bound on achievable quality is the inversion pipeline b̂ = e_B(vec2text_A(a)): expensive (iterative decoding) but it directly instantiates "recover T then re-embed", so its cosine with b is an achievable-quality witness for short chunks. (6) Report the "twin" oracle: cosine/recall of *native* B against B re-embedded from a paraphrase of T — this calibrates how much of the measured error is below the noise floor of the target model itself (dev.to's 82% top-result overlap between two native models illustrates how low cross-model agreement already is).

### Gaps
- No published inversion or translation results exist for 400–500-token chunks; the ceiling for standard RAG chunk sizes is unmeasured.
- No paper reports both a supervised linear baseline and the vec2vec unsupervised method on the same pairs, so the paired-data advantage is unquantified (Embedding-Converter does not compare against Procrustes; Drift-Adapter does not compare against a vec2vec-style model).

## Q6. Statistical sample complexity of learning the map (linear and MLP)

### Takeaway
For a linear map R^{d1}→R^{d2}, each output coordinate is an OLS/ridge problem with d1 parameters whose excess risk is ≈ σ² d1/n once n ≳ 6ρ² d1 log d1 (Hsu–Kakade–Zhang); for d1=1536 that means ~10K pairs adds ~15% of the noise floor as estimation error and ~100K pairs ~1.5%, with ridge replacing d1 by a smaller effective dimension because embedding spectra decay fast. Empirically, Procrustes/affine adapters saturate at 5K–20K pairs (Drift-Adapter), unsupervised vec2vec needs ~50K unpaired embeddings, and Google's 35M-param MLP trains on millions of diverse samples and is diversity-limited rather than size-limited.

### Cited Findings
- Hsu, Kakade & Zhang (COLT 2012), random-design OLS: if n ≥ 6ρ₀²d(log d + t), the excess MSE is bounded by ≈ σ²(d + 2√(dt) + 2t)/n with probability 1 − 3e^{−t}; for ridge, n ≥ 6ρ_λ² d_{1,λ}(log d̃_{1,λ} + t) and the leading noise term is σ² d_{2,λ}/n with effective dimensions d_{1,λ}, d_{2,λ} < d that shrink as eigenvalues decay. — [ar5iv 1106.2363](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1106.2363); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v23/hsu12.html)
- Drift-Adapter: 20,000 pairs (2% of 1M items) is near-optimal; 5,000 pairs already gives ARR > 0.97; low-rank affine uses r = 64. — [arXiv 2509.23471](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23471v1)
- Drift (Show HN): orthogonal rotation from "a few thousand paired vectors," solved by SVD in ~15 s on CPU. — [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48475082)
- vec2vec (unsupervised): 1M embeddings per model used; 50K "almost as good"; 10K "still learns something" but degraded. — [arXiv 2505.12540v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- Embedding-Converter: 35M-param MLP, batches of 64 sampled uniformly from 14 BEIR datasets for 50,000 iterations (≈3.2M samples seen), validation on SciFact (5,183 passages); 1/5x-size network loses ~1 nDCG point, 5x-size gains nothing; MS-MARCO-only training loses 1.75 points. — [ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1237.pdf)

### Inferences (worked numbers)
- Treat the d2 output coordinates as d2 independent regressions sharing the design matrix; total excess MSE = d2 · σ²_coord · d1/n = (irreducible MSE) · (d1/n). Hence the *relative* estimation overhead is d1/n regardless of d2:
  - 1536→3072 (ada-002/3-small → 3-large): n = 10K → +15.4% of the floor; n = 100K → +1.5%; n = 1M → +0.15%. The HKZ validity condition n ≥ 6ρ²·1536·log(1536) ≈ 68K·ρ² suggests OLS is unreliable below ~100K pairs without ridge; with ridge the effective d is the number of principal directions carrying most variance, typically a few hundred for anisotropic text embeddings, so 10K–20K pairs suffices in practice — consistent with Drift-Adapter's 5K–20K saturation.
  - 3072→1536 or 768-d targets: same d1/n logic; the output dimension does not change the sample requirement.
  - Orthogonal Procrustes has only d(d−1)/2 free parameters constrained to a rotation and is the most sample-efficient (few thousand pairs) but the least expressive (95–97% recall in Drift-Adapter); low-rank affine (r=64) sits between.
- For MLPs the classical bound (∝ √(P/n) for P params) is vacuous at P = 35M, and the empirical evidence says the binding constraint is *coverage* of the input manifold (domain diversity), not n per se: a translator trained on one domain will be well-estimated on that domain's subspace and extrapolate poorly elsewhere. Practical rule from the evidence: ≥10K pairs per *domain/language stratum* you expect to serve, drawn from the deployment corpus, then check the error-vs-n curve has flattened.
- Paired data is cheap to obtain in the deprecation setting: embedding 20K–100K sampled chunks with the new model costs $0.16–$5 at 400 tokens/chunk on 3-large (0.4–2 min of H100 time self-hosted), so sample complexity is not an economic obstacle; the obstacle is having source text for the *sample* (which can be a tiny curated subset even when the bulk corpus lacks text).

### Gaps
- No source gives learning curves (error vs n) for a *full* linear map at d=1536–3072 on real embedding pairs; the 10K/100K numbers above are theory-derived.
- Effective dimension d_λ of specific embedding models (spectrum decay) was not retrieved; treat "a few hundred" as an assumption.

## Q7. How translation error propagates to Recall@k (with quantization as the calibrated analogy)

### Takeaway
Retrieval is remarkably tolerant to per-vector distortion: int8 quantization keeps ~99.3% of nDCG@10, binary (1 bit/dim, which is equivalent to a per-vector cosine of only ~0.80 with the original) keeps 92.5% and 96.45% with int8 rescoring — Cohere reports 99.99% (int8) and 90–98% (binary). Translation errors in the cosine-0.85–0.92 range (vec2vec) are therefore in the regime where recall losses of a few percent are expected, and the 2026 "score-gap survival" model (fidelity governed by score margin / noise std, with coherent perturbation of neighbors making naive independent-noise estimates 3–4x too pessimistic) gives a way to predict the loss for a given translator from its residual statistics.

### Cited Findings
- HuggingFace/mixedbread embedding quantization (MTEB retrieval, nDCG@10): int8 retains ~99.3% with 3.66x speedup and 4x memory reduction; binary retrieval alone 92.53%, binary + int8 rescoring 96.45%, 24.76x speedup, 32x memory reduction. Per model: mxbai-embed-large-v1 (1024-d) float32 54.39 → int8 52.79 (97%) → binary+rescore 52.46 (96.45%); e5-base-v2 (768-d) 50.77 → int8 47.54 (94.68%) → binary 37.96 (74.77%). 250M embeddings on AWS: $3,623/mo float32 → $905 int8 → $113 binary (mxbai). — [HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/embedding-quantization)
- Cohere (March 2024): int8 (1 byte/dim) 4x memory reduction "while keeping 99.99% of the original search quality"; binary (1 bit/dim) 32x reduction keeping "90-98%"; rescoring top-30 binary hits with int8 from disk lifts quality from 95% to 99.99%. — [Cohere blog](https://cohere.com/blog/int8-binary-embeddings); [Simon Willison summary](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Mar/26/cohere-int8-binary-embeddings)
- Binary quantization at 41M Wikipedia texts with rescoring "retaining over 95% of float32 retrieval accuracy", 30x compression. — [ReadyTensor](https://app.readytensor.ai/publications/scaling-semantic-search-to-41-million-documents-with-binary-quantized-embeddings-BMKDEIBrpL9l)
- "Closed Forms and Synthetic Twins" (2026): predicts ANN recall under transforms (PQ/binary/int8) with a gap-survival model, logit(p) ≈ β₀ + β₁·log(score_margin / noise_std), where p is the fraction of the original top-k surviving; "near neighbors perturb coherently so the naive independent-perturbation model over-predicts fidelity loss by 3-4x"; predictions within 0.03 of measured recall on an unseen million-document corpus. — [arXiv 2609.00364](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00364)
- "SVD Provably Denoises Nearest Neighbor Data" (2026): for points on a k-dim subspace with isotropic Gaussian noise of per-coordinate variance σ², the true nearest neighbor is recoverable (after SVD) when σ ∈ O(1/k^{1/4}) and not identifiable when σ ≫ 1/k^{1/4}. — [arXiv 2604.03831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03831)
- Drift-Adapter measured recall recovery: 95–97% (Procrustes), 97–98% (affine), 98–99% (MLP) of Recall@10/MRR vs full re-embedding; 1–5% of queries (rare entities, semantic boundaries) unrecovered. — [arXiv 2509.23471](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23471v1)
- vec2vec translated vectors reach cosine 0.85–0.92 with top-1 nearest-neighbor accuracy 0.95–1.00 among 8,192 candidates. — [arXiv 2505.12540v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- Two native models (text-embedding-004 vs text-embedding-3-large) agree on only 82% of top results on average across test queries. — [dev.to](https://dev.to/humzakt/zero-downtime-embedding-migration-switching-from-text-embedding-004-to-text-embedding-3-large-in-1292)

### Inferences (error-propagation model)
- **From cosine to score noise.** For unit vectors, if cos(f(a), b) = c then ‖f(a) − b‖² = 2(1 − c). A query q (unit) scores s' = q·f(a) = s + q·e with e = f(a) − b. If e is not aligned with q, Var(q·e) ≈ ‖e‖²/d_eff where d_eff is the effective dimension of the residual; so translation cosine 0.90 (‖e‖ = 0.45) yields per-document score noise of std ≈ 0.45/√d_eff — tiny relative to score gaps for d_eff in the hundreds, which is why top-1 accuracy stays ~1.0 at cosine 0.87–0.92 in vec2vec. The damaging component is the part of e *correlated with queries* (systematic, e.g., the translator collapsing a topic), which the gap-survival model captures through the margin/noise ratio and which the coherence finding says is partially shared by true neighbors (they move together, preserving order).
- **Calibration against binary quantization.** For a vector with roughly Gaussian coordinates, cos(x, sign(x)) = E|x|/σ = √(2/π) ≈ 0.80 (derivation, not a cited number). Binary quantization is thus a *per-vector cosine 0.80* perturbation that still keeps 92.5% of nDCG@10 unrescored and 96.45% rescored (HF) / 90–98% (Cohere). A translator achieving cosine 0.85–0.92 with the true target embedding is a *smaller* perturbation than binary quantization in norm terms — consistent with Drift-Adapter's 95–99% recall retention — but its errors are structured (semantic) rather than i.i.d., so the analogy is a sanity bound, not a guarantee; measure recall directly.
- **Recall@k is more tolerant than Recall@1.** Rank swaps only hurt when a true top-k item crosses the k-th boundary; with k=10–100 and a reranker on top, typical translation noise is absorbed (the quantization pipelines above achieve their 96–99.99% figures precisely by over-fetching then rescoring). The same over-fetch-and-rescore design — translate docs, retrieve top-3k, rescore with a native-B cross-encoder or with int8 native B vectors for the fraction of docs that have been re-embedded lazily — is the natural way to make translation losses invisible in production.
- **Where it breaks.** The SVD-denoising result formalizes the failure mode: if residual noise exceeds ~1/k^{1/4} of the signal scale on the semantic subspace, true neighbors become unidentifiable; empirically this is the GloVe→MPNet (71.5%) and cross-modal CLIP (top-1 0–61%) regime — large architecture/training gaps, not dimension mismatches, are what push translators past the tolerance threshold.

### Gaps
- No paper reports the residual-cosine distribution of a translator *together with* measured Recall@k on the same corpus, which is what's needed to fit the gap-survival model for translation specifically; the quantization numbers are an analogy.
- The √(2/π) binary-cosine figure and the score-noise variance argument are derivations, not sourced measurements.

## Q8. Mixed-space retrieval: native-B queries vs translated A→B documents (and the reverse)

### Takeaway
Measurements show the asymmetry is benign but not free: Google's Embedding-Converter, evaluated exactly in the "queries native in B, corpus translated into B" setting, lands between source and target (recovering ~26–54% of the quality gap), and translating the *corpus* beats translating the *queries* in every setting (0.5362 vs 0.5263 in-domain gecko; 0.5314 vs 0.5171 OpenAI→gecko). Running new-model queries directly against old-model vectors with no translation loses up to 12 nDCG points (QDC paper) and is explicitly warned against by Voyage/MongoDB, while query-side compensation into the old space recovers to within ~1–3 points — and in a continual-learning setting even beat re-indexing because documents drift more than queries.

### Cited Findings
- Embedding-Converter Table 4 (nDCG@10, in-domain / out-of-domain): gecko003→gecko004 — source 0.5067/0.4271, target 0.5609/0.4814, **corpus converter 0.5362/0.4542, query converter 0.5263/0.4348**; openai-3-small→gecko004 — source 0.5209/0.4250, target 0.5609/0.4814, **corpus converter 0.5314/0.4408, query converter 0.5171/0.4342**. Protocol: "Queries are consistently encoded using the target model (gecko004) to isolate" the corpus-conversion effect. Appendix D covers reverse conversion, mixed embeddings, open-source↔black-box, and multilingual cases. — [ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1237.pdf)
- Query Drift Compensation (2025): new-model queries against old-model corpus without compensation — MS MARCO nDCG@10 40.2→34.8 (−5.4), HotpotQA −12.4, NQ −4.3 after 5 tasks; with KD + QDC (project new queries into the old space) MS MARCO 39.3 (−0.9), HotpotQA −3.4; average 54.1 nDCG@10 matching joint training; Recall@10 on MS MARCO 60.6 vs 54.2 without QDC; re-indexing with the new model scored lower (50.4 avg) than QDC (54.1), attributed to "unequal drift in query and document embeddings," documents drifting more because they are longer. — [arXiv 2506.00037](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00037)
- MongoDB/Voyage: comparing new-model query embeddings against old-model stored embeddings "degrades retrieval quality" even with matching dimensions; re-embed the whole corpus. — [MongoDB](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/voyageai/tutorials/migrate-embedding-model/)
- OpenAI forum moderator: new text-embedding-3 vectors are "absolutely incompatible" with ada-002; suggested running separate databases and fusing results with score remapping. — [OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/new-embedding-model-mapping-with-old-ada002-possible/607804)
- Drift-Adapter maps *queries* into the legacy space and keeps the old ANN index; Drift (Show HN) likewise applies the rotation at query time. — [arXiv 2509.23471](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23471v1); [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48475082)
- Align-then-Adapt (2026): a lightweight adapter transforms the query space toward the document space; "asymmetric retrieval consistently outperforming zero-shot retrieval across different document embedders" without re-indexing. — [arXiv 2604.03403](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03403)
- Backward-compatible training (Tencent binary BCT): "new query embeddings can be directly compared with old document embeddings" by constraining training, so the index need not be refreshed. — [arXiv 2302.08714](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.08714)

### Inferences
- Two distinct architectures with different ceilings: **(i) forward, A→B on documents, native-B queries** — can exploit the new model's query understanding and approaches target quality as translation improves; measured at 26–54% of the gain today. **(ii) backward, B→A on queries, untouched A index** — zero re-write cost, 95–99% of *old-model* recall retained, but quality is capped at the old model (plus whatever the new query encoder adds through better query representations, which QDC suggests can be non-trivial). A migration product should offer (ii) as the instant bridge and (i) as the quality path, with lazy native re-embedding of hot documents on top.
- Asymmetry is benign in the sense that no measured setting shows mixed-space retrieval *below* the source model when a translator is used (every converted number in Table 4 exceeds the source), whereas untranslated mixing is strictly harmful (QDC, Voyage). The residual gap to target is larger out-of-domain (converter recovers ~50% in-domain vs ~29–50% OOD), so the translator's training distribution must match the query distribution.
- Corpus-side translation beating query-side translation (by ~1–1.5 nDCG points) is consistent with queries being short (little to lose encoding them natively) and documents being long (where the new model's representation matters most).

### Gaps
- No study evaluates a *hybrid* (translated docs + progressively re-embedded docs in one index) for ranking-consistency artifacts; score-calibration between translated and native vectors within one index is unmeasured.
- QDC's finding that re-indexing underperformed is specific to continual fine-tuning of one model, not to switching vendors; do not generalize.

## Q9. Known hard limits: information the old model never encoded

### Takeaway
Translation cannot recover anything the source encoder discarded: truncated tails beyond the old context window, languages or modalities the old model was not trained on, and fine-grained distinctions it collapsed — the DPI makes this absolute, and empirical failure cases (GloVe→MPNet 71.5%, text↔CLIP top-1 near 0, GTE↔Qwen-4B underperformance, 1–5% long-tail queries) are where these gaps are largest.

### Cited Findings
- DPI: I(f(A); B) ≤ I(A; B) for any f. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_processing_inequality)
- vec2vec: cross-modal (text models → CLIP) translations degrade to cosine 0.62–0.78 and top-1 0.00–0.61 (mean rank up to 3,233 of 8,192); GTE↔Qwen (4B, 14x larger, different context length) underperformed; MIMIC OOD GTE→E5 top-1 0.54. — [arXiv 2505.12540v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- Drift-Adapter: GloVe→transformer upgrade only 71.5% recall recovery; a single global adapter "assumes uniform drift"; 1–5% of long-tail queries unrecovered; "Deferral, Not Elimination: full re-embedding eventually necessary". — [arXiv 2509.23471](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23471v1)
- Embedding-Converter: HotpotQA converted 0.5923 < source 0.6248 < target 0.6892 (a multi-hop dataset where conversion *lost* ground); has "not yet explored multimodal embeddings". — [ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1237.pdf)
- vec2text: inversion accuracy "inversely correlates with example length" (92% exact at 32 tokens → 8% at 128 for ada-002), i.e., embeddings of long inputs do not pin down their text. — [arXiv 2310.06816](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06816)
- Cohere embed-english-v2.0 (English-only) was retired in favor of multilingual v3/v4; Embed v4 adds image input ($0.47/M tokens) and a 128K context. — [Cohere changelog](https://docs.cohere.com/changelog/2026-04-04-embed-v2-aya-8b-retirement); [Maxim](https://www.getmaxim.ai/bifrost/model-library/compare/cohere/embed-v4.0)
- MTEB quality gap that motivates upgrades (and that translation cannot fully close): MIRACL 31.4% (ada-002) → 44.0–54.9% (newer OpenAI); 3-large ≈ +14 points MTEB retrieval over ada-002. — [Hackernoon](https://hackernoon.com/your-embedding-model-will-deprecate-heres-what-to-do); [ResumeLens](https://www.resumelens.org/blog/ai/embedding-model-migration)

### Inferences
- **Context-window truncation is the most common unrecoverable case in RAG**: if chunks were embedded by a 512-token model (e.g., e5-base) and the new target handles 8K, every token past 512 contributes to B but is absent from A; for such chunks E[Var(B|A)] is large and no translator can help — the only fix is re-embedding those chunks (which can be *targeted*: chunk length is known metadata, so a hybrid plan re-embeds only over-length chunks and translates the rest).
- **Language/modality gaps** behave the same way (English-only v2 → multilingual v4: non-English chunks were encoded as near-noise and must be re-embedded); vec2vec's near-zero CLIP results show that even strong unsupervised methods cannot bridge modality-level gaps.
- **Capability gaps are partially translatable**: the new model's better *query* understanding is fully available in forward translation (queries are native), and the measured 26–54% gain recovery in Embedding-Converter is the corpus-side share; the residual is the part of B that depends on text features A blurred.
- **Diagnostic**: stratify the held-out translation error by chunk length, language, and source-model confidence (e.g., A-vector norm/anisotropy); strata with error far above the global plateau are the "never encoded" set and should be scheduled for native re-embedding first. This turns the hard limit into a budgeting rule: re-embed the unrecoverable fraction, translate the remainder.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies the share of a typical RAG corpus affected by old-model truncation or unsupported languages.
- Appendix D of Embedding-Converter (multilingual and open↔black-box conversions) was not extracted in detail; its numbers would sharpen the language-gap claims.
