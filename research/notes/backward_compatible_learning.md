# Backward-Compatible Representation Learning, Model Stitching, and Backfill-Free Embedding Upgrades (prior-art notes)

Scope: computer-science literature (2015–2026) on serving a NEW embedding model's queries against a gallery/index embedded by an OLD model without re-embedding ("backfilling"). Notation used throughout, following Shen et al.: `φ_q/φ_g` = metric when queries are embedded by φ_q and the gallery by φ_g; "cross-test" = new/old; "self-test" = new/new; "paragon" = fully backfilled new/new.

---

## KQ1. Backward Compatible Training (BCT) and its training-time follow-ups: formulations, results, trade-offs

### Takeaway
BCT (Shen, Xiong, Xia, Soatto; AWS; CVPR 2020) formalized the problem and showed that adding an "influence loss" (run the frozen OLD classifier on NEW features) makes new/old retrieval beat old/old on IJB-C face search (70.7% vs 59.3% TPIR@FPIR=1e-2) at a ~1–3% cost to new/new accuracy; a plain ℓ2 regression toward old features fails (3.1% TAR). Every follow-up (LCE, UniBCT, RACT/Hot-Refresh, AdvBCT, Darwinian, BT², stationary d-Simplex, hyperbolic) is a different way of trading the inherent "compatibility tax" on the new model against cross-test accuracy; the best methods recover 50–60% of the backfilled-model gain (BCT's own "update gain" metric) without backfilling, and the tax compounds across sequential updates unless dimensions are added (BT²) or the geometry is fixed a priori (d-Simplex).

### Cited Findings

**BCT (Shen et al., CVPR 2020) — formulation**
- Formal criterion (Eq. 1): φ_new is backward compatible with φ_old iff d(φ_new(x_i), φ_old(x_j)) ≥ d(φ_old(x_i), φ_old(x_j)) for all y_i≠y_j and ≤ for all y_i=y_j; because this is intractable at scale they use the *empirical* criterion M(φ_new, φ_old; Q, D) > M(φ_old, φ_old; Q, D) (Eq. 2), i.e., new-query/old-gallery accuracy must exceed old/old — [BCT arXiv:2003.11942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11942)
- Influence loss (Eq. 8): L_BCT(w_c, w_φ; T_new, T_BCT) = L(w_c, w_φ; T_new) + λ·L(w_c_old, w_φ; T_BCT), where w_c_old (the old model's classifier head) is frozen and T_BCT ∈ {T_old, T_new}; for new classes absent from the old classifier they either synthesize classifier weights as the mean old-feature per class ("β-sys") or use KL knowledge distillation on old-classifier outputs ("β-kd") — [BCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11942)
- Update-gain metric (Eq. 9): G = [M(φ_new,φ_old) − M(φ_old,φ_old)] / [M(φ*_new,φ*_new) − M(φ_old,φ_old)], i.e., fraction of the full-backfill improvement obtained backfill-free — [BCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11942)
- Setup: IMDB-Face (~1.7M images, 59K identities) for training; old model on a random 50% ID subset, new on 100%; ResNet-101 backbone, 128-d embedding, Cosine-Margin loss (m=0.4); evaluated on IJB-C (~130k images, 3,531 identities) 1:1 verification TAR@FAR=1e-4 and 1:N open-set search TPIR@FPIR=1e-2; 8×V100 — [BCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11942)
- Table 1 (IJB-C 1:1 TAR@FAR=1e-4): old/old 77.86; independently-trained new/old **0.0**; ℓ2-regression-regularized new/old 3.10; Learning-without-Forgetting adaptation 77.26 (fails criterion); BCT φ_new-β/old 80.25 (update gain 26.26%); β-kd 80.34 (27.25%); β-sys 80.59 (30.00%); paragon new/new 86.96 — [BCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11942)
- Table 1 (IJB-C 1:N TPIR@FPIR=1e-2): old/old 59.34; independent new/old 0.0; ℓ2 0.50; LwF 59.27; BCT-β 67.23 (gain 44.98%); β-kd 69.02 (55.11%); β-sys 70.70 (64.77%); paragon 76.88 — [BCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11942)
- Compatibility tax (Table 2): self-test of BCT models vs paragon — 1:1: 85.36/84.95/85.58 vs 86.96; 1:N: 73.86/73.56/74.40 vs 76.88; authors: "less than 3% drop of accuracy in both tasks" — [BCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11942)
- Robustness (Table 3): BCT works when the new model changes architecture (ResNet-152: 80.54 TAR / 68.71 TPIR cross-test), dimension (128→256, by feeding the first 128 dims to the old head: 80.92 / 69.45), and loss (old NormSoftmax → new CosineMargin: 81.81 vs old/old 80.10). **Failure cases**: old plain Softmax → new Cosine-Margin (67.11 vs old/old 73.27, not compatible) and adding a ReLU on the new embedding (34.70). A learned linear transform to match dimensions was "tried … without success" — [BCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11942)
- Sequential/transitive compatibility (Table 4, 25%→50%→100% data chain): φ3 trained only against φ2 is still compatible with φ1 (TAR 53.98 vs φ1/φ1 41.45) though lower than φ2/φ1 (56.34); φ3/φ2 = 80.40 vs φ2/φ2 75.96 — [BCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11942)
- Market-1501 person re-ID (Table 5, mAP): old/old 42.9, old-query/new-gallery independent 26.7, BCT new/old 45.0 (gain 12.0%), BCT new/new 60.1 vs paragon 60.3 — [BCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11942)
- Partial backfilling (Appendix B, Fig. 3): with BCT queries, search accuracy grows "proportionally" as the gallery is backfilled from 0% to 100% toward paragon, enabling background backfill after immediate deployment — [BCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11942)
- Stated limitation: "the accuracy gap of the new models trained with BCT relative to the new model oblivious of previous constraints" — [BCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11942)
- Citation count: OpenAlex lists 75 citations for the CVPR DOI 10.1109/cvpr42600.2020.00640 (+5 for the arXiv record) — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Towards%20Backward-Compatible%20Representation%20Learning&per-page=3&select=title,publication_year,cited_by_count,doi). **Learning Compatible Embeddings (LCE; Meng et al., ICCV 2021)**
- Aligns class centers between models (directly or via a transformation T) and adds a boundary loss L_b = Σ max(0, θ(f^(2→1)_i, w^1_{y_i}) − b^1_{y_i}) that confines mapped new features within an angular boundary around old class centers ("point-to-set" rather than instance-wise matching); supports direct, backward (T^(2→1)) and forward (T^(1→2)) manners; transformation = RBT-style residual bottleneck with K=4 blocks (K=0 for direct) — [LCE ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.01958)
- Training MS1Mv2 (5.8M images, 85k IDs), eval IJB-C TAR@FAR=1e-4; ablation: upper bound (no compatibility) 94.32 self-test; LCE 94.29 self / 91.92 cross (direct) — a 0.03-pt self-test sacrifice — [LCE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.01958)
- Architecture change (old ResNet50 → new ResNet100): BCT self 94.01 / cross 94.69; LCE self 94.64 / cross 95.07; for a *smaller* new model (ResNet18) both methods show negative "performance gain" (self-test below old) — [LCE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.01958)

**Hot-Refresh / RACT (Zhang et al., ICLR 2022)**
- Defines cold-refresh (deploy only after the whole gallery is backfilled, "weeks or even months" for massive data) vs hot-refresh (deploy immediately, backfill on-the-fly, accuracy improves gradually) — [Hot-Refresh ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.09724)
- Identifies "model regression"/negative flips during hot refresh: new-to-old positive pairs can be less similar than new-to-new negatives. NFR@k = |Q̂+_k ∩ Q−_k| / |Q̂+_k|. RACT loss (Eq. 6): −log[ exp(⟨φ_new(x),φ_old(x)⟩/τ) / (exp(⟨φ_new(x),φ_old(x)⟩/τ) + Σ_k exp(⟨φ_new(x),φ_old(k)⟩/τ) + Σ_k exp(⟨φ_new(x),φ_new(k)⟩/τ)) ] — [Hot-Refresh](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.09724)
- GLDv2-clean (1,580,470 images, 81,313 classes); splits: expansion (30%→100%), open-data, open-class; R50 old → R101 new. mAP at 0% backfill (new query, old gallery): GLDv2 old 9.91 / vanilla-BCT 11.37 / RACT 11.40; ROxford 64.28 / 65.87 / 66.54; RParis 83.27 / 81.52 / 83.75 — [Hot-Refresh](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.09724)
- Hot-refresh curve (GLDv2, R50→R101): 20% backfill vanilla ≈11.0 / RACT ≈11.9; 50%: 12.3 / 12.5; 100%: 14.5 / 15.1; an uncertainty-based (margin-of-confidence) backfill order accelerates convergence vs random — [Hot-Refresh](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.09724)
- Limitations stated: raw gallery images must be stored for hot refresh ("may not be satisfied due to privacy issues"); uncertainty-ordering fails when compatibility is insufficient; "still a long way to go for completely wiping out the problem of model regression" — [Hot-Refresh](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.09724)

**UniBCT (Zhang et al., IJCAI 2022)**
- Taxonomy of upgrade scenarios: extended-data, open-data, extended-class, open-class, identical-data (architecture change). Method: structural prototype refinement — old features are graph vertices, edges = cosine similarity in the new space, iterative propagation V^(t) = λEV^(t−1) + (1−λ)V^(0) — [UniBCT text](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/IJCAI/2022/towards_universal_backwardcompatible_representation_learning__6c17d74c.txt)
- MS1Mv3 (5,179,510 images, 93,431 IDs), IJB-C, ResNet-18 old → ResNet-50 new, 512-d. Extended-data TAR@FAR=1e-4: old 93.36; new (independent) 96.35; ℓ2-regression cross-test 0.12 (self 94.78); contrastive 92.26 (94.58); UniBCT 94.13 cross / 94.85 self. Open-class is hardest: cross-test 93.46 for 1:1 but top-1 identification 67.47% fails the empirical criterion — [UniBCT](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/IJCAI/2022/towards_universal_backwardcompatible_representation_learning__6c17d74c.txt)

**AdvBCT / boundary-aware (Pan et al., Ant Group, CVPR 2023)**
- Motivation: conventional upgrade "takes days or weeks for a large amount of data" before the old model can be replaced — [AdvBCT arXiv:2305.02610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02610)
- Adds a discriminator (adversarial loss) to align old/new embedding distributions plus an elastic angular boundary constraint; metrics P_comp = sigmoid((M(φn,φo)−M(φo,φo))/(M(φ*,φ*)−M(φo,φo))), P_up = sigmoid((M(φn,φn)−M(φ*,φ*))/M(φ*,φ*)), P_β-score = F-score combination — [AdvBCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02610)
- Benchmark (GLDv2 train, mAP; ResNet-18 old → ResNet-18 new, extended-data 30%→100%): old self RParis/ROxford/GLDv2-test = 75.45/49.15/10.03; independent new self 81.15/63.85/16.48 with cross-test 4.93/1.20/0.2; BCT self 80.58/56.34/14.61, cross 77.37/49.66/11.30; LCE self 81.57/60.85/16.48, cross 77.83/51.35/12.17; UniBCT self 80.93/57.24/16.06, cross 78.30/50.97/13.25; Hot-refresh self 79.57/58.15/13.88, cross 76.53/50.05/10.35; AdvBCT self 82.78/62.13/15.71, cross 78.55/52.31/11.49 — [AdvBCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02610)
- Enlarged backbone (R18 old → R50 new, data): independent R50 self 87.66/76.56/22.12; BCT cross 78.95/54.42/12.78 (self 85.54/68.66/19.11); LCE cross 79.36/57.18/13.30 (self 87.49/75.16/21.85); AdvBCT cross 79.08/59.03/12.75 (self 86.79/75.71/20.77) — [AdvBCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02610)
- Observation: LCE and AdvBCT self-test can *exceed* the unconstrained model φ* on RParis (82.78 vs 81.15), "compatibility has positive impacts on discrimination … if the constraints are selected properly" — [AdvBCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02610)

**Darwinian Model Upgrades (DMU; Zhang et al., 2022)**
- Quantifies BCT's "undifferentiated inheritance": on GLDv2-test 30%→100% BCT gains +11.57% new-to-old (Δ↑) but degrades new-to-new by 4.03% (Δ↓); DMU (selective backward compatibility weighted by old-feature entropy + a lightweight forward-adaptation MLP for old gallery features) gets Δ↑ 19.18% / Δ↓ 2.27% (M(φn,φo)=22.56 vs BCT 21.12; M(φn,φn)=25.00 vs 24.55) — [DMU ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.06954)
- Comparison table (GLDv2-test, 30%→100%): BCT 24.55/21.12; Focal Distillation 25.24/20.02; UniBCT 24.60/21.77; DMU 25.00/22.56 (self/cross mAP). IJB-C TAR@FAR=1e-4: BCT 93.96/92.82, DMU 94.17/92.96. Sequential 25→50→75%: gen-1 BCT cross 18.88 (Δ↓ 4.11%) vs DMU 19.84 (Δ↓ −1.21%); gen-2 BCT 19.82 (Δ↓ 2.16%) vs DMU 21.16 (0.67%) — [DMU](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.06954)

**BT² (Zhou et al., Meta AI et al., ICCV 2023)**
- Lemma 1: "There exist cases where backward compatibility will significantly limit the potential of the new model"; proposes to park incompatible information in extra dimensions via learnable orthonormal basis transformations P = e^A (A skew-symmetric), adding d=32 dims; queries are truncated when compared against old gallery vectors — [BT² arXiv:2211.03989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03989)
- ImageNet-500→1k, ResNet50-128 both (CMC top-1 / mAP): old/old 43.1/30.9; independent new/old 0.1/0.2; new/new 67.9/52.3; BCT new/old 41.3/33.3 and new/new 63.7/51.2; BCT(+32 dims, naive) 37.4 / 65.7; RACT("Contrast") 39.0 / 65.6; BT² new/old **47.8**/33.8 and new/new **66.5**/54.4 — [BT²](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03989)
- CIFAR-50→100: BCT cross-test 23.5% top-1 is *below* old/old 33.6% (compatibility unstable); BT² 38.7 / 64.4 vs independent 62.7 — [BT²](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03989)
- ResNet50 → ViT-B/16 (ImageNet-500→1k): independent new/new 78.0; BCT new/old 41.1, new/new 74.8; BT² new/old 53.6, new/new 76.9 (+8 dims 50.7/74.8; +16: 51.6/76.4; +128: 53.4/77.8) — [BT²](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03989)
- Series AlexNet→VGG13→ResNet50→ViT on ImageNet-1k: BCT ViT self 73.9 vs independent 78.0; BCT ResNet50/VGG cross 48.9 vs VGG/VGG 63.2 (compatibility lost after two hops); BT² ViT self 75.6, ViT/alex 57.9, ViT/vgg 62.5, ViT/res 72.0 — [BT²](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03989)
- Modality change (CLIP text as "old", ViT-B/16 image model as new): BCT breaks compatibility (9.1% vs CLIP txt/img 11.7%) and loses 4.4 pts self-test; BT² 11.4% / 77.6% vs 78.0% — [BT²](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03989)

**Stationary representations / d-Simplex (Biondi et al., CVPR 2024) and CoReS**
- Theorem 1: under a d-Simplex fixed classifier with pre-allocated classes and the "unconstrained feature model" assumption, stationary representations optimally approximate the two compatibility inequalities in expectation (proof via Ball-Line-Picking distances between hyperballs centered on prototypes); also shows the two constraints cannot both be satisfied, so stationarity is "the best approximation achievable" — [Biondi arXiv:2405.02581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02581)
- HOC loss: L_HOC = λ·L_SCE + (1−λ)·L_NCE(φ_t, φ_{t−1}), argued equivalent to training under constraint (1a) — [Biondi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02581)
- IAM-CL2R benchmark: ResNet18 pre-trained on ImageNet32 (100 classes), fine-tuned on CIFAR100R over 7 or 31 tasks with *asynchronous replacement* by third-party models pre-trained on 300/600 classes (also SENet18, RegNetY-400MF with 384-d vs 512-d features); eval 1:N on CIFAR-10. Average Compatibility (AC) / AA_7 / AA_31: ER baseline ×/36.22/31.30; FAN ×/36.32/30.79; BCT-ER ×/35.59/29.88; LCE-ER ×/34.89/29.30; AdvBCT-ER ×/35.73/30.10; CVS ×/36.31/31.34; d-Simplex-FD 0.05/56.58/56.27; d-Simplex-HOC **0.95/68.13/67.40** — [Biondi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02581)
- Compatibility matrices show BCT-ER and CVS "score near zero cross-test accuracy after model replacements" (e.g., 12–14% vs 69% self-test) — [Biondi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02581)
- Criticism of transformation-chain methods (FCT etc.): "As the number of chained mappings increases, the entire chain necessitates re-evaluation each time the representation model is updated … unsuitable for sequential learning and large gallery-sets" — [Biondi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02581)

**Other 2024–2025 training-time variants (abstract-level only)**
- OCA (orthogonal transformation layer, ECCV-2024 workshop): expands dimensions with an orthogonal layer "preserving the original feature space's geometry", CIFAR-100/ImageNet-1k, claims SOTA; no numbers retrieved — [arXiv:2408.08793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08793)
- Hyperbolic BCT (2025): lifts embeddings to hyperbolic space, constrains new embeddings to the entailment cone of old ones, uncertainty-weighted contrastive alignment; explicitly framed as querying "a vector database of old embeddings"; no numbers retrieved — [arXiv:2506.05826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05826)
- XBT (cross-modal BCT for CLIP-style models, 2024): a projection from new to old space "pretrained solely with text data", reducing image-text pairs needed; evaluated on Flickr30k/COCO/ImageNet; no numbers retrieved — [arXiv:2405.14715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14715)

### Inferences
- Across BCT, LCE, UniBCT, RACT, AdvBCT the *cross-test* (new query/old gallery) accuracy only modestly exceeds old/old (e.g., GLDv2-test mAP 10.0 old → 11.3–13.3 cross vs 16.5 paragon; IJB-C TPIR 59.3 → 67–71 vs 76.9). Backfill-free compatibility delivers roughly 30–65% of the gain that full backfilling would, and the new model itself is typically 1–5 points worse than an unconstrained model.
- The failure of ℓ2 regression in BCT (3.1% TAR) vs its success in Budnik & Avrithis (see KQ3) and FCT is explained by direction and target: regressing a *new, better* model onto the old space while also training on new data is unstable, whereas regressing a student onto a *fixed* teacher space, or learning a separate post-hoc map on frozen models, works.
- Methods that fix geometry a priori (d-Simplex) or add orthogonal dimensions (BT²) are the only ones reported to survive multi-generation chains; this matters for any product that must support repeated upgrades.
- All methods in this section require control of the NEW model's training and access to the OLD model's classifier or features on training data; none applies to a third party holding only two frozen black-box encoders.

### Gaps
- No paper in this group reports wall-clock or dollar cost of backfilling they avoided; the "weeks or months" statements are qualitative.
- Numbers for OCA, hyperbolic BCT and XBT could not be extracted (abstract pages only).
- Google Scholar forward-citation count of BCT not retrieved (OpenAlex: 75; Semantic Scholar rate-limited).

## KQ2. Model stitching: what linear stitchability says about representational equivalence

### Takeaway
Lenc & Vedaldi (2015) introduced stitching layers; Bansal–Nakkiran–Barak (NeurIPS 2021) and Csiszárik et al. (NeurIPS 2021) show that a single trainable *linear* (1×1 conv / affine) layer suffices to connect the bottom of one network to the top of another with ≤3% (different seeds) to ≤5% (supervised vs self-supervised) accuracy penalty on CIFAR-10/ImageNet, while CKA between the same pairs can be as low as 0.35. Caveat for translation businesses: stitching layers are trained with the downstream *task loss*, and direct least-squares matching of activations is markedly worse (≈80% relative accuracy at worst layers, Csiszárik).

### Cited Findings
- Lenc & Vedaldi (CVPR 2015) proposed "transformation and stitching layers in CNNs" to measure equivariance, invariance and equivalence of representations; Bansal et al. summarize their result as showing "certain early layers in a network trained on the Places dataset are compatible with AlexNet (see Table 4 in Lenc and Vedaldi)" — [Lenc & Vedaldi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5908); [Bansal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07682)
- Bansal et al. definition: stitched model A_{>ℓ} ∘ s ∘ r with s from a simple family (1×1 convolution with BatchNorm before/after for CNNs; token-wise linear map for transformers), trained with Adam on the task's train set while A and B are frozen; stitching penalty = loss(stitched) − loss(A) — [Bansal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07682)
- Qualitative summary (their Table 1): different initialization — CKA "varies (can be 0)" vs stitching "close (up to 3% error)"; supervised vs self-supervised (SimCLR/SwAV/DINO ResNet-50 on ImageNet, all ≈75%±1 top-1 except SimCLR 68.8%) — CKA 0.35–0.9 vs stitching "close (up to 5% error)"; more data/width/time — CKA "far (can be 0 for data, 0.7 for width)" vs stitching "better" (plugging in the stronger bottom improves the weaker top) — [Bansal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07682)
- "Stitching connectivity" conjecture: independently trained networks of the same architecture (ResNet-18, 0.5×/2× width, ResNet-164, Myrtle-CNN, ViT) can be stitched at *every* layer with test error close to the base models; a randomly initialized bottom gives a high penalty, confirming the stitcher is not itself learning the task — [Bansal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07682)
- "Stitching is not learning": they restrict the family to linear maps precisely because "if the stitching family is sufficiently powerful, it can learn to transform any representation into any other representation"; also note stitching "depends on the downstream task" unlike CKA, and is asymmetric (φ may stitch into σ but not vice versa) — [Bansal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07682)
- Csiszárik et al. ("Dr. Frankenstein"): same-architecture, different-init ResNet-20 on CIFAR-10 can be matched with "a single, affine stitching layer" to near-perfect accuracy with task-loss training, while **direct least-squares matching achieves only ≈80% relative accuracy on the worst layers**; task-loss training initialized from least squares converges most reliably; the gap widens at shallower layers — [Csiszárik et al. ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.14633)
- Csiszárik also report low stitching penalty across datasets ("ImageNet and CelebA"), that task-loss stitching tolerates 80–90% sparsity in the stitching matrix while least squares collapses, and that principal-component magnitude "is not in direct correspondence with the information content of the latent directions" — [Csiszárik et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.14633)
- FCT (Apple) cites Li et al. 2015 "Convergent learning" and runs a CKA analysis: linear CKA between an old ResNet-50 and an alternate-seed old model = 0.70 (0.66 with mixup), vs 0.27 for a SimCLR model — yet the lower-CKA SimCLR features were the *better* side-information for translation (65.0 vs 63.5 top-1) — [FCT arXiv:2112.02805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02805)

### Inferences
- Linear stitchability (with task supervision) implies that well-trained networks' *task-relevant* subspaces are related by (approximately) linear maps, which is the basic premise behind any linear or shallow embedding translator; but the Csiszárik least-squares result warns that a translator fit purely by regression on activations will underperform one fit with a retrieval/ranking objective.
- Stitching studies are at intermediate layers and measured by classification accuracy, not nearest-neighbor retrieval; retrieval is stricter because it needs *distances* preserved across the whole gallery, not just a decodable subspace (cf. Vector Linking's finding that only short-range distances are preserved, KQ6).

### Gaps
- Neither stitching paper reports retrieval metrics (recall@k/mAP) for stitched representations.
- Exact per-layer penalty numbers for ImageNet ResNet-50 stitching are only in figures in Bansal et al.; not extractable as text.
- Lenc & Vedaldi's Table 4 numbers were not retrieved directly.

## KQ3. Asymmetric retrieval / query-side-only transformation (index unchanged)

### Takeaway
When only the query encoder differs from the gallery encoder, the best training objective is plain regression (cosine) of the query model onto the fixed gallery model's embeddings; Budnik & Avrithis measure an 8–16% mAP loss versus using the gallery model symmetrically, while Duggal et al. (Amazon) show that with compatibility-aware NAS a small query model can stay within 0.3–1.6% of a ResNet-101 paragon at 23–80× lower query FLOPs. Hu et al.'s KDD'22 BC-Aligner shows a *linear* backward map trained jointly with the new model costs ~3% on the intended task and <1% on downstream consumers, whereas the same linear map fit post-hoc costs 6–10% on consumers.

### Cited Findings

**Budnik & Avrithis, "Asymmetric metric learning for knowledge transfer" (CVPR 2021; arXiv 2006.16331)**
- Motivation: "a database (gallery) of images is represented and indexed according to a large model, while queries are captured from mobile devices … rather than re-indexing the entire database, it is preferable to adapt different smaller models"; asymmetric testing = queries in student space, database in teacher space — [Budnik & Avrithis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16331)
- Losses: regression ℓ_reg(a) = −sim(f_θ(a), g(a)); contrastive/triplet/multi-similarity with asymmetric similarity s^asym(a,x) = sim(f_θ(a), g(x)) (anchor by student, positives/negatives by teacher); relational KD; DarkRank — [Budnik & Avrithis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16331)
- Setup: SfM-120k training (133k train / 30k val), ROxford5k/RParis6k (medium/hard), GeM pooling + learned whitening (whitening learned in teacher space for asymmetric testing); teachers VGG16 (512-d, 79.4 GFLOPs) and ResNet101 (2048-d, 42.9 GFLOPs); students MobileNetV2 (1.9–2.5 GFLOPs) and EfficientNet-B3 (5.6–6.3 GFLOPs) — [Budnik & Avrithis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16331)
- Table 2 (mAP medium ROxf/RPar, hard ROxf/RPar): teacher ResNet101 symmetric 65.4/76.7/40.1/55.2. MobileNetV2 student vs ResNet101 database, **asymmetric**: regression 49.2/65.0/23.3/40.7; Contr+ 47.1/61.5/21.8/37.7; contrastive 32.3/51.5/9.6/28.2; triplet 1.3/3.7/0.7/2.4; RKD 1.6/4.1/0.8/2.5; DarkRank 1.3/4.1/0.6/2.7. EfficientNet-B3 asymmetric regression 52.9/65.2/27.8/42.4 — [Budnik & Avrithis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16331)
- "When compared with symmetric testing using the corresponding teacher alone, the loss of using the student on queries is 8-16%"; "Knowledge transfer of weak information like relations or ranking fails completely"; regression works here "contrary to [BCT], where regression fails" — [Budnik & Avrithis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16331)
- Prior "feature translation" work (Hu et al., CVPR 2019, "Towards visual feature translation"), which learns a shallow translator between two large fixed networks, "performs poorly, with up to 40% mAP loss" — [Budnik & Avrithis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16331)

**Duggal et al., "Compatibility-aware heterogeneous visual search" (Amazon, CVPR 2021)**
- Compatibility rule for a small query model φ_q vs large gallery model φ_g: M(φ_q, φ_g) > M(φ_q, φ_q); trained with BCT-style objective λ1·L(φ_q, κ_q) + λ2·L(φ_q, κ_g) (κ_g = gallery model's classifier); plus compatibility-aware NAS (CMP-NAS) — [Duggal ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.06047)
- IJB-C TPIR@FPIR=1e-1: paragon ResNet-101/ResNet-101 86.7%; homogeneous MobileNetV2 77.1%; CMP-NAS heterogeneous 85.1% at 23× FLOPs reduction. DeepFashion2 top-10: paragon 65.2%, CMP-NAS 64.9% at 80× FLOPs reduction — [Duggal](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.06047); [Amazon Science](https://www.amazon.science/publications/compatibility-aware-heterogeneous-visual-search)

**Hu, Bansal, Cao, Rao, Subbian, Leskovec, "Learning Backward Compatible Embeddings" (Stanford + Amazon, KDD 2022)**
- Problem: embedding team updates versions M_0…M_k; consumer models C_j trained on ver-j embeddings break; "Feeding z_k into C_j will give random/arbitrary predictions"; keep-all (store all versions) is "too costly … web-scale recommender systems that utilize billions of embeddings" — [Hu et al. PDF](https://ai.stanford.edu/~jure/pubs/bcemb-kdd22.pdf)
- Method: learn a light-weight backward transformation B_k: R^{D_k}→R^{D_{k−1}} (linear, B_k(z)=W_k z) jointly with M_k via L_k(M_k) + λ·L_align(B_k∘M_k, M_{k−1}) (λ=16), compose B_{j+1}∘…∘B_k for any historical version; error-amplification analysis (Eq. 9) motivates a multi-step alignment loss ‖W^j_{k−1} δ_k(x)‖² for all j — [Hu et al.](https://ai.stanford.edu/~jure/pubs/bcemb-kdd22.pdf)
- Six variants: {Linear, NoTrans} × {single-step, multi-step loss} × {Joint, Post-hoc}. "Posthoc-Align: We first train M_k to minimize L_k. We then fix M_k and train B_k to minimize L_align in a post-hoc manner." Post-Lin-SLoss is "broadly adopted in cross-lingual word embedding alignment" — [Hu et al.](https://ai.stanford.edu/~jure/pubs/bcemb-kdd22.pdf)
- Benchmark: Amazon Product Reviews (Musical Instruments 27.5k users/10.6k items/231k interactions; Video Games 55k/17k/496k; Grocery 127k/41k/1.14M), GraphSAGE link prediction (Recall@50) with 5 model versions at t=0.5…0.9, growing architecture; 5 unintended binary tasks (ROC-AUC) via 1-hidden-layer MLPs — [Hu et al.](https://ai.stanford.edu/~jure/pubs/bcemb-kdd22.pdf)
- Table 3 (relative degradation from Keep-All, intended / unintended / alignment error): Musical Instruments — Non-BC 0.00/−26.45/2.61; **Post-Lin-SLoss 0.00/−10.25/1.27**; Post-Lin-MLoss 0.00/−14.43/1.34; Joint-NoTrans (≈BCT) −9.00/−0.80/0.41; Joint-Lin-SLoss −3.67/−1.07/0.48; BC-Aligner −2.96/−0.65/0.38. Video Games — Post-Lin-SLoss 0/−8.25/1.10; BC-Aligner −3.35/−0.59/0.28. Grocery — Post-Lin-SLoss 0/−6.36/1.30; BC-Aligner −3.21/−0.07/0.13 — [Hu et al.](https://ai.stanford.edu/~jure/pubs/bcemb-kdd22.pdf)
- Author summary: post-hoc linear alignment "performs poorly on unintended task, degrading the performance by 6–10% … likely due to the large embedding alignment error"; BC-Aligner suffers "around 3% (resp. 0.5%) degradation in the intended (resp. unintended) task" — [Hu et al.](https://ai.stanford.edu/~jure/pubs/bcemb-kdd22.pdf)

**Reverse-direction query mapping in retrieval**
- Online Backfilling (Meta/Google; arXiv 2301.03767): "reverse transformation module" ψ, a lightweight MLP mapping NEW embeddings back to the OLD space (Eq. 9 minimizes dist(ψ(φ_new(x)), φ_old(x))), chosen over FCT's old→new direction to avoid side information; inference overhead "only 132.8K multiply-accumulate operations … negligible (<0.01%)" vs ResNet-18's 1.8G MACs — [Online backfilling ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.03767)
- Query Drift Compensation (text retrieval, CoLLAs 2025) projects new query embeddings to the old space by subtracting a mean drift vector (details in KQ5) — [QDC arXiv:2506.00037](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00037v2)

### Inferences
- The asymmetric-retrieval literature consistently finds that *instance-level regression onto the fixed index space* is the right objective when the index cannot change, and that relational/ranking-only distillation fails outright (mAP ≈1–4). For a translator company this suggests training objective choice matters more than architecture.
- Quality loss when only the query side is adapted: ~8–16% mAP (landmark retrieval, student models), 0.3–1.6% (face/fashion with NAS and large gallery model), 6–10% downstream AUC for a *post-hoc linear* map vs <1% when the map is co-trained with the model. The post-hoc number is the relevant prior for a third party.

### Gaps
- Duggal et al. do not report what fraction of the heterogeneous gain comes from compatibility training vs NAS in the extracted summary.
- No asymmetric-retrieval paper measures the effect on ANN-index (HNSW/IVF) recall, only on exact-search mAP.

## KQ4. Backfilling cost analyses and partial/online backfilling

### Takeaway
No academic paper quantifies backfilling in dollars; they state "weeks or even months" (Hot-Refresh), "days or weeks" (AdvBCT), "billions of samples" (BT²), and FCT gives the only per-example comparison: ≈0.8–5.7M MACs for a transformation vs 4.1G MACs to re-run ResNet-50 on a 224² image (≈700–5000× cheaper), with 0.5–1 KB of embedding+side-info vs 164.5 KB per image. L²R (CIKM 2023) reports 79–81% of document-inference compute saved over three sessions of a text index. Industry sources put a from-scratch embedding of 3B images at $180k–$300k on hosted APIs and a 50M-document re-embed at "a weekend of pipeline runtime, several thousand dollars in API fees".

### Cited Findings
- FCT (Apple) Figure 3: MACs per example — backfilling 4.1 G; FCT transformations 0.8 M / 1.9 M / 5.7 M (different capacities) giving top-1 62.9 / 64.1 / 65.0 vs backfill 68.1 and BCT 46.9 (0 extra MACs); storage per example — 0.5–1.0 KB (embedding + side info) vs 164.5 KB (image). "backfilling cost (both computation and storage) scales with image resolution, but FCT transformation cost only depends on the embedding dimension" — [FCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02805)
- FCT decentralized design: on-device transformation "massively distributed on edge-devices", raw images stay encrypted server-side — [FCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02805)
- Hot-Refresh: cold-refresh "may cost weeks or even months" of backfilling for industrial galleries; limitation that gallery raw images must be retained — [Hot-Refresh](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.09724)
- AdvBCT: re-computing database embeddings "takes days or weeks for a large amount of data" — [AdvBCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02610)
- BT²: backfilling "can be especially costly in the real world where the gallery often contains billions of samples"; even FCT-style transformation "still requires a costly procedure of applying a neural network to update billions of images" — [BT²](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03989)
- Biondi et al.: reprocessing "may also be unsustainable for extensive gallery sets or unfeasible if the original images are no longer accessible due to privacy concerns"; social networks "update millions of images every month", robotics/automotive "hundreds of images every second" — [Biondi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02581)
- FastFill (Apple, ICLR 2023): MLP old→new transformation h_θ with an uncertainty head ψ_ϑ; backfill ordering by predicted uncertainty; averaged-over-backfill-curve mAP/top-1 on ImageNet: BCT 37.02/58.92, RACT 39.47/60.91, FCT 40.47/62.80, FastFill 44.84/66.49; Places-365 FCT 16.81/35.73 vs FastFill 19.48/38.32; VGGFace2 70.26/95.87 vs 71.57/96.35; "the cost of applying the transformation is negligible compared to running the embedding model on images"; on a biased VGGFace2-Gender-550 model the minority-group accuracy gap closes with ~25% backfilling (vs 100% for random order); limitation: no criterion for when to stop partial backfilling — [FastFill ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.04766)
- Online Backfilling with No Regret (ICML 2023 workshop / arXiv 2301.03767): distance-rank merge of old and new retrieval results during backfill + reverse transformation + metric-compatible contrastive learning; ImageNet-1k ResNet-50, old model on 50% of classes: AUC of mAP over the backfill curve — old 31.2, new 51.3, BCT 32.0 ("4% gain"), theirs 53.4 ("110% gain", exceeding the independent new model); argues BCT "achieves feature compatibility at the expense of feature discriminability" and that backward-compatible learning is "suboptimal in principle because gallery embeddings cannot benefit from model upgrades" — [Online backfilling](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.03767)
- L²R (Cai et al., CIKM 2023; DPR/BERT-base dual encoder, LL-LoTTE and LL-MultiCPR benchmarks with sessions D0–D3): backward-compatible variant saves "79% (2.73M vs. 13.16M) and 81% (1.47M vs. 7.85M) of computation costs for inferring document representations" across 3 sessions — [L²R ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.11512)
- Tencent BEBR (2023): web-search index "~6 billion documents", video copyright ~10 billion image embeddings; binary embeddings with compatible training give "30%–50% index costs" savings "with almost no loss of accuracy at the system level" and allow "indexing among multiple embedding versions within a unified system" — [BEBR arXiv:2302.08714](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.08714)
- GDELT (industry blog, 2024): embedding 3 billion images via hosted multimodal APIs ≈ $180,000 (AWS Titan at $0.00006/image) to $300,000 (GCP/Azure at $0.0001/image); ~972k new images/day cost $1,750–$2,912/month; "If the underlying embedding model is depreciated, the entire dataset must be re-embedded from scratch" — [GDELT blog](https://blog.gdeltproject.org/ai-in-production-a-deep-dive-into-the-costs-of-multimodal-embedding-search-over-3-billion-images/)
- Practitioner blog (tianpan.co, 2026): for 50 million documents "plan for a weekend of pipeline runtime, several thousand dollars in API fees" plus GPU costs; storage doubles/triples during the migration window; recommends shadow index → dual-read with 60–80% top-k overlap on a golden query set → staged cutover → warm rollback ≥1 week — [tianpan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-23-embedding-rotation-database-migration-not-deploy) (secondary source, personal blog)
- Hu et al. (KDD'22): keep-all versioning is rejected because "embeddings need to be produced and stored for every version" and migrating consumers "can take years" — [Hu et al.](https://ai.stanford.edu/~jure/pubs/bcemb-kdd22.pdf)

### Inferences
- Compute asymmetry is the central economic argument: re-running a vision backbone is ~10³× the cost of applying a small MLP to a stored vector; for text, the ratio is similar (transformer forward over hundreds of tokens vs a d×d matrix). This is the margin a translation business would monetize.
- The "no-regret" and FastFill results show that even when backfilling *will* eventually happen, a translator (old→new or new→old) materially raises accuracy during the transition and lets ~25% backfilling capture most of the fairness/accuracy benefit — a secondary use case (migration acceleration) independent of permanent backfill avoidance.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed source gives GPU-hours or dollar figures for backfilling a specific production gallery; all production cost numbers come from blogs.
- Zilliz "cost to re-embed" FAQ and other vendor cost pages could not be fetched (empty/blocked content).

## KQ5. Text / dense-passage retrieval and vector-database practice

### Takeaway
Vector-database vendors (Weaviate docs, Pinecone/Qdrant guidance) uniformly state that a new embedding model requires re-vectorizing the collection and provide alias/named-vector/blue-green tooling rather than any translation; academically, three 2023–2025 papers apply compatibility ideas to dense text retrieval: L²R (ranking-alignment KL loss; 79–81% index-inference compute saved), Query Drift Compensation (mean-shift projection of new queries into the old space recovers joint-training nDCG@10, 54.1 vs 50.1 for re-indexing in their continual setting), and Tencent's BEBR (compatible binary embeddings, Recall@20 0.801 vs 0.765 for standard BCT on a 10B-item system). Spotify/UiPath's Procrustes paper (2025) is the first to benchmark post-hoc query-model upgrades over frozen document indexes on MTEB tasks (see KQ6 for numbers).

### Cited Findings
- Weaviate official tutorial: two migration methods — (A) create a new collection with the new vectorizer and switch via collection alias (zero-downtime, instant rollback, preferred for production); (B) add a named vector to the existing collection ("the old vector cannot be deleted"). "Both methods require re-vectorization. Existing vectors cannot simply be reused with a new model" — [Weaviate docs](https://docs.weaviate.io/weaviate/tutorials/vectorizer-migration)
- Pinecone indexes are dimension-immutable; community/vendor guidance is to create a new index and re-upsert; search results found no official Pinecone page on cross-model translation — [search summary, markaicode/pinecone community](https://community.pinecone.io/t/errorwithoutstacktrace-pineconeclient-error-calling-upsert-errorwithoutstacktrace-vector-dimension-1536-does-not-match-the-dimension-of-the-index-2048/1308)
- Qdrant publishes a migration tool for moving collections between Qdrant instances and from Pinecone/Chroma/Weaviate (data transfer, not embedding translation); v1.18+ allows adding named vector fields to an existing collection — [Qdrant migration docs](https://qdrant.tech/documentation/database-tutorials/migration/) (from search snippet; page not fetched)
- Practitioner consensus (blog): "embeddings are not round-trippable"; Weaviate supports "collection aliases and coexisting named vectors", Pinecone and Qdrant "multiple indexes or collections you can alias", pgvector "a second embedding_v2 column built with CONCURRENTLY"; the article "explicitly rejects alternatives: no learned translation, no compatibility training is discussed" — [tianpan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-23-embedding-rotation-database-migration-not-deploy)
- **L²R** (CIKM 2023): L^com_t = L^rank_t + λ·L^align_t with L^align = KL(p(D|q) ‖ p'(D|q)) between document distributions computed with existing (old) and new embeddings; DPR with BERT-base; LL-LoTTE S@5: full re-indexing 50.0→46.5 over sessions vs backward-compatible (old index reused) 50.6→44.6; LL-MultiCPR MRR@10: re-index 17.25→20.39 vs compatible 22.61→25.84 — [L²R](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.11512)
- **Query Drift Compensation** (Goswami et al., CoLLAs 2025): nomic-embed (137M params, 768-d) fine-tuned sequentially on MS MARCO→NQ→HotpotQA→FEVER→FiQA-2018, each corpus indexed with the model current at that task; embedding distillation L_D = cosine distance between old/new query and doc embeddings; drift Δ^(t−1→t) = (1/N_t)Σ_q [f_t(q) − f_{t−1}(q)] over the new task's training queries, cumulative Δ^(t'→t) = Σ_j Δ^(j→j+1), compensated query q̂ = f_t(q) − Δ^(t'→t) ("simple vector subtraction without any re-indexing"); average nDCG@10: joint training 54.1, naive FT 49.4, FT+QDC 53.5, FT+KD+QDC 54.1, **FT with re-indexing 50.1** — [QDC](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00037v2)
- **Tencent BEBR**: compatible training of binary embeddings uses a cross-model NCE loss between the new query binary embedding and old document binary embeddings with queue-based hard negatives (~16× batch); video-copyright Recall@20: old 0.727, normal BCT 0.765, two-stage BCT 0.783, proposed 0.801; deployed on ~6B-document web search and ~10B image embeddings, QPS +90%/+72% — [BEBR](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.08714)
- Biondi et al. explicitly extend the motivation to text: "in textual domains, books can be structured into chapters … the challenge lies in the impracticality of reprocessing such extensive content with each advancement in representation models" (citing RAG) — [Biondi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02581)
- Pinterest OmniSearchSage (2024): "produces a single search query embedding in the same space as pin and product embeddings and compatible with pre-existing pin and product embeddings" (PinSage etc.); mechanism and numbers not in abstract — [OmniSearchSage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16260)
- Spotify/UiPath (Maystre et al., 2025) frame "partial upgrades: query model upgraded while document embeddings cannot be recomputed" as a core application and evaluate on MMTEB retrieval tasks (numbers in KQ6) — [Maystre et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)

### Inferences
- The vector-DB ecosystem has standardized on *operational* mitigations (aliases, dual-write, shadow indexes) and treats translation as impossible; this is both a market gap and a credibility hurdle for a translation product.
- The QDC result is notable: in a continual fine-tuning regime where the new model is *derived* from the old one, a single mean-shift vector beats re-indexing. This will not transfer to unrelated models (different architectures/training), where the drift is not a constant offset — the Procrustes/vec2vec results in KQ6 are the relevant evidence there.
- Industry compatible-training deployments (Tencent, Pinterest) exist at 10⁹–10¹⁰ scale, but all are first-party (they control training).

### Gaps
- No Microsoft/Google/Amazon paper specifically on "upgrading a semantic search index without re-indexing" for text was found beyond Hu et al. (Amazon, recommender embeddings) and Maystre et al. (Spotify).
- Elastic, Vespa, Milvus/Zilliz and Vertex AI docs on model migration were not fetched (Zilliz FAQ returned empty content); only search snippets suggest they echo the "must re-embed" position.
- OmniSearchSage compatibility mechanism/numbers not retrieved.

## KQ6. Training the new model to be compatible (a) vs post-hoc translation between two frozen models (b)

### Takeaway
Category (b) — what a third party must do — is addressed by: FCT/FastFill's transformation-only ablation (MLP old→new on paired data recovers 61.8% top-1 vs 68.1% new/new and 46.5% old/old on ImageNet, i.e., ~70% of the upgrade gain with no retraining of either model); Hu et al.'s post-hoc linear alignment (6–10% downstream degradation vs <1% joint); Maiorca et al.'s orthogonal Procrustes with 500–2000 anchors (CIFAR-100 stitching 0.71 vs 0.76 unstitched); Maystre et al.'s Procrustes with 10k paired texts on MTEB retrieval (cross-model nDCG@10 goes from ~0 to 30–75, sometimes exceeding the old same-model baseline, sometimes far below); and vec2vec's *unpaired* translation (cosine 0.87–0.92, top-1 matching 0.95–1.00 within 8192-item batches). Post-hoc maps work far better between models trained on similar objectives/data and degrade sharply across backbone families; nobody reports post-hoc translation reaching full re-index quality.

### Cited Findings

**Transformation-based methods requiring paired data but no retraining of either encoder**
- FCT "no side-information" ablation (ψ(x)=0): an MLP h trained with MSE on D_new mapping old ResNet50-128 (ImageNet-500) embeddings to new ResNet50-128 (ImageNet-1k) embeddings yields φ_new/h(φ_old) top-1 = **61.8%** vs BCT 46.9%, new/new 68.1%, old/old 46.5% ("14.9% greater than BCT"); with a 256-d old model and no side info 62.0%; with SimCLR side info 65.0% — [FCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02805)
- FCT across architectures (old ResNet18-128 → new ResNet50-128, with side info): old/old 40.1, transformed 64.2 top-1; MobileNet-128 old: 42.3 → 64.3; ResNet50-64: 44.7 → 63.6 — [FCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02805)
- FCT out-of-domain: same ImageNet models evaluated on Places-365 gallery — old 14.5, new 21.9, compatibility 20.3 top-1 ("only 1.6% below the new model") — [FCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02805)
- FCT two-hop chain v1→v2→v3 (ImageNet-250→500→1k): 57.4% top-1 vs direct v1→v3 59.9% vs v3/v3 68.1%; without side info 44.9%/53.9% — [FCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02805)
- FCT limitation: only non-parametric consumers (nearest-neighbor retrieval) are served; transformed features give "limited accuracy improvement" compared with true new features; cross-domain old/new models untested — [FCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02805)
- LCE "cross model compatibility" variant (transformations learned on pre-existing frozen face models, RBT-style residual blocks): backward 95.19–95.20 / forward 95.02–95.27 TAR@FAR=1e-4 on IJB-C for ArcFace/NormFace model pairs, vs RBT (Wang et al. 2020) 92.33–95.29 — [LCE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.01958)
- Hu et al. Post-Lin-SLoss (linear W fit post-hoc by MSE on frozen M_k, M_{k−1}): 0% intended-task loss but −10.25/−8.25/−6.36% unintended-task AUC and alignment error 1.27/1.10/1.30 vs 0.38/0.28/0.13 for jointly trained BC-Aligner; Non-BC −26.45/−30.81/−22.07% — [Hu et al.](https://ai.stanford.edu/~jure/pubs/bcemb-kdd22.pdf)
- Darwinian DMU's forward-adaptation module (MLP transforming old gallery features toward the new space) is described as "marginal" overhead — [DMU](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.06954)

**Post-hoc linear/orthogonal alignment of frozen encoders (anchors / paired samples)**
- Maystre et al. - MovieLens-25M BPR retraining (monthly versions): unaligned embeddings "fail to recover similar movies"; Procrustes "achieves the best performance among alignment methods" (compared against warm-start, regularized, BC-Aligner, linear); numbers only in figures — [Maystre et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13406)
- Maiorca et al. (NeurIPS 2023, "Latent Space Translation via Semantic Alignment"): standard-scale both spaces using anchor statistics, estimate T by orthogonal Procrustes (SVD), affine, least-squares, or lstsq+ortho using "parallel anchors … in a quantity comparable with the dimensionality" (500–2000; plateau ~500–1000; ~250 gives ~15% drop); CIFAR-100 fine cross-architecture stitching: no-stitch 0.76±0.09, ortho 0.71±0.09, affine 0.68±0.11; TREC: 0.87±0.12 / 0.79±0.11 / 0.82±0.12; cross-modal N24News text→vision classifier ≈0.40; conclusion "the transformation relating to these pre-trained encoders is indeed mostly orthogonal"; no retrieval metrics — [Maiorca et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00664)
- Relative representations (Moschella et al., ICLR 2023): represent samples by cosine similarity to a fixed anchor set, giving invariance to angle-preserving transforms and enabling zero-shot stitching across seeds/architectures/modalities; numbers not retrieved — [arXiv:2209.15430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15430)
- Inverse relative projection (2024) combines the relative space with a direct transformation estimate for zero-shot stitching across text/image encoders — [arXiv:2406.15057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15057)

**Unpaired / black-box translation**
- vec2vec (Jha, Zhang, Shmatikov, Morris, 2025): adapters A_i: R^d→R^z, shared backbone T, output adapters B_j (residual MLPs, LayerNorm, SiLU); losses = adversarial (on outputs and latents) + reconstruction + cycle-consistency + vector-space preservation; trained on 1M unpaired 64-token NQ sequences per pair; models GTE, Granite, E5, Stella, GTR, CLIP (512–2560-d; BERT/T5/RoBERTa/CLIP backbones) — [vec2vec](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- Results (8192-sample batches, in-distribution): Granite→GTE cosine 0.87, top-1 0.95, mean rank 1.18; GTE→Stella 0.92/1.00/1.00; E5→GTE 0.87/0.99/1.02; naive baseline cosine ≈0, top-1 0; oracle optimal-assignment baseline 0.70–0.85 cosine but top-1 0 on cross-backbone pairs; OOD: tweets Granite→GTE 0.85/0.95, MIMIC GTE→Stella 0.86/1.00; CLIP pairs weaker (0.62–0.78); 80% of Enron emails and 67% of tweets invertible after translation — [vec2vec](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- Limitations: GAN instability ("best of multiple initializations"; only 3/15 seeds converged for some cross-backbone pairs); no retrieval (nDCG/recall) evaluation; supervised paired baseline not reported — [vec2vec](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12540v4)
- Vector Linking (2026): recovers cross-model object correspondences from two embedding clouds "using only vectors"; empirical basis: "independently trained contrastive encoders exhibit local geometric consistency: short-range distances are approximately preserved up to a scale factor" but "long-range distances are not"; iterative anchor-based hashing with Beta-Bernoulli link confidence; applications include vector-database integration without re-embedding; numbers not in abstract — [arXiv:2605.31100](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31100)
- Multi-way alignment / Generalized Procrustes (ICLR/ICML 2026, Gladia lab): GPA builds a shared orthogonal "universe" for >2 models, GCPA adds a directional correction and "improves any-to-any retrieval" — [search result](https://gladia.di.uniroma1.it/publication/2026-achara-multiway/) (not fetched)

**Papers that explicitly classify the two regimes**
- FCT: "Unlike prior works [LCE, BCT, Wang 2020], models in FCT are trained independently, and hence their accuracies are not compromised"; but FCT still *trains the old model's side-information* at old-model time, so it is not fully post-hoc — [FCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02805)
- BT² related-work: methods "either introduce a transformation module [17, 29, 37] or enhanced regularization loss functions [24, 46, 47]"; transformation modules "still require a lightweight backfilling" — [BT²](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03989)
- Biondi: transformation approaches "do not entirely eliminate the re-processing cost" and chains "necessitate re-evaluation each time the representation model is updated" — [Biondi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02581)
- Budnik & Avrithis on learned translators between two fixed large networks (Hu et al. CVPR 2019): "performs poorly, with up to 40% mAP loss" — [Budnik & Avrithis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16331)

### Inferences
- Best-documented post-hoc results: (i) MLP old→new with paired training images recovers ~70% of the upgrade gain in image retrieval (FCT no-side-info: 46.5→61.8 vs 68.1); (ii) orthogonal Procrustes with 10k paired texts turns cross-model nDCG@10 from ~0 into something usable, and *can* exceed a weak old index's own performance when the new query model is much stronger (FEVER sentence-t5 corpus 31→41; TREC-COVID LaBSE corpus 16→54), but can also halve performance relative to the old model's own queries (HotpotQA MiniLM corpus 50→10–19). The outcome depends on the pair and dataset, so a translation business needs per-pair validation and cannot promise re-index parity.
- Orthogonal maps beat unconstrained linear maps in most (not all) pairs, which is consistent with the stitching literature's finding that task-relevant structure is near-isometric; non-linear MLPs (FCT, vec2vec, DMU) add a few points over linear in vision.
- Unpaired translation (vec2vec) proves feasibility in principle for a third party without any paired corpus, but it is unstable and unevaluated on retrieval; paired or anchor-based linear methods are the practical baseline.
- A consistent warning across Hu et al., Csiszárik et al., and Budnik & Avrithis: maps fit purely by regression on activations underperform maps trained (or co-trained) with the downstream ranking/task objective by several points.

### Gaps
- No paper evaluates post-hoc translation on ANN indexes (HNSW/IVF-PQ recall) or at >10⁷ gallery scale; all post-hoc retrieval numbers are exact search on MTEB-size corpora.
- No study compares, on the same text-retrieval benchmark, (a) compatible training of the new model vs (b) post-hoc Procrustes/MLP translation vs (c) full re-indexing.
- vec2vec and Vector Linking give matching/cosine metrics, not nDCG/recall; Maystre et al. give nDCG only as figure heatmaps (values transcribed above from the PDF text layer; exact pairing of row/column labels should be verified against the figure).
- OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar forward-citation lists of BCT were not enumerated (only counts).
